### 37. The w′ split by spherical-harmonic degree: four warps per edge, dx combined on chip

- **The change (Codex 1):** the ten paths' edge degrees are 0, 1, 1, 0, 2, 2, 1, 3, 3, 2; each of four warps owns one
  degree (2, 3, 3, 2 paths) and only its 1, 3, 5 or 7 edge_gradient components. dx partials go to shared memory,
  one barrier, then the original 512 dx atomics per edge; dsh is written per degree, dr combined on chip. Warp
  roles rotate by a hash of the edge.
- **CPU evidence (Codex):** the combined prototype builds at 64 registers, 9,232 B shared memory, no stack; the
  unchanged body capped at 64 spills (49 STL, 39 LDL).
- **Against 36's loss:** 36 put more edges in flight per SM and lost. This keeps 32 warps but only 8 edges per SM
  (20 today), so the per-SM working set of dout, x and table rows shrinks. That is the hypothesis to test.

### 32. w shared by mirror pairs that stay on-chip

### The conductor's reading

The conductor reads the forward's largest stall as "wait" on fixed-latency chains (1.44 cycles per issued
instruction), and the not-selected share (0.91) as schedulers that already have other warps to pick; so an exact win
must remove instructions (idea 24, the splits) or shorten chains (the instruction order, ILP across the k-loop's two
batches). The first half holds. The second reads the ncu differently, and the cheapest exact cut is neither:

- **"Wait" is the k-loop's chains.** Of the forward's 26.97 points of wait, 17.29 are in the k-loop: 7.45 the HMMAs'
  spacing and pass chains, ≈ 2.1 instructions waiting for an HMMA's source registers, ≈ 3.8 the W fragments' loads
  and splits, ≈ 4.0 other pairs.
- **Not-selected marks collisions, not spare work.** 72 % of it is in the k-loop, nearly half of that on HMMAs,
  beside 6.13 points of the tensor pipe's throttle: two warps of a scheduler in their k-loops at once, both wanting
  the pipe. In the TP warps are rarely passed over, and on ≈ 46 % of all cycles no warp issues at all. A shorter
  chain pays wherever no other warp was ready. The collisions call for spreading the k-loops over time (22(iii));
  they do not show that the latency is hidden already.
- **The registers bind, not the order.** The k-step's 12 accumulator tiles (part 0: 8) are independent, and with

- **Removing instructions:** idea 24 (36 per edge-slice) stands. The splits do not shrink: W stored split needs

- **The cheapest exact cut frees registers** (idea 27). x and the TP's receiver indices are live across the whole

### The conductor's questions

**(1) Where do the x loads' ≈ 150 µs come from?** Mostly not from the loads (finding 4). About 116 µs are TP
arithmetic that a constant x lets ptxas fold. The gathers cost ≤ ≈ 37 µs on the H200, part of it their address math,
and the k-loop already hides their latency. The latency that shows sits one step earlier, in the index loads and the
a tile's load (finding 5); idea 26 takes them one batch ahead, bitwise. On the exact routes for the gathers themselves:
- x rows staged once per receiver chunk: a chunk has two receivers, so a row is used at most twice within it (for a
  neighbour both receivers share), and x through shared memory lost before (15(b)).
- Senders sorted within a chunk: the receiver sums would change order, a rounding change; not worth a gate for
  ≤ 2–3 %.
- 64-bit loads: with x stored component by component, a lane would own two channels, doubling the TP's accumulators
  and x registers at the 168 cap.
- L2 prefetch hints: on the H200 x (10 MB) stays in L2 in MD and the gathers' latency is hidden. Once 26 stages the
  next batch's senders, a `prefetch.global.L2` per row is a one-line lab variant, not expected to pay here.
- The A100-SXM4 is not a case of its own (revision 11): the first job's F-x-row0 0.844 came from one odd host; on
  another it ran 0.988, and the order lab found no effect of atom order or flush on either GPU.

**(2) Can W_GROUP's vote be folded into the pair barrier or done once per chunk?** It already is the pair barrier.
The B2R.RESULT is where `bar.red.or` blocks until the partner arrives (98 % "barrier"); the reduction costs nothing
beyond the barrier that hands over each batch's a tile. Once per chunk cannot work: the partner's a tile changes
every batch, and the k-loop reads it. Moving the exit vote to the C-write barrier would move the vote, not the wait,
because the staging still has to finish before the next k-loop. The first warp waits for its partner's staging loads
(finding 5), so the stall falls with them (idea 26). F-wg-no-bar's 2.1 % bounds both barriers; the C-write barrier's
third, the pair's k-loop imbalance, stays.

**(3) Do 22(i) and (iii) still stand?** (i) does not, as its own step. It existed to fit (ii)'s registers; alone it
saves only code (no-instruction stalls ≈ 3 %, idea 18's bound), and its build added 27 address instructions per
part-1 k-loop iteration. (iii) stands, and the ncu supports it: the tensor pipe idles 62 % of the time, the k-loop's
stalls are mostly fixed-latency waits and the pipe's throttle, and within a warp the TP's FP32 instructions (32 per
edge-slice in part 0, 137 in part 1) never issue beside HMMAs. More warps add more of the same phase under a
128-register cap; (iii) puts an independent edge's TP into every k-step of the same warp. It has to be branch-free
so that ptxas can interleave the two (22).

## 13. Step-invariant kernels out of the force graph, and fills into producers

**Status.** Demoted by round 2's lesson: launch-structure savings did not reach the MD step. Revision 13 listed the

**Mechanism** (µs per force call in its `ops.csv`):
- **Species-only kernels, 27.9 µs in 7 launches.** They read only the one-hot node attributes (`[5000, 89]`) and
  the three species columns: two `triton_per_fused__to_copy_argmax_0` (14.0 µs together; 157 blocks of 64 threads,
  4 % occupancy), `triton_per_fused_argmax_index_0` (4.0), two `triton_poi_fused_argmax_index_0` (2.9 and 2.8) and
  `triton_poi_fused_index_3` and `_6` (2.2 and 2.0). Their results can be cached outside the graph. Species change
  only at swap-MC moves (`--profile swap-anneal`), which must refresh the cache too.
- **Edge-index kernels, 18.1 µs in 3 launches:** the segment starts of `fused_folded_message_blocks` (a searchsorted
  of `[373760]` into `[5001]`, 9.1 µs at 2 % occupancy) and two index kernels on the edge index and per-edge values
  (5.3 and 3.7 µs). They cannot move into the rebuild: `SkinNeighbourList.compact` (`fastmace/neighbors/skin.py`)
  moves the pairs inside the cutoff to the front of the list every step (torch's cumsum, where and index_copy_), so
  the edge list changes every step. They could fuse into the compaction's own kernels, which produce that order:
  ≈ −10 µs at best.
- **Fills, 42.0 µs in 18 launches** (the breakdown's "Copies and fills"; 41.8 µs in 18 at `27d27f21`, where earlier
  revisions counted 89 µs in 27 fill and copy launches). Two matter:
  - the 61 MB zero fill of `tabled_outer_sum_forward`'s output (`[15000, 1024]`, 14.2 µs, at HBM bandwidth), the
    target of its atomics. It goes only if that kernel writes its rows whole, which receiver-sorted edges allow: a
    restructured tabled forward;

## 20. Unbiased 3xTF32 products: NbCl5's stress headroom

**Status.** Parked after round 7. F-bias found the tensor cores' accumulation truncating; B1 removes ≈ 40 % of the
forward's slope for 1.5 % of the H200's forward (0.3 % on the A100-SXM4), and the gate passes (NbCl5 1.96 of 2), so
nothing is adopted. B1 is ready if a gate needs headroom ("Round 7: F-bias"). From revision 7: the conductor's
question how NbCl5's stress headroom could be won back if 14(A)'s order failed the gate.

**Round 12.** The H200's default failed NbCl5's stress in the CG_FOLD gate job: 2.10, at 1.370e-7 eV/Å³ against a
limit of 1.302e-7 (the float32 reference 6.51e-8), as the A100-SXM4's does (2.42, accepted by the owner). On the
model below, B1 takes ≈ 0.2 off the ratio (40 % of the forward's ≈ 45 % share of the offset), to ≈ 1.9, inside the
job-to-job spread of ±0.2. Its cost is ≈ +22 µs on the H200 (1.5 % of a 1,493 µs forward). Whether to buy that back
is the owner's call; until then 20 stays parked.

**The answer: no sum dominates; a bias does.**

**The offset.** In the gate's value files at `7f7ef7db`, each crystal's production stress error is the float32
calculator's error pattern plus an isotropic offset:

| Crystal | Production − float32 calculator, diagonal (1e-8 eV/Å³) | Off-diagonal | Ratio | Ratio without the offset |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NbCl5 | +7.23 (sd 0.70 over six configurations) | ≤ 1.33 | 1.958 | 0.954 |
| SrTiO3 3×3×3 | +24.5 (sd 3.0) | ≤ 6.3 | 0.925 | 0.938 |
| NaCl | +0.87 (sd 0.43) | ≤ 0.44 | 0.920 | 0.902 |

- The total energies carry an offset too, with one sign in every configuration: +2.0 meV (NbCl5), +0.39 (SrTiO3),
  +0.10 (NaCl), against the float32 calculator.
- The common pattern is most likely the float32 input (positions and cell), which both calculators share.
- SrTiO3 passes with the largest offset because its float32 calculator's own isotropic error is −4 to −22e-8.

**Where it comes from.** The A100 bisection at `3812b56e` (`gate-table.txt` in
`results/omat0-tp-backward-20261104/a100-sxm4-stress-bisect-3812b56e/`; NbCl5 limits 1.23–1.49e-7):

| Switched off | NbCl5 stress ratio | Energy ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| nothing (three runs) | 2.53–2.97 | 14.5–15.0 |
| one other default each (`no-tables`, `no-tablew`, `no-block`, `degree10`) | 2.40–3.10 | 10.9–15.2 |
| `tf32x3_gemms` | 1.30 | 2.07 |
| the tensor-core forward | 1.63 | 11.15 |
| both | 0.42 | 0.92 |

Both 3xTF32 paths carry the offset, the GEMMs the larger share (≈ 55 % of the stress excess, most of the energy's).

**Why 3xTF32 is biased.** A float64 model of the splits (NumPy, 2·10⁵ random dot products; products exact; the
scratch scripts `split_bias*.py`):
- **Our mask split** (`TC_SPLIT = "mask"`): the high part is truncated, so the low part has the value's sign. The
  tensor core truncates the low part once more, and the dropped low·low term has the product's sign. Each product
  shrinks by 2.74e-7 on average (relative, toward zero).
- **CUTLASS's 3xTF32** (v4.8.1, `MmaTensorOpFastF32::MmaFastF32` in `mma_tensor_op_fast_f32.h`: big part
  `round_toward_zero`, small part `round_half_ulp_truncate`): only the dropped small·small term is biased, −0.59e-7.
- **Rounded high parts** ("round", or B1 below): 0.00.
- **If the tensor cores truncate their FP32 accumulation**, as Fasi et al. found on the A100 (round-toward-zero
  normalisation), each mma loses another half ulp of its accumulator on average. Whatever the split, the model
  then gives −4.8e-7 at K = 64 (the forward: 8 k-steps of 3 mmas), −10e-7 at K = 128 and −20e-7 at K = 256. K = 128
  holds 71 % of the H200's GEMM outputs (`shapes.json` of `runs/mirror-h200`).

Two observations favour the truncating accumulation:
- With the splits alone, the forward (−2.74e-7) would outweigh the GEMMs (−0.59e-7), yet the bisection gives the
  GEMMs the larger share. A truncating accumulation weighs the GEMMs' longer K more.
- The RTX 5060 Ti's gates (`results/omat0-kernel-lab-20261103`, 3 November): SrTiO3's energy error was 0.73 meV
  with SIMT, 1.15 with "round" and 1.39 with "mask". (1.15 − 0.73) / (1.39 − 1.15) = 1.75; the model's ratio of the
  accumulation's share to the mask split's is 4.83 / 2.75 = 1.76. One sample each, on sm_120.

**14(A)'s order.**
- A summation order cannot move a bias. It adds zero-mean noise of about the size by which a float32 calculation's
  maximum error moves between runs.
- The A100 bisection's ten NbCl5 limits ranged 1.23–1.49e-7: the float32 calculator's own maximum error moves by
  ±0.7e-8 (±10 %) between runs. That alone moves NbCl5's ratio by ±0.2, and 14(A)'s order would move it by about
  ±0.1. Today's margin is 0.04 (0.29e-8).
- So 14(A) may fail, and so may a rerun of today's code. NbCl5's ratio was 2.05 under the A100 plan on the H200
  (hopper sweep, 5 November), 1.93 at `62556704` and 1.96 at `7f7ef7db`.
- Round 7: 14(A)'s gate gave 2.076, +0.12 on today's 1.96.

**Winning the headroom back**, cheapest first. None of these changes a summation order.
1. **B1, the forward.**
   - Round the W values to 22 significant bits (round to nearest even) as `tc_wfill` fills the slice. Under the mask
     split they are then exact: 11 bits in the high part, at most 11 in the low part.
   - Split a with a rounded high part (`hi = (x + 0x1000) & 0xffffe000`, the low part raw). Its low part then has a
     random sign, so the tensor core's truncation of it and the dropped low·low term average out.
   - Model: bias −2.74e-7 → 0.00; error rms 1.18 → 0.59 units of 2^-24 of the row scale.
   - Cost: one IADD per a value, 4 per k-step and warp at G = 2, ≈ +10 µs: F-wfrag-no-wsplit saved 248 µs for 96
     split instructions per k-step, ≈ 2.6 µs each. Rounding both operands' high parts instead costs ≈ +70 µs
     (28 values per k-step) for the same bias.
2. **B2, the GEMMs.** A copy of `MmaTensorOpFastF32` with the big part rounded (`round_half_ulp_truncate`) for one
   operand; rebuild `tf32x3_sm80.cubin` and `tf32x3_sm90.cubin`. Model: −0.59e-7 → +0.3e-7 (the small part's
   half-up ties). Cost: about none, one integer add per element as the fragments are converted.
3. **B3, the accumulation, only if F-bias finds it truncating.** Partial sums added by FADD, which rounds to
   nearest. In the model, a fresh accumulator per k-step takes K = 64's −4.8e-7 to −0.4e-7, and four k-slices take
   K = 128's −10e-7 to −2.3e-7.
   - The forward: 4 FADD per tile and k-step (48 at G = 2) and 4 registers per tile in flight. Too dear at 168
     registers; per two k-steps at half the cost, or with the registers of idea 22's step (i).
   - The GEMMs: CUTLASS's serial split-K (deterministic), or a warp-level operator that adds each k-iteration's
     fresh fragment.

**Expected.**
- If the splits carry the offset (exact accumulation): B1 and B2 remove it, ratio ≈ 0.95.
- If the accumulation truncates: B1 and B2 remove only the splits' part, 36 % of the forward's bias at K = 64 and
  6 % of the GEMMs' at K = 128. With the bisection's shares the offset falls by ≈ 20 % (ratio ≈ 1.75), and B3 is

Other ways to cut the forward's shared loads (fourth round):

- **16 edges per warp in two path halves** (48 accumulators; each W fragment serves two n-tiles, as in idea 17 at
  G = 2). The first half's w waits in r_s while the second half runs, and a and sh cover 16 edges. So each warp's
  slices double (9,472 → 18,944 B), and 12 warps need 288,768 B of the 232,448 B. Idea 17 makes the same split
  across two warps, whose slices already exist.
- **The TP on the MMA's accumulator layout, without the r_s round trip** (96 of ≈ 550 wavefronts per batch). A
  lane holds w for four channels × two edges there, so the TP's message accumulators would grow fourfold (16–24 →
  64–96 per lane), and the receiver sum would need shuffles across the four lanes of a channel. The round trip
  itself costs 0.2 % (F-tc-no-redistribute 0.998, round 4).
- **The edges as the MMA's M dimension** (a as operand A, W as operand B). The shared bytes per MMA depend on the
  warp tile's shape, not on which operand holds W. M = 16 also wants 16 edges per warp, the accumulator problem of
  the 16-edge batches.
- **64-channel blocks.** A block would stage each edge's a once for two warps of 32 channels. Each warp's tile stays
  192 × 8, so its W loads per MMA stay too; the staging is ≈ 20 of ≈ 550 wavefronts.
- **W in shared memory below 32 bits per element.** The 3xTF32 product uses about 22 of W's 24 significant bits
  (hi and lo). Fewer stored bits is a lower-precision product, i.e. a 16-bit path for the gate to judge, and a
  packed hi/lo pair still needs 32 bits plus unpacking in place of the split.

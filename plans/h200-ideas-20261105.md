### What round 2 says

1. **Launch structure does not reach the step.**
   - SEGMENT_GROUPED's bound saved 15–52 µs per group of four GEMMs, but the A100 MD step stayed at 0.9987 /
     1.0004. PDL saves less than 5 µs per group.
   - The two convolution kernels are 60 % of the step: the forward ≈ 1.69 ms and the w′ ≈ 1.6 ms. Round 3
     therefore goes to their algorithm and data layout, and idea 13 moves to the end of the ranking.
2. **The x cost is not its bytes.**
   - DEGREE_PARTS halved the x rows each edge gathers (eight row gathers per edge and channel block, now four).
     The kernel gained 87 µs (1,777.9 → 1,691.3), 29 % of F-no-xload's 295 µs.
   - **The SASS at `891bf370` shows why.**
     - Both parts load a batch's eight senders into one register, one after the other.
     - Each load issues only after the previous value has passed its range check, and each edge's x loads
       follow its sender.
     - The a and sh `float4` loads issue after this chain, and the receivers last.
     - The chain is as long with half the rows.
   - **F-no-xload overstates the x cost:** with x a constant, ptxas folds the x·e·coef products and frees the
     24–32 x registers. Idea 15 adds a cleaner bound (F-x-local).
3. **The TP's arithmetic is not where its time goes on the H200.**
   - The coefficient multiplies cost 2.9 %, the S tile's per-lane product bounds at 3.4 %, and the staging at
     3–4.5 %.
   - What is left: the product (about half of the forward), the TP's latency and the x gathers.

### Corrections to revision 2

- **TC_PRESPLIT is not a mere follow-up.**
  - Revision 2 argued that the k-loop is tensor-bound, so fewer W splits would only free idle issue slots.
  - But the H200's pass bounds leave ≈ 110 µs of the k-loop's other work unhidden by the MMAs, and on the H200 the
    presplit planes fit beside 12 warps. It is now idea 16.
- **Mirror-edge sharing of the product is not dropped.**
  - Revision 2 dropped it for its 0.96 GB round trip.
  - But the H200's forward uses ≈ 5 % of the HBM bandwidth, while its tensor pipe and its TP latency bind. So
    the round trip can pay. It is now idea 14.
- **Idea 11's padding is gone:** with DEGREE_PARTS the parts are 4 and 6 paths, i.e. 128 and 192 W rows, exact
  multiples of 64.
- **F-no-tp (0.619)** also drops the x gathers and frees their registers, so it bounds the TP and x together, not
  the TP alone.

### What the failures say

1. **The w′ is latency-bound.**
   - **What did not help:**
     - fewer L1 wavefronts on its table reads (TABLE_PAIRS);
     - 40 fewer FMULs per edge (HOIST_SCALE), which is 1.6 % of its instructions;
     - table locality (one slab 0.925, so L2 and HBM misses cost at most 7.5 %);
     - no atomics (1.089).
   - **The SASS census:**
     - A channel pass is 574 instructions: FFMA 330, LDG 90, FMUL 71, CS2R 30, FADD 20, IADD3 17, MOV 10, REDG 4.
     - The 90 loads match 30 for the table (three `float2` per path), 40 for dout, 16 for sh and 4 for x.
     - With four passes and the 202-instruction end reduction, an edge costs ≈ 2,500 warp instructions. Over
       373,760 edges that is ≈ 0.9 ms of issue at one instruction per cycle per scheduler, against 1.61 ms
       measured.
     - At most 89 registers are live (96 allocated, 20 warps per SM). The register caps of 72 and 80 spill.
     - The kernel is 984 static instructions (16 KB): its channel passes are a loop, so its code is small.
   - **So:** what can still pay is the latency of its loads. The 40 dout loads per pass are L2 round trips, because
     the ≈ 19 four-edge blocks of a receiver land on about as many SMs (idea 12).
2. **The H200 forward runs its two halves one after the other.**
   - **The bounds at `27d27f21` (base 2,323 µs) add up:**
     - the product: no-product 0.527, i.e. 1,099 µs;
     - the TP with its x gathers: no-TP 0.669, i.e. 769 µs;
     - together 1,868 µs, which leaves 455 µs for staging and the rest.
     - The A100's bounds add up the same way: 3.25 ms = 1.71 ms product + 1.26 ms TP + rest.
   - **The product half is bound by the tensor pipe at mma.sync's rate:**
     - one MMA pass instead of three gives 0.717, about as much as no MMA at all (0.705);
     - the product alone took 1.082 ms with mma.sync and 0.554 ms with wgmma (the microbenchmark).
   - **The SASS census of `02a81e13` (KP = 64):**
     - **k-loop:** per k-step 30 `HMMA.1688.F32.TF32`, 86 TF32-split instructions (LOP3 + FADD) and 12 LDS. That
       is 240 HMMA, ≈ 690 split instructions and 96 LDS per 8-edge batch and warp.
     - **TP:** ≈ 95 instructions per edge and lane, 88 of them FMUL or FFMA (part 0: FMUL 45, FFMA 43). The
       receiver-change stores sit behind a branch.
     - **Whole batch:** ≈ 2,900 instructions per warp.
     - **HMMA rate:** at the product bound the tensor pipe takes ≈ 11–12 cycles per HMMA per scheduler.
       Three warps' k-loops then issue on only ≈ 40 % of the cycles, and during the TP the tensor pipe is idle.
   - **Registers:** the peak (163 of 168 live) is in the k-loop, where the x values for the TP (32 registers,
     gathered before the k-loop) and the 40 accumulators are live. The TP loop peaks at 149.
   - **Why the halves do not overlap is open.** After the W fill the warps take their own chunks, with no block
     barrier. So either they stay in lockstep by themselves, or the HMMAs starve the other warps' FP32
     instructions. Idea 10's census tells which.
3. **A faster product alone did not help.**
   - **WGMMA** halved the product, but its two W planes in shared memory left room for only 8 warps instead of 12.
     The TP, which needs warps to hide its latency, lost more than the product gained.
   - **The pipelined version** kept the A fragments and accumulators live across the TP (244 registers) and lost
     more.
   - **The lever is overlap,** or a faster product that keeps 12 warps (ideas 10, 11 and 16), or less product
     (idea 14).
4. **On the RTX 5060 Ti the TP is its per-lane arithmetic.**
   - It is half the forward (F-no-tp 0.517).
   - Its coefficient multiplies (0.977), x loads (0.950) and sh loads (1.017) are small.

## 14. Pair-shared product: w once per mirror pair

**Mechanism.**
- **The symmetry.**
  - Every edge i ← j with shift s has a mirror j ← i with shift −s in the full neighbour list.
  - The mirror's vector is the exact negation: (p_i − p_j) − s = −((p_j − p_i) + s) holds in IEEE arithmetic. So
    both edges have the same length bit for bit, if the edge vector is formed that way (the CPU check below).
  - OMAT-0's radial input is symmetric in the species pair: its fitted table rows for (s, t) and (t, s) are bitwise
    equal, which SYMMETRIC_PAIRS relies on.
  - So the radial features a, and every w the k-loop forms from them, are the same bits for both edges. Today
    both edges compute them. The product is about half of the forward (F-tc-no-product 0.527 at `27d27f21`), and
    with its staging about two thirds (F-no-tp 0.619 at `6ebc795d`).
- **Why it can pay on the H200.**
  - The edge and its mirror sit in different receivers' warps, so sharing needs w in memory: `[E/2, 1280]` floats,
    0.96 GB per call. Revision 2 dropped the idea for that.
  - But today's forward moves only ≈ 0.37 GB through HBM in 1.69 ms (a and sh once per part, and the messages;
    the H200 renders it with `STORE_W = false`, so no w is stored): ≈ 5 % of 4.8 TB/s. Its time goes to the tensor
    pipe and the TP's latency, so the HBM has room.
- **At each neighbour-list rebuild** (beside the force call, as today):
  - one radix sort by (smaller atom, larger atom, shift) puts every edge next to its mirror;
  - each pair gets a slot p and an owner edge, and `pair_of[e]` maps both edges to p;
  - ownership goes by the parity of i + j, so that each receiver owns about half of its edges; self-images
    (i = j) go by the sign of the shift.
- **Variant (B), bitwise:**
  1. **Product pass:** today's k-loop over the owners in pair order, with STORE_W on and no TP, writing
     `w_pair[p]`. The radial table evaluates a for the owners only.
  2. **TP pass:** today's fused forward over all edges in receiver order, with the k-loop replaced by 4–6
     coalesced loads per edge and lane from `w_pair[pair_of[e]]`. It needs no W slice in shared memory and no
     k-loop registers, so it can run more warps per SM for the TP's latency.

  Every message is the same sum in the same order. Traffic: 0.96 GB written, 1.9 GB read.
- **Variant (A), rounding:** each receiver's edges are partitioned at rebuild time into owned and partner edges.
  - Pass 1 is today's fused forward over the owned edges, also writing w to the partner's slot.
  - Pass 2 is the product-free forward over the partner edges, starting each receiver from pass 1's message.
  - Traffic: 0.96 GB each way, plus 0.2 GB of messages. The sum order changes (owned edges first).

**Attacks.** The product with its a staging (≈ 1,100 µs), and half of interaction 2's `radial_table_forward`
(114.7 µs), if nothing else needs a for every edge.

**Ceiling.** Split today's forward into P, the product with its a staging (F-no-tp 0.619 at `6ebc795d`,
≈ 1,100 µs), and T, the rest (the TP, the x gathers and the flushes, ≈ 590 µs at `891bf370`).
- Without the round trip, (B) and (A) both take ≈ ½ P + T ≈ 1,140 µs (0.67), i.e. −550 µs.
- The round trip: (B) writes 0.96 GB and reads 1.9 GB, (A) writes and reads 0.96 GB. Unhidden, at ≈ 4 TB/s, that
  adds ≈ 720 µs to (B) and ≈ 480 µs to (A).
- How much of it hides decides the idea. The product passes are tensor-bound, so their stores should hide. (B)'s
  TP pass and (A)'s partner pass would both have to read at ≈ 3 TB/s, with the loads issued a batch ahead.
- Both: ≈ −57 µs more from the radial table.

**Expected.** −150 to −350 µs (3–6 % of the step).

**Exactness.**
- (B) is bitwise if w is bitwise equal on each pair. A GPU test on the gate's three structures compares every
  pair.
- (A) changes the sum order: it needs the force/stress gate.

**Falsifier.**
1. **CPU:** read how the neighbour list forms edge vectors and shifts, and check the pair symmetry of the radial
   input for OMAT-0 (the SYMMETRIC_PAIRS check). If the mirror's vector is not the exact negation, only (A) is
   left.

## 15. The x gathers: indices a batch ahead, then x through shared memory

**What the SASS of the `891bf370` forward shows** (168 registers, no spills):
- **The sender chain:**
  - Both parts load a batch's eight senders into one register (R25 in part 1, R17 in part 0), one after the
    other.
  - Each load issues only after the previous value has passed `snd < 0 || snd >= N`.
  - Each edge's x loads (three in part 1, one in part 0) follow its sender.
- **The order:** the a and sh `float4` loads issue after the chain, the eight receiver loads last.
- **So a batch's staging is serial:**
  - one L2 round trip for the first sender;
  - about six dependent L1 hits for the rest, which share its 32-byte sector;
  - then the x round trip;
  - the a loads, which come from HBM, queue behind all of it.
- **The TP's code:** unrolled over the 8 edges, with the receiver flush inlined between them (finding "Code size"
  above).

**(a) Indices a batch ahead (bitwise, ≈ 30 lines).**
- One warp-wide load per batch: lanes 0–7 read `senders[e0 + b]`, lanes 8–15 `receivers[e0 + b]`. It goes out
  during the previous batch's TP, into one register per lane.
- Each lane gets its x addresses with `__shfl_sync`, so all of a batch's x loads (24 in part 1, 8 in part 0) issue
  back to back.
- The a and sh loads go first, since they depend on nothing.
- The eight rv registers go too: the TP shuffles rv[b] when it needs it.

**(b) x through shared memory, and a rolled TP (bitwise).**
- **The copy:** each lane copies its x values into the warp's slice with `cp.async.ca` right after (a)'s index
  load, and waits (`cp.async.wait_group`) before the TP. No registers are held across the k-loop, whose length
  hides the latency.
- **The slice:** 8 edges × 3 rows × 32 floats = 3 KB per warp in part 1, 1 KB in part 0. The block grows from
  175,104 to 211,968 B of the 232,448 B allowed, so 12 warps stay.
- **The TP:** it reads x and w (from `r_s`) per edge, so its edge loop can stay rolled, with one flush out of
  line.
  - That frees ≈ 70 registers in the TP (w 48, x 24, rv 8, less what the rolled loop holds).
  - It shrinks the TP's hot code about eightfold.

**Attacks.** The x gathers and the chain in front of them; the TP's registers and code size.

**Ceiling.** The x cost at `891bf370`, not yet measured: at most ≈ 210 µs (F-no-xload's 295 µs at `6ebc795d`,
less DEGREE_PARTS' 87).

**Expected.** (a) −20 to −80 µs; (a) and (b) −40 to −150 µs.

**Exactness.** Bitwise: the same values reach the same instructions in the same order.

**Falsifier.**
1. **Lab:**
   - `F-no-xload` again at `891bf370`;
   - a new `F-x-local`: every edge gathers x from atoms 0–7 (`x[b * IN_DIM + …]`). It keeps the instructions and the
     registers, its loads hit L1, and nothing waits on the senders.

   F-x-local bounds what (a) and (b) can get. F-no-xload − F-x-local is the confound of the constant.
2. **CPU:** NVRTC sm_90 builds of (a) and (b), at ≤ 168 registers and no spills. In (a)'s SASS the indices must
   arrive in one load and the x loads issue together.
3. **Rule:** build (a) if F-x-local ≤ 0.97; build (b) if (a) gets less than half of F-x-local's saving.

**Effort.** (a) half a day; (b) two days (the slice, `cp.async` groups, the rolled TP with its out-of-line flush).

## 16. TC_PRESPLIT on the H200, then a pass-major MMA order

**Why it is back.**
- Revision 2 kept TC_PRESPLIT only as a follow-up of idea 10: the k-loop is tensor-bound, so fewer W splits would
  only free idle issue slots.
- The H200's own bounds at `27d27f21` say part of the k-loop's other work is not hidden:
  - one MMA pass costs as much as none (F-tc-1pass 0.717, F-tc-no-mma 0.705): ≈ 440 µs of splits, LDS and loop
    work, which bind when the MMAs are few;
  - passes 2 and 3 add ≈ 329 µs each, so three passes of MMAs alone would take ≈ 990 µs of the product's 1,099;
  - the other ≈ 110 µs is k-loop work that the MMAs do not hide. The W splits are most of it: 96 of the ≈ 150
    instructions per k-step in part 1 at `891bf370`.
- The A100's numbers do not carry over:
  - F-tc-no-wsplit 0.862 and TC_PRESPLIT's 1.362 (12 → 8 warps at 164 KB) were measured before TC_WFRAG, and the
    variant spilled 64 B;
  - the round-5 re-run on the TC_WFRAG default failed: the variant's pattern (`split_tf32(wp[0], …)`) matches only
    the old fragment loads.
- The H200's one TC_PRESPLIT number (1.297) is void: the re-render lacked VEC_A and spilled 32 B.

**Mechanism.**
- **(a) TC_PRESPLIT (the switch exists):** the block stores its W slice once as TF32 hi and lo planes. The k-loop
  loads both with LDS.128 and splits nothing on the W side.
  - **Per k-step in part 1:** the 96 W-split instructions go, and 12 LDS.128 come.
  - **Shared memory:** the two planes in fragment order take 2 × 49,152 B in part 1; with the staging (113,664 B)
    that is 211,968 B of 232,448 B, so 12 warps stay. The render's W region must then be sized by the fragment
    layout, not by the SIMT layout's 40-float rows (61,440 B per plane would not fit twice).
  - **Registers:** the void run spilled 32 B at 168. Idea 15(a) frees the eight rv registers in the k-loop.
- **(b) Pass-major order (bitwise):** with both planes in shared memory, each k-step can issue the first product
  of all 12 tiles, then the second, then the third, re-reading fragments instead of holding them.
  - Every accumulator still gets al·bh, ah·bl, ah·bh in that order.
  - Dependent MMAs then come 12 apart. Today they are a median of 13 instructions apart (minimum 3): ptxas keeps
    4–5 tiles in flight, as many as the W fragments' 8 registers per tile allow.
  - It pays only if Hopper's mma.sync TF32 rate is well above what the k-loop gets: ≈ 10.2 cycles per HMMA per
    scheduler at the margin (passes 2 and 3 at `27d27f21`). The prior is low: three warps with 4–5 tiles in flight
    each already give a scheduler 12–15 independent HMMAs.

**Attacks.** (a) the k-loop work the MMAs do not hide (≈ 110 µs at `27d27f21`, ≈ 100 µs at KP = 64); (b) the MMA
rate.

**Ceiling.**
- (a): ≈ −100 µs.
- (b): the MMAs take ≈ 875 µs at `891bf370` (89.7 M HMMA at 10.2 cycles). At 8 or 7 cycles per HMMA that falls by
  ≈ 190 or 275 µs.

**Expected.** (a) −30 to −100 µs; (b) 0 to −150 µs.

**Exactness.** Bitwise: the same products into the same accumulators, in the same order.

**Falsifier.**
1. **Lab:** `F-tc-no-wsplit` in its TC_WFRAG form against the `891bf370` base. The lab's variant matches only the
   old fragment loads (`split_tf32(wp[0], …)`); the new form replaces `split_tf32(wv.x, ah[0], al[0]);` by
   `ah[0] = __float_as_uint(wv.x); al[0] = ah[0];`, and so on for y, z and w. Build (a) if it is ≤ 0.96: the
   planes' extra LDS take part of the bound back.
2. **The re-render:** `tc-presplit` with VEC_A and DEGREE_PARTS, compared with the switch's own render, bitwise
   against the base, at ≤ 168 registers.
3. **For (b):** an HMMA.1688.F32.TF32 throughput microbenchmark on the H200 (independent accumulators, 1–4 warps
   per scheduler; a minute). Build (b) only if it sustains ≤ 7 cycles per HMMA with the k-loop's operand loads.

**Risks.** Shared-memory bandwidth.
- (a) doubles the W fragment reads (24 LDS.128 per k-step), (b) triples them (36). At today's round length that is
  ≈ 77 and ≈ 115 B per cycle of an SM's 128.
- (b) may therefore have to keep the hi fragments in registers for passes 2 and 3 (48 registers, once 15(b) has
  freed them).

**Effort.** (a) one day: the switch and its tests exist; the lab rule, the H200 constants and the spills remain.
(b) two days.

## 10. De-phase the fused forward's warps: skew, then rotation

**Status.** The census and F-skew did not run in round 2; they are in the next lab job. The numbers below are for
`02a81e13`. At `891bf370` the parts have 4 and 6 paths (24 and 36 HMMA per k-step).

**Mechanism.**
- **Today:** each of the 12 warps (3 per scheduler) loops over 8-edge batches:
  1. staging, and the x gathers for the TP;
  2. the k-loop: the 3xTF32 product, bound by the tensor pipe;
  3. the redistribution through `r_s`;
  4. the TP, FP32 work that waits on latency.
- **Likely lockstep:**
  - After the W fill and its one `__syncthreads`, the chunk loop has no block barrier: each warp takes its own
    chunks from the counter (`G/` source).
  - But the warps start together, and every batch runs the same instruction stream.
  - Two warps that share a throughput-bound pipe keep their phase offset: the leader runs alone at the start of the
    phase, the follower alone at its end. Nothing pulls warps that start together apart.
  - The three warps of a scheduler would then be in their k-loops at the same time, with most issue slots idle,
    and then in their TPs at the same time, with the tensor pipe idle. The additive bounds are what that would look
    like.
  - Lockstep would also put the staging loads of all warps into one burst, with nothing to overlap them. That fits
    how much the a staging (F-no-aload 0.864) and the x gathers (F-no-xload 0.830) cost at `27d27f21`, although the
    forward moves only ≈ 2.6 GB (a, sh and x) at ≈ 1.1–1.4 TB/s on average.
- **The other explanation:** HMMAs at full rate may take the register-file bandwidth that the other warps' FP32
  instructions need. Then the halves add up even with mixed phases, and this idea is dead. The census below tells
  the two apart.
- **The change:** keep the k-loops of the warps that share a scheduler apart, so that the other warps run their
  TPs and staging meanwhile. Two variants, cheapest first:
  - **(a) Skew (`F-skew`).**
    - Group g = warp ≫ 2 holds one warp per scheduler (warp & 3).
    - Before its first chunk, group g waits g offsets on `clock64()`; `__nanosleep` only guarantees [0, 2t].
    - Two settings: one k-loop at the full tensor rate (≈ 1.5 µs), and a third of today's batch period (≈ 2.5 µs; a
      batch takes ≈ 7.6 µs per warp at `02a81e13`).
    - If phase offsets are neutral, as argued above, they persist. Nothing else changes: about ten lines.
  - **(b) Rotation (`F-rotate`).** Named barriers pass a token between the three groups:
    - group g waits at its own barrier before its k-loop and arrives at the next group's barrier after it
      (`bar.sync` / `bar.arrive`, 256 threads each; barrier 0 stays with `__syncthreads`);
    - the tensor pipe then serves one warp per scheduler at a time, while the other two run their TPs.

    This resembles CUTLASS's Hopper ping-pong schedule, with three groups and no producer.
- **Not two blocks per SM.** Two co-resident 6-warp blocks would de-phase nothing, because the warps of today's
  block are already free. They would only cost a second W slice.
- **Not the rejected producer/consumer split.** Every warp still stages, multiplies and contracts its own edges.
  Nothing passes between warps, and no registers are reallocated. The A100 producer/consumer microbenchmark lost
  because each role kept only part of the warps for its own latency; here every warp keeps its whole loop.

**Attacks.** The TP (≈ 0.77 ms at the `27d27f21` split) and the exposed staging latency, by running them under
other warps' k-loops.

**Why it can work on the H200.** Cycles are per scheduler and per round, i.e. one batch for each of its three warps.
The instruction counts are from the census of `02a81e13`, the phase times from the `27d27f21` bounds.
- **During the k-loops, issue is mostly free:** the three warps issue ≈ 3 × 1,100 instructions in the ≈ 8,200
  cycles the tensor pipe needs for their 720 HMMAs, ≈ 40 %.
- **During the TPs, the tensor pipe idles:** the TPs and their loads are ≈ 3 × 940 instructions in ≈ 6,450 cycles,
  ≈ 44 % issue. So the TP too waits on latency, not on issue.
- **Today a round takes ≈ 15,000 cycles** (1,786 µs at 1.98 GHz over 236 rounds) and issues ≈ 3 × 2,900 = 8,700
  instructions, 58 %.
- **Overlapped,** a round costs at least the larger of the tensor time (≈ 8,200) and the issue (≈ 8,700), and at
  least one warp's own chain of k-loop, TP and staging.

**Ceiling.** ≈ −0.75 ms (13 % of the step): at the issue limit a round takes ≈ 8,700 of today's ≈ 15,000 cycles.
Hiding the TP alone (≈ 0.77 ms at the `27d27f21` split) gives about the same. Ideas 14 and 16 shrink the k-loops
this idea hides the TP under, so its ceiling falls if they land first.

**Expected.** −0.15 to −0.45 ms if the census shows lockstep; nothing if it does not.

**Exactness.** Bitwise: every receiver is still summed by one warp in edge order. Only which warp takes which chunk
changes.

**Falsifier.**
1. **Phase census** (lab, H200, a minute of GPU time):
   - **Stamps:** `clock64()` per warp at the k-loop's start and end and at the TP's end, for every fourth batch,
     with `%smid` and the warp index (a few MB).
   - **Metric:** the lockstep fraction L, the share of k-loop time in which all three warps of a scheduler are in
     their k-loops. Independent phases give ≈ 0.2–0.3 (the product's share squared); lockstep gives ≈ 1.
   - **Rule:** keep the idea if L ≥ 0.6. If L < 0.6 while the halves still add up, the register-file explanation
     holds: drop the idea.
2. **F-skew** (both settings) against the base, with the census on it.
   - Keep it if it is ≤ 0.95.
   - Build the rotation if the census shows the offsets collapsing back into lockstep, or if F-skew stays ≥ 0.97
     while the base had L ≥ 0.6.
3. **The ceiling:** F-no-tp (0.619 at `6ebc795d`) and F-tc-no-product, which has not run against a VEC_A base.

**Follow-ups if it works.** Once the TP runs under the k-loop, the two halves share the issue slots. TC_PRESPLIT is
now idea 16 on its own. 16-edge batches with the edges as the mma's M dimension remain a follow-up: they halve the
W splits per edge, and the ledger's tensor-core forward entry names them as the version that would pay.

**Other GPUs.** The A100's halves add up too, so the census and F-skew are worth one A100 run. A default is per
GPU, after decision-66 A/Bs.

**Effort.**
- census and F-skew: half a day in `tools/omat0_kernel_lab.py`;
- rotation: one to two days. Warps that run out of chunks must keep arriving at the barriers until the last group
  finishes; tests on uneven neighbourhoods.

## Closed in round 2

### 2. Degree-pure path parts (DEGREE_PARTS): adopted

- `fused_forward.DEGREE_PARTS` (`3ed00b73`, defaults in `891bf370`): part 0 holds the four scalar-input paths
  (x row 0), part 1 the six vector-input paths (x rows 1–3). Bitwise.
- MD b/a: H200 0.9818 / 0.9845; A100-SXM4 0.9920 / 0.9932; RTX 5060 Ti 0.9980 / 1.0032 (off there).
- The gate passes on the H200 and on an A100 PCIe. Benchmark_loup: H200 5.504 ms, A100 PCIe 10.89 ms.
- Lab: 1,777.9 → 1,691.3 µs on the H200, −87 µs. That is 29 % of F-no-xload's 295 µs; finding 2 of round 2 and
  idea 15 follow from it.

### 3. Piece-sorted radial tables: dead

- RTX 5060 Ti: the sorted kernel took 0.866 of the base, but the sort alone 155 µs against the 509 µs kernel.
- On the H200 only interaction 2's table (114.7 µs) was left. Idea 14 halves it another way.

### 4. S tile: dropped

- F-s-tile 0.966 on the H200 at `891bf370` (≈ 57 µs, before the cost of building S); A100 PCIe 0.951.
- With DEGREE_PARTS the coefficient multiplies are 2.9 % (F-tp-no-coef 0.971).

### 6. One launch per BlockLinear (SEGMENT_GROUPED): rejected

- Bound: 15–52 µs per group of four on the H200 and the A100 PCIe.
- Built (`f8facf08`) and bitwise, but the A100-SXM4 MD step gave 0.9987 / 1.0004. On the H200 SEGMENT_ROWS itself
  is off, so the switch does not act there.

### 7. Bulk copies for the a/sh staging: dropped

- F-no-ash4 0.955 on the H200 and 0.971 on the A100-SXM4: the staging is 3–4.5 %, not enough for the mbarriers.

### 8. PDL between our own kernels: dropped

- 1.8–4.7 µs per group of four GEMMs on the H200 (A100 PCIe −1.3 to 4.5 µs), below the 3 µs stop rule.

## Rejected in round 1

### 1. Term-pair interleaved w′ value table (TABLE_PAIRS)

- **Idea:** store TABLE_W's value table as `[groups, 3, Q, 2]`, so that a warp's `float2` load covers 256
  contiguous bytes instead of 768 B over six lines. Bitwise.
- **Measured:**
  - H200 1.0008 / 1.0003;
  - A100-SXM4 1.0058 / 1.0062;
  - RTX 5060 Ti 1.0024 / 0.9998.
- **Lesson:** the L1-wavefront model of the first version was wrong on every GPU. Either the q-major reads already
  coalesce within the lines they touch, or the w′ waits on latency rather than L1 data. The SASS census and
  finding 1 point to latency.
- **Where it could return:** as the layout for idea 12(a), if the table loads there must bypass L1. With the
  q-major layout, the three term-pair loads of a weight reuse the same lines through L1.

### 9. Hoist the piece scale out of the w′ slope (HOIST_SCALE)

- **Measured:**
  - H200 1.0057 / 1.0017;
  - A100 1.0104 / 0.9988;
  - RTX 5060 Ti 0.9985 / 0.9991.
- **Lesson:** 40 FMULs per edge are 1.6 % of the w′'s instructions, and the w′ is not issue-bound.

## Considered and dropped

- **Reassociating the product through the receiver sum** (Σ_k W_k Σ_e a_e[k] T(x_j, Y_e) instead of
  Σ_e (a_e W) T(x_j, Y_e)).
  - With interaction 2's full x it needs 64 times the accumulators per lane. It pays only for species-only x, as
    in interaction 1's reassociated first sum.
  - w has rank 64 here, and rad-low-rank found no lower rank (65 of 65).
- **Separate accumulators for the small products on the H200 (`tc-2acc`, `tc-3acc`).**
  - On the A100 they spilled 144 and 280 B at 168 registers (1.306, 1.504); the H200 has the same cap.
  - Idea 16(b) gets the same chain lengths from the order alone.
- **Two receivers of a chunk walked together by sender, to share x loads.** It doubles the accumulators (24 → 48
  in part 1).
- **A copy of a block's senders' x in shared memory.** The senders of a block's 24 receivers are ≈ 200 atoms, i.e.
  ≈ 300 KB of x rows for part 1.
- **The w′ reading idea 14's stored w.** The w′ still needs w′ = dw/dr from the table, and the table costs at most
  7.5 % (one slab).
- **Changing the w′'s lane-to-channel ownership to pairs or quads.**
  - Measured: the channel-pair variant ran 1.126 in the H200 sweep (128 registers, so 16 instead of 20 warps per
    SM). Quads need more registers.
- **Two edges per w′ warp (one dout load for both).** It has the same register problem as the channel pairs. Idea
  12 gets the dout reuse without it.
- **A dout locality walk measured by BACKWARD_WALK.** The first version cited BACKWARD_WALK's 1.000 on the H200
  against this. BACKWARD_WALK walks the `edge_outer_sum` backward (interaction 1), not the w′, so the w′'s dout
  locality was untested. It is now idea 12.
- **Deferred CG in the forward (the TP after the receiver sum).**
  - Per edge and lane it accumulates the (m1, m2) outer products and contracts with CG once per receiver: ≈ 36 %
    fewer floating-point operations in the TP.
  - But the accumulators grow from 16–24 to 41–47 per lane, on a kernel whose TP loop holds 149 live registers and
    whose k-loop holds 163. It would cost warps (tc-warps10: 1.081).
- **A rotated-frame (SO(2), eSCN-style) TP.**
  - Rotating each edge onto z cuts the CG terms from 92 to 20 per channel.
  - Rotating the messages back costs ≈ 202 FMAs per channel at l ≤ 3.
- **FP64 tensor cores (DMMA) for the TP.** TC_TP's lesson applies: the preparation of the operands costs as much as
  the FP32 terms. The tensor pipe is also the product's bottleneck.
- **Fewer pieces of higher degree, to fit TABLE_W into the 50 MB L2 for a SIMT-slab forward.**
  - Measured: simt-slab-real 1.076, but simt-slab3 0.838 on the H200, so the table's 164 MB is what costs.
  - Degree 9 on four-times-wider pieces shrinks the table only 2.4× (≈ 68 MB), and it adds ≈ 70 % Horner work to
    the w′.
- **Removing the GEMMs' clone/cat copies.** SEGMENT_ROWS does this for the 1:1 irrep products (A100 default) and
  gave 1.0006 / 1.0000 on the H200.
- **A Hilbert or Morton atom order for gather locality.**
  - x is 10 MB and L2-resident.
  - The forward's work counter spreads receiver chunks over all SMs anyway.
  - The research engine's Morton order gave no gain.
  - Idea 15 hides the x latency without a new order.
- **Radial tables on a side stream beside interaction 1's atom chain.**
  - Measured: launch-two-streams (0.985–1.151×) and lin-concurrent-branches were rejected.
  - Reason: it is the same overlap mechanism.
  - Re-probe it only if the H200 timeline shows interaction 1's atom chain below 50 % SM occupancy.
- **DMMA float64 tensor cores for the product.**
  - Reason: 66.9 TFLOPS against ≈ 170 measured for the mma.sync 3xTF32 product.
- **Radial tables evaluated inside the fused forward.**
  - Reason: eight blocks restage each edge, 8 × 1.6 KB per edge ≈ 4.7 GB of L2 reads per call. This is the
    same failure as omat0-table-in-forward.
- **Running the w′ in (pair, piece) order.**
  - Reason: the table reads become local, but dout (20 KB per edge, 102 MB in all) becomes random.
  - Measured: one slab (0.925) caps the gain from table locality at 7.5 %.
- **An S tile in the w′'s FACTORED sums.**
  - Reason: u = Sᵀg saves only the u FMAs; v keeps the CG form, ≈ 228 → ≈ 218 instructions.
- **Split-K or stream-K for the small GEMMs.**
  - Ledger: lin-split-k was rejected.
  - Reason: it also changes the sums.
- **Smaller CUTLASS tiles.**
  - Measured: omat0-cutlass-tile was rejected on the A100 (1.012–1.017).
  - Reason: the warps per SM stay the same.
- **Skipping skin-shell edges.**
  - Reason: 373,760 edges is 74.75 per atom, the count inside 6 Å for SrTiO3's density (≈ 76), so the force
    call already sees only edges inside the cutoff.

## Interactions

- **Idea 14 halves the k-loops.**
  - Ideas 10, 16 and 11 act on the k-loops that remain (pass 1, or the product pass), so their ceilings about halve
    if 14 lands first.
  - 14's own ceiling grows when the product pass runs faster.
- **Shared memory decides what combines in part 1** (232,448 B per block):
  - today 175,104 B; 15(b) adds 36,864 B; 16(a) needs the fragment-sized planes (211,968 B in all);
  - 15(b) and 16(a) together need ≈ 249 KB, which does not fit;
  - 14's TP pass has no W slice, so 15(b) fits there freely.

  So a likely end state is 14 (B), with 16(a) in the product pass and 15 in the TP pass.
- **Ideas 10 and 16(b)** both attack the k-loop's idle time. The census (10) and the HMMA microbenchmark (16) tell
  which applies. 16(a) is independent of both.
- **Ideas 16(a) and 11** both remove the W splits; 11 is also the excluded wgmma.
- **Idea 15(a)** is independent and cheap, and frees registers that 16(a) may need.
- **Idea 12** (the w′) is independent of the forward work.
- **Ideas 13 and 5** are independent and small.

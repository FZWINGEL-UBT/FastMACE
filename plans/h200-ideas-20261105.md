### 22(iii) and 24 as lab transforms

`lab_round12.py` (notes folder `h200-ideas-round12-cpu/`): `round12_variants(kernel)` returns name → (kind,
transform, ()) for the lab's `forward_variants`, as round 11's file did. No variant changes the launch rule or the
shared request.

| Variant | Kind | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `iii` | exact | 22(iii), branch-free, with the render's `#pragma unroll 2` k-loop |
| `iii-nf` | exact | `iii` plus a copy of the k-loop without the 8 flush checks, taken when no receiver of batch n lies beyond the current row (`!__any_sync(FULL, p_rv > cur)`) |
| `iii-u1` | exact | `iii` with the k-loop unrolled once |
| `iii-br` | exact | round 9's prototype form, the TP behind `b < p_nb` after the k-step's HMMAs: the control |
| `fold` | rounding | 24 on the plan's render |
| `iii-fold`, `iii-nf-fold` | rounding | 24 on `iii` and `iii-nf`, every copy of the TP folded alike |

- **Guards:** each transform rebuilds every part of the render from the pieces it reads and raises, as the lab's
  `_sub` does, unless the rebuild equals the part byte for byte. It therefore applies only to the form it was written
  for: W_GROUP, VEC_A, STORE_W off, X_LATE with or without K_PIPE, and LANE_INDICES (H200) or the A100-SXM4's
  broadcast index loads. Every variant keeps the staging text that `forward_launcher` reads as VEC_A, and refuses a
  result without it (round 11's ILLEGAL_ADDRESS).
- **Renders:** both GPUs' X_LATE renders (round 9's lab renders with `lab_round11.py`'s `xlate`) and, from before
  K_PIPE's A/B on the A100-SXM4 closed, both with K_PIPE (`xlate-kp`). All seven variants apply to all four renders.
- **Checked against main** (`3fab8e7a`, whose `fused_forward.py` is unchanged since `fc0d7531`): main's own
  `apply_x_late`, run on round 11's base renders (`runs/round11/r11-h200` and `r11-a100`), gives the X_LATE inputs
  above byte for byte on both GPUs, and the seven transforms of main's render are the built sources byte for byte.
  The lab's base on main is the production op's source, i.e. this render. Main's K_PIPE render differs from
  `xlate-kp` in two comment lines per part only, and the transforms apply to it too.
- **22(iii) as built:**
  - x of edge b + 1 is gathered in k-step b (at b = 7 lane 8's sender is −1, so zeros);
  - batch n + 1's sh waits in one float4 per lane until batch n's TP is done;
  - batch n's receivers and senders sit in one register per lane and are shuffled per edge, on the A100-SXM4 too;
  - w of edge b comes from r_s, which keeps batch n's rows until a second pair barrier before the C writes (3
    barriers per batch);
  - a chunk's remaining rows are flushed at the hand-over after the k-loop, and empty chunks get zero rows at once;
  - sh_s and r_s are zeroed once per part, so the first TP reads zeros.
- **Exactness:** each edge's TP keeps its operations and their order, and the flushes write the same rows. A
  missing edge (the empty slots of a chunk's last batch, or a warp waiting for its partner) adds w (x e c) with
  x = sh = w = 0, i.e. ±0. That changes an accumulator only if it is −0.0, which only an underflow makes, and
  `torch.equal` counts ±0 equal.

### The conductor's reading

The conductor reads the forward's largest stall as "wait" on fixed-latency chains (1.44 cycles per issued
instruction), and the not-selected share (0.91) as schedulers that already have other warps to pick; so an exact win
must remove instructions (idea 24, the splits) or shorten chains (the instruction order, ILP across the k-loop's two
batches). The first half holds. The second reads the ncu differently, and the cheapest exact cut is neither:

- **"Wait" is the k-loop's chains.** Of the forward's 26.97 points of wait, 17.29 are in the k-loop: 7.45 the HMMAs'

- **Not-selected marks collisions, not spare work.** 72 % of it is in the k-loop, nearly half of that on HMMAs,

  chain pays wherever no other warp was ready. The collisions call for spreading the k-loops over time (22(iii));

1. **More warps are not the route.** `wg14` and `wg16` lost 2.9 % and 1.7 %. Without x live across the k-loop

2. **Both parts run far above their tensor time:** 626 and 1,027 µs against 432 and 650. Idea 25 needed part 0 near

3. **No pipe and not the issue slots bound the forward.** The tensor pipe is active 38 % of the time: the HMMAs
   occupy it ≈ 610 µs (0.381 × 1,600), not the ≈ 1,082 µs of round 1's microbenchmark, which paid its own splits and

### The conductor's questions

**(1) Where do the x loads' ≈ 150 µs come from?** Mostly not from the loads (finding 4). About 116 µs are TP
arithmetic that a constant x lets ptxas fold. The gathers cost ≤ ≈ 37 µs on the H200, part of it their address math,

  neighbour both receivers share), and x through shared memory lost before (15(b)).

- the w′ over edges stored physically in mirror order is open: 0.887–0.899 of the kernel on the H200, ≈ 2 % of the
  step after the copies;
- 2,098 atom pairs (0.56 % of the edges, at the cutoff) have only one direction, so a kernel cannot assume strict
  pairs.

### What round 3 says

1. **The forward's k-loop is limited by its shared-memory loads.**
   - Removing the W splits without touching the loads gains 15 % (F-wfrag-no-wsplit 0.853).
   - Replacing the same splits by a second LDS.128 per fragment loses 7 % (TC_WFRAG_SPLIT 1.070).
   - So the second load per fragment costs ≈ 22 % of the forward, more than the 96 split instructions per k-step
     it replaces.
   - A warp's 8-edge batch in part 1 moves ≈ 550 shared-memory wavefronts (census under "Where the time goes"), 70 %
     of them W fragments: 96 LDS.128 per batch.
2. **The warp tile is the lever.**
   - The product is a GEMM whose warp tile is 192 W rows × 8 edges in part 1 (128 × 8 in part 0). Each m16n8k8 MMA
     takes a 512-byte W fragment for only 8 edges.
   - Per MMA, that tile reads 2.5 times the shared bytes of a 48 × 32 tile, which has the same 48 accumulators.
   - 16-edge batches failed on registers because they doubled the accumulators. Idea 17 keeps the accumulators
     and changes the shape.
3. **F-skew did not test de-phasing as planned.**
   - Its anchor (`const int gid = lane >> 2, tq = lane & 3;`, line 117 of the run's `forward.cu`) is inside the
     batch loop. So the odd warps slept before every batch's k-loop, ≈ 118 times each on the H200, not once before
     their first chunk.
   - The cost barely grows with the requested time (1.094 at 100 ns, 1.114 at 1 µs). A sleep floor of about a
     microsecond per `__nanosleep` would do that; so would a cost of de-phasing itself. The variant cannot tell them
     apart.
   - A one-time offset (the same line before `while (true)`) has not been measured. The conductor dropped idea 10;
     the one-line re-test is optional under "Next measurements".
4. **dout locality is worth nothing in the w′ (FF-dout-one-row 1.001).** What the w′ gains from mirror order
   (0.887–0.899) is the locality of its sh and dsh rows.

### Corrections to revision 3

- **Idea 16 is closed.** Its step (a) is TC_WFRAG_SPLIT in all but name, and it lost. Step (b) needs more shared
  loads or more registers, the two things the k-loop lacks.
- **Idea 14 cannot assume strict pairs:** 0.56 % of the edges have no mirror; they own themselves. (Round 4 found

## 20. Unbiased 3xTF32 products: NbCl5's stress headroom

**Status.** Parked after round 7. F-bias found the tensor cores' accumulation truncating; B1 removes ≈ 40 % of the
forward's slope for 1.5 % of the H200's forward (0.3 % on the A100-SXM4), and the gate passes (NbCl5 1.96 of 2), so
nothing is adopted. B1 is ready if a gate needs headroom ("Round 7: F-bias"). From revision 7: the conductor's
question how NbCl5's stress headroom could be won back if 14(A)'s order failed the gate.

**The answer: no sum dominates; a bias does.**

**The offset.** In the gate's value files at `7f7ef7db`, each crystal's production stress error is the float32
calculator's error pattern plus an isotropic offset:

| Crystal | Production − float32 calculator, diagonal (1e-8 eV/Å³) | Off-diagonal | Ratio | Ratio without the offset |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NbCl5 | +7.23 (sd 0.70 over six configurations) | ≤ 1.33 | 1.958 | 0.954 |
| SrTiO3 3×3×3 | +24.5 (sd 3.0) | ≤ 6.3 | 0.925 | 0.938 |
| NaCl | +0.87 (sd 0.43) | ≤ 0.44 | 0.920 | 0.902 |

- The total energies carry an offset too, with one sign in every configuration: +2.0 meV (NbCl5), +0.39 (SrTiO3),
  +0.10 (NaCl), against the float32 calculator.
- The common pattern is most likely the float32 input (positions and cell), which both calculators share.
- SrTiO3 passes with the largest offset because its float32 calculator's own isotropic error is −4 to −22e-8.

**Where it comes from.** The A100 bisection at `3812b56e` (`gate-table.txt` in
`results/omat0-tp-backward-20261104/a100-sxm4-stress-bisect-3812b56e/`; NbCl5 limits 1.23–1.49e-7):

| Switched off | NbCl5 stress ratio | Energy ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| nothing (three runs) | 2.53–2.97 | 14.5–15.0 |
| one other default each (`no-tables`, `no-tablew`, `no-block`, `degree10`) | 2.40–3.10 | 10.9–15.2 |
| `tf32x3_gemms` | 1.30 | 2.07 |
| the tensor-core forward | 1.63 | 11.15 |
| both | 0.42 | 0.92 |

Both 3xTF32 paths carry the offset, the GEMMs the larger share (≈ 55 % of the stress excess, most of the energy's).

**Why 3xTF32 is biased.** A float64 model of the splits (NumPy, 2·10⁵ random dot products; products exact; the
scratch scripts `split_bias*.py`):
- **Our mask split** (`TC_SPLIT = "mask"`): the high part is truncated, so the low part has the value's sign. The
  tensor core truncates the low part once more, and the dropped low·low term has the product's sign. Each product
  shrinks by 2.74e-7 on average (relative, toward zero).
- **CUTLASS's 3xTF32** (v4.8.1, `MmaTensorOpFastF32::MmaFastF32` in `mma_tensor_op_fast_f32.h`: big part
  `round_toward_zero`, small part `round_half_ulp_truncate`): only the dropped small·small term is biased, −0.59e-7.
- **Rounded high parts** ("round", or B1 below): 0.00.
- **If the tensor cores truncate their FP32 accumulation**, as Fasi et al. found on the A100 (round-toward-zero
  normalisation), each mma loses another half ulp of its accumulator on average. Whatever the split, the model
  then gives −4.8e-7 at K = 64 (the forward: 8 k-steps of 3 mmas), −10e-7 at K = 128 and −20e-7 at K = 256. K = 128
  holds 71 % of the H200's GEMM outputs (`shapes.json` of `runs/mirror-h200`).

Two observations favour the truncating accumulation:
- With the splits alone, the forward (−2.74e-7) would outweigh the GEMMs (−0.59e-7), yet the bisection gives the
  GEMMs the larger share. A truncating accumulation weighs the GEMMs' longer K more.
- The RTX 5060 Ti's gates (`results/omat0-kernel-lab-20261103`, 3 November): SrTiO3's energy error was 0.73 meV
  with SIMT, 1.15 with "round" and 1.39 with "mask". (1.15 − 0.73) / (1.39 − 1.15) = 1.75; the model's ratio of the
  accumulation's share to the mask split's is 4.83 / 2.75 = 1.76. One sample each, on sm_120.

**14(A)'s order.**
- A summation order cannot move a bias. It adds zero-mean noise of about the size by which a float32 calculation's
  maximum error moves between runs.
- The A100 bisection's ten NbCl5 limits ranged 1.23–1.49e-7: the float32 calculator's own maximum error moves by
  ±0.7e-8 (±10 %) between runs. That alone moves NbCl5's ratio by ±0.2, and 14(A)'s order would move it by about
  ±0.1. Today's margin is 0.04 (0.29e-8).
- So 14(A) may fail, and so may a rerun of today's code. NbCl5's ratio was 2.05 under the A100 plan on the H200
  (hopper sweep, 5 November), 1.93 at `62556704` and 1.96 at `7f7ef7db`.
- Round 7: 14(A)'s gate gave 2.076, +0.12 on today's 1.96.

**Winning the headroom back**, cheapest first. None of these changes a summation order.
1. **B1, the forward.**
   - Round the W values to 22 significant bits (round to nearest even) as `tc_wfill` fills the slice. Under the mask
     split they are then exact: 11 bits in the high part, at most 11 in the low part.
   - Split a with a rounded high part (`hi = (x + 0x1000) & 0xffffe000`, the low part raw). Its low part then has a
     random sign, so the tensor core's truncation of it and the dropped low·low term average out.
   - Model: bias −2.74e-7 → 0.00; error rms 1.18 → 0.59 units of 2^-24 of the row scale.
   - Cost: one IADD per a value, 4 per k-step and warp at G = 2, ≈ +10 µs: F-wfrag-no-wsplit saved 248 µs for 96
     split instructions per k-step, ≈ 2.6 µs each. Rounding both operands' high parts instead costs ≈ +70 µs
     (28 values per k-step) for the same bias.
2. **B2, the GEMMs.** A copy of `MmaTensorOpFastF32` with the big part rounded (`round_half_ulp_truncate`) for one
   operand; rebuild `tf32x3_sm80.cubin` and `tf32x3_sm90.cubin`. Model: −0.59e-7 → +0.3e-7 (the small part's
   half-up ties). Cost: about none, one integer add per element as the fragments are converted.
3. **B3, the accumulation, only if F-bias finds it truncating.** Partial sums added by FADD, which rounds to
   nearest. In the model, a fresh accumulator per k-step takes K = 64's −4.8e-7 to −0.4e-7, and four k-slices take
   K = 128's −10e-7 to −2.3e-7.
   - The forward: 4 FADD per tile and k-step (48 at G = 2) and 4 registers per tile in flight. Too dear at 168
     registers; per two k-steps at half the cost, or with the registers of idea 22's step (i).
   - The GEMMs: CUTLASS's serial split-K (deterministic), or a warp-level operator that adds each k-iteration's
     fresh fragment.

**Expected.**
- If the splits carry the offset (exact accumulation): B1 and B2 remove it, ratio ≈ 0.95.
- If the accumulation truncates: B1 and B2 remove only the splits' part, 36 % of the forward's bias at K = 64 and

## Considered and dropped

In revision 9:

- **x or the TP's accumulators parked in shared memory during the k-loop**, to free registers for warps. x through
  shared memory is closed (15(b)); the accumulators would add shared loads to every TP, on a kernel whose short
  scoreboard is already 0.26 cycles per instruction. Idea 22(i) frees the same registers without them.
- **Part 1 split into two parts of three paths**, for fewer accumulators per warp. Both halves gather x rows 1–3,
  and halving those gathers is what DEGREE_PARTS paid for.
- **The S tile staged lane-parallel** (idea 4 again, against the FP32 work). F-s-tile bounded it at 3.4 % before
  building S; idea 24 takes about half of that without S.
- **One pass per mirror pair, the reverse message by atomics.** One w per pair, but ≈ 20 KB of messages per reverse
  edge, ≈ 3.8 GB of atomics per force call.
- **A two-pass product with a in TF32.** Its error is up to 2⁻¹¹ of a, ≈ 8,000 times float32's rounding, against a
  gate that allows 2×.
- **Fewer split instructions:** the raw value as the high operand (the tensor core reads only TF32's bits) saves a
  register, not an instruction, since the low part still needs the masked value.

In revision 8:

- **W stored pre-split again, now at G = 2** (TC_WFRAG_SPLIT on W_GROUP). At round 3's prices, doubling G = 2's 48
  W LDS.128 per warp and batch costs ≈ 11 % of the forward, and removing its 384 W splits saves ≈ 7 %.
- **x rows 1–3 interleaved per channel**, one 16-byte gather per lane instead of three. The kernel review's F2 found
  that the gathers' cost is their traffic, and the layout keeps it (12 B per lane, 16 with padding).
- **Arrive/wait barriers for W_GROUP.** Each warp still waits for its partner at both points of the batch; the 2.1 %
  of F-wg-no-bar is the pair's imbalance, which a split barrier does not remove.
- **A reduce-scatter for the w′'s dsh and dr reductions.** That is BUTTERFLY, off on the H200 since `7f7ef7db`.

Other ways to cut the forward's shared loads (fourth round):

- **16 edges per warp in two path halves** (48 accumulators; each W fragment serves two n-tiles, as in idea 17 at
  G = 2). The first half's w waits in r_s while the second half runs, and a and sh cover 16 edges. So each warp's
  slices double (9,472 → 18,944 B), and 12 warps need 288,768 B of the 232,448 B. Idea 17 makes the same split
  across two warps, whose slices already exist.
- **The TP on the MMA's accumulator layout, without the r_s round trip** (96 of ≈ 550 wavefronts per batch). A
  lane holds w for four channels × two edges there, so the TP's message accumulators would grow fourfold (16–24 →
  64–96 per lane), and the receiver sum would need shuffles across the four lanes of a channel. The round trip
  itself costs 0.2 % (F-tc-no-redistribute 0.998, round 4).
- **The edges as the MMA's M dimension** (a as operand A, W as operand B). The shared bytes per MMA depend on the
  warp tile's shape, not on which operand holds W. M = 16 also wants 16 edges per warp, the accumulator problem of
  the 16-edge batches.
- **64-channel blocks.** A block would stage each edge's a once for two warps of 32 channels. Each warp's tile stays
  192 × 8, so its W loads per MMA stay too; the staging is ≈ 20 of ≈ 550 wavefronts.
- **W in shared memory below 32 bits per element.** The 3xTF32 product uses about 22 of W's 24 significant bits
  (hi and lo). Fewer stored bits is a lower-precision product, i.e. a 16-bit path for the gate to judge, and a
  packed hi/lo pair still needs 32 bits plus unpacking in place of the split.

Earlier rounds:

- **Reassociating the product through the receiver sum** (Σ_k W_k Σ_e a_e[k] T(x_j, Y_e) instead of
  Σ_e (a_e W) T(x_j, Y_e)).
  - With interaction 2's full x it needs 64 times the accumulators per lane. It pays only for species-only x, as
    in interaction 1's reassociated first sum.
  - w has rank 64 here, and rad-low-rank found no lower rank (65 of 65).
- **Separate accumulators for the small products on the H200 (`tc-2acc`, `tc-3acc`).**
  - On the A100 they spilled 144 and 280 B at 168 registers (1.306, 1.504); the H200 has the same cap.
  - Idea 16(b) would have got the same chain lengths from the order alone; round 3 closed it.
- **Two receivers of a chunk walked together by sender, to share x loads.** It doubles the accumulators (24 → 48
  in part 1).
- **A copy of a block's senders' x in shared memory.** The senders of a block's 24 receivers are ≈ 200 atoms, i.e.
  ≈ 300 KB of x rows for part 1.
- **The w′ reading idea 14(A)'s stored w.** Pass 1 stores w for the partner edges only, the w′ still needs
  w′ = dw/dr from the table, and the table costs at most 7.5 % (one slab).
- **Changing the w′'s lane-to-channel ownership to pairs or quads.**
  - Measured: the channel-pair variant ran 1.126 in the H200 sweep (128 registers, so 16 instead of 20 warps per
    SM). Quads need more registers.
- **Two edges per w′ warp (one dout load for both).** It has the same register problem as the channel pairs, and
  round 3 found the dout reuse worth nothing (idea 12, FF-dout-one-row 1.001).
- **A dout locality walk measured by BACKWARD_WALK.** The first version cited BACKWARD_WALK's 1.000 on the H200
  against this. BACKWARD_WALK walks the `edge_outer_sum` backward (interaction 1), not the w′, so the w′'s dout
  locality was untested. It became idea 12, dead in round 3 (FF-dout-one-row 1.001).
- **Deferred CG in the forward (the TP after the receiver sum).**
  - Per edge and lane it accumulates the (m1, m2) outer products and contracts with CG once per receiver: ≈ 36 %
    fewer floating-point operations in the TP.
  - But the accumulators grow from 16–24 to 41–47 per lane, on a kernel whose TP loop holds 149 live registers and
    whose k-loop holds 163. It would cost warps (tc-warps10: 1.081).
- **A rotated-frame (SO(2), eSCN-style) TP.**
  - Rotating each edge onto z cuts the CG terms from 92 to 20 per channel.
  - Rotating the messages back costs ≈ 202 FMAs per channel at l ≤ 3.
- **FP64 tensor cores (DMMA) for the TP.** TC_TP's lesson applies: the preparation of the operands costs as much as
  the FP32 terms. The product also keeps the tensor pipe busy for most of the k-loop (≈ 875 of ≈ 1,000 µs at the
  measured HMMA rate, idea 17).
- **Fewer pieces of higher degree, to fit TABLE_W into the 50 MB L2 for a SIMT-slab forward.**
  - Measured: simt-slab-real 1.076, but simt-slab3 0.838 on the H200, so the table's 164 MB is what costs.
  - Degree 9 on four-times-wider pieces shrinks the table only 2.4× (≈ 68 MB), and it adds ≈ 70 % Horner work to
    the w′.
- **Removing the GEMMs' clone/cat copies.** SEGMENT_ROWS does this for the 1:1 irrep products (A100 default) and
  gave 1.0006 / 1.0000 on the H200.
- **A Hilbert or Morton atom order for gather locality.**
  - x is 10 MB and L2-resident.
  - The forward's work counter spreads receiver chunks over all SMs anyway.
  - The research engine's Morton order gave no gain.
  - LANE_INDICES (15(a)) took the index chain out of the x gathers without a new order.
- **Radial tables on a side stream beside interaction 1's atom chain.**
  - Measured: launch-two-streams (0.985–1.151×) and lin-concurrent-branches were rejected.
  - Reason: it is the same overlap mechanism.
  - Re-probe it only if the H200 timeline shows interaction 1's atom chain below 50 % SM occupancy.
- **DMMA float64 tensor cores for the product.**
  - Reason: 66.9 TFLOPS against ≈ 170 measured for the mma.sync 3xTF32 product.
- **Radial tables evaluated inside the fused forward.**
  - Reason: eight blocks restage each edge, 8 × 1.6 KB per edge ≈ 4.7 GB of L2 reads per call. This is the
    same failure as omat0-table-in-forward.
- **Running the w′ in (pair, piece) order.**
  - Reason: the table reads become local, but dout (20 KB per edge, 102 MB in all) becomes random.
  - Measured: one slab (0.925) caps the gain from table locality at 7.5 %.
- **An S tile in the w′'s FACTORED sums.**
  - Reason: u = Sᵀg saves only the u FMAs; v keeps the CG form, ≈ 228 → ≈ 218 instructions.
- **Split-K or stream-K for the small GEMMs.**
  - Ledger: lin-split-k was rejected.
  - Reason: it also changes the sums.
- **Smaller CUTLASS tiles.**
  - Measured: omat0-cutlass-tile was rejected on the A100 (1.012–1.017).
  - Reason: the warps per SM stay the same.
- **Skipping skin-shell edges.**
  - Reason: 373,760 edges is 74.75 per atom, the count inside 6 Å for SrTiO3's density (≈ 76), so the force
    call already sees only edges inside the cutoff.

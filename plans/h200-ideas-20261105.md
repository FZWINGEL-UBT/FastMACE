The w′ research of the same day (section "Mirror-pair w′"):
- sharing the table work between an edge and its mirror buys nothing beyond the mirror order's cache reuse
  (`B-mirror-share` 1.197 on the H200, where the branch costs more than it saves);
- the w′ over edges stored physically in mirror order is open: 0.887–0.899 of the kernel on the H200, ≈ 2 % of the
  step after the copies;
- 2,098 atom pairs (0.56 % of the edges, at the cutoff) have only one direction, so a kernel cannot assume strict
  pairs.

### What round 3 says

1. **The forward's k-loop is limited by its shared-memory loads.**
   - Removing the W splits without touching the loads gains 15 % (F-wfrag-no-wsplit 0.853).
   - Replacing the same splits by a second LDS.128 per fragment loses 7 % (TC_WFRAG_SPLIT 1.070).
   - So the second load per fragment costs ≈ 22 % of the forward, more than the 96 split instructions per k-step
     it replaces.
   - A warp's 8-edge batch in part 1 moves ≈ 550 shared-memory wavefronts (census under "Where the time goes"), 70 %
     of them W fragments: 96 LDS.128 per batch.
2. **The warp tile is the lever.**
   - The product is a GEMM whose warp tile is 192 W rows × 8 edges in part 1 (128 × 8 in part 0). Each m16n8k8 MMA
     takes a 512-byte W fragment for only 8 edges.
   - Per MMA, that tile reads 2.5 times the shared bytes of a 48 × 32 tile, which has the same 48 accumulators.
   - 16-edge batches failed on registers because they doubled the accumulators. Idea 17 keeps the accumulators
     and changes the shape.
3. **F-skew did not test de-phasing as planned.**
   - Its anchor (`const int gid = lane >> 2, tq = lane & 3;`, line 117 of the run's `forward.cu`) is inside the
     batch loop. So the odd warps slept before every batch's k-loop, ≈ 118 times each on the H200, not once before
     their first chunk.
   - The cost barely grows with the requested time (1.094 at 100 ns, 1.114 at 1 µs). A sleep floor of about a
     microsecond per `__nanosleep` would do that; so would a cost of de-phasing itself. The variant cannot tell them
     apart.
   - A one-time offset (the same line before `while (true)`) has not been measured. The conductor dropped idea 10;
     the one-line re-test is optional under "Next measurements".
4. **dout locality is worth nothing in the w′ (FF-dout-one-row 1.001).** What the w′ gains from mirror order
   (0.887–0.899) is the locality of its sh and dsh rows.

### Corrections to revision 3

- **Idea 16 is closed.** Its step (a) is TC_WFRAG_SPLIT in all but name, and it lost. Step (b) needs more shared
  loads or more registers, the two things the k-loop lacks.
- **Idea 14 cannot assume strict pairs:** 0.56 % of the edges have no mirror. Those pairs show that the neighbour
  list's two lengths do not always agree to the bit. So (B) is bitwise only if the force call's edge vectors are
  exact negations, or are made so (see idea 14).
- **The census applies to round 3's base:** the runs' `forward.cu` is byte for byte the render this plan's SASS
  census used (the H200 gate run's, `STORE_W = false`).

### What round 2 says

1. **Launch structure does not reach the step.**
   - SEGMENT_GROUPED's bound saved 15–52 µs per group of four GEMMs, but the A100 MD step stayed at 0.9987 /
     1.0004. PDL saves less than 5 µs per group.
   - The two convolution kernels are 60 % of the step: the forward ≈ 1.69 ms and the w′ ≈ 1.6 ms. The third round
     of ideas (14–16) therefore went to their algorithm and data layout, and idea 13 to the end of the ranking.
2. **The x cost is not its bytes.**
   - DEGREE_PARTS halved the x rows each edge gathers (eight row gathers per edge and channel block, now four).
     The kernel gained 87 µs (1,777.9 → 1,691.3), 29 % of F-no-xload's 295 µs.
   - **The SASS at `891bf370` shows why.**
     - Both parts load a batch's eight senders into one register, one after the other.
     - Each load issues only after the previous value has passed its range check, and each edge's x loads
       follow its sender.
     - The a and sh `float4` loads issue after this chain, and the receivers last.
     - The chain is as long with half the rows.
   - **F-no-xload overstates the x cost:** with x a constant, ptxas folds the x·e·coef products and frees the
     24–32 x registers. Idea 15 adds a cleaner bound (F-x-local).
3. **The TP's arithmetic is not where its time goes on the H200.**
   - The coefficient multiplies cost 2.9 %, the S tile's per-lane product bounds at 3.4 %, and the staging at
     3–4.5 %.
   - What is left: the product (about half of the forward), the TP's latency and the x gathers.

### Corrections to revision 2

- **TC_PRESPLIT is not a mere follow-up.**
  - Revision 2 argued that the k-loop is tensor-bound, so fewer W splits would only free idle issue slots.
  - But the H200's pass bounds leave ≈ 110 µs of the k-loop's other work unhidden by the MMAs, and on the H200 the
    presplit planes fit beside 12 warps. It became idea 16, closed in round 3 (TC_WFRAG_SPLIT 1.070).
- **Mirror-edge sharing of the product is not dropped.**
  - Revision 2 dropped it for its 0.96 GB round trip.
  - But the H200's forward uses ≈ 5 % of the HBM bandwidth, while its k-loop (bound by its shared loads, round 3)
    and its TP latency bind. So the round trip can pay. It is now idea 14.
- **Idea 11's padding is gone** (the idea itself died on registers in round 3): with DEGREE_PARTS the parts are 4
  and 6 paths, i.e. 128 and 192 W rows, exact multiples of 64.
- **F-no-tp (0.619)** also drops the x gathers and frees their registers, so it bounds the TP and x together, not
  the TP alone.

### What the failures say

1. **The w′ is latency-bound.**
   - **What did not help:**
     - fewer L1 wavefronts on its table reads (TABLE_PAIRS);
     - 40 fewer FMULs per edge (HOIST_SCALE), which is 1.6 % of its instructions;
     - table locality (one slab 0.925, so L2 and HBM misses cost at most 7.5 %);
     - no atomics (1.089).
   - **The SASS census:**
     - A channel pass is 574 instructions: FFMA 330, LDG 90, FMUL 71, CS2R 30, FADD 20, IADD3 17, MOV 10, REDG 4.
     - The 90 loads match 30 for the table (three `float2` per path), 40 for dout, 16 for sh and 4 for x.
     - With four passes and the 202-instruction end reduction, an edge costs ≈ 2,500 warp instructions. Over
       373,760 edges that is ≈ 0.9 ms of issue at one instruction per cycle per scheduler, against 1.61 ms
       measured.
     - At most 89 registers are live (96 allocated, 20 warps per SM). The register caps of 72 and 80 spill.
     - The kernel is 984 static instructions (16 KB): its channel passes are a loop, so its code is small.
   - **So:** what can still pay is the latency of its loads. The 40 dout loads per pass are L2 round trips, because
     the ≈ 19 four-edge blocks of a receiver land on about as many SMs (idea 12). Round 3 found that worth nothing
     (FF-dout-one-row 1.001).
2. **The H200 forward runs its two halves one after the other.**
   - **The bounds at `27d27f21` (base 2,323 µs) add up:**
     - the product: no-product 0.527, i.e. 1,099 µs;
     - the TP with its x gathers: no-TP 0.669, i.e. 769 µs;
     - together 1,868 µs, which leaves 455 µs for staging and the rest.
     - The A100's bounds add up the same way: 3.25 ms = 1.71 ms product + 1.26 ms TP + rest.
   - **The product half looked bound by the tensor pipe at mma.sync's rate** (round 3 found its shared-memory loads
     limit it):
     - one MMA pass instead of three gives 0.717, about as much as no MMA at all (0.705);
     - the product alone took 1.082 ms with mma.sync and 0.554 ms with wgmma (the microbenchmark).
   - **The SASS census of `02a81e13` (KP = 64):**
     - **k-loop:** per k-step 30 `HMMA.1688.F32.TF32`, 86 TF32-split instructions (LOP3 + FADD) and 12 LDS. That
       is 240 HMMA, ≈ 690 split instructions and 96 LDS per 8-edge batch and warp.
     - **TP:** ≈ 95 instructions per edge and lane, 88 of them FMUL or FFMA (part 0: FMUL 45, FFMA 43). The
       receiver-change stores sit behind a branch.
     - **Whole batch:** ≈ 2,900 instructions per warp.
     - **HMMA rate:** at the product bound the tensor pipe takes ≈ 11–12 cycles per HMMA per scheduler.
       Three warps' k-loops then issue on only ≈ 40 % of the cycles, and during the TP the tensor pipe is idle.
   - **Registers:** the peak (163 of 168 live) is in the k-loop, where the x values for the TP (32 registers,
     gathered before the k-loop) and the 40 accumulators are live. The TP loop peaks at 149.
   - **Why the halves do not overlap is open.** After the W fill the warps take their own chunks, with no block
     barrier. So either they stay in lockstep by themselves, or the HMMAs starve the other warps' FP32
     instructions. Idea 10's census was not run; round 3 closed the idea.
3. **A faster product alone did not help.**
   - **WGMMA** halved the product, but its two W planes in shared memory left room for only 8 warps instead of 12.
     The TP, which needs warps to hide its latency, lost more than the product gained.
   - **The pipelined version** kept the A fragments and accumulators live across the TP (244 registers) and lost
     more.
   - **The lever** turned out to be the product's shared-memory loads (round 3): idea 17 shares them, and idea 14
     halves the product.
4. **On the RTX 5060 Ti the TP is its per-lane arithmetic.**
   - It is half the forward (F-no-tp 0.517).
   - Its coefficient multiplies (0.977), x loads (0.950) and sh loads (1.017) are small.

KP is the forward's k-loop length, padded to steps of 8 for the tensor cores:
- **At `27d27f21`:** K = 65 folded inputs, padded to 72, with a staged by scalar loads.
- **At `02a81e13`:** SKIP_ZERO_COLUMN drops the all-zero bias column (K = 64). That also activates VEC_A,

**The forward at `891bf370`** (SASS census of the H200 gate run's lab render):
- **Parts:** the z = 0 blocks run part 0, the z = 1 blocks part 1. With one block per SM, all part-0 blocks run
  before the part-1 blocks.
  - part 0: the 4 scalar-input paths, 16 accumulators per lane, x row 0 (one gather per edge and lane), 24 HMMA
    per k-step;
  - part 1: the 6 vector-input paths, 24 accumulators, x rows 1–3 (three gathers), 36 HMMA per k-step.
- **Resources:** 168 registers, no spills. Shared memory: the W region (61,440 B, sized by the SIMT layout's
  40-float rows; part 1's fragment-ordered slice uses 49,152 B of it) and 12 × 9,472 B of per-warp staging.
- **k-loop, part 1, per k-step:** 36 HMMA, 12 LDS.128 for W, 96 W-split instructions (12 tiles × 4 elements ×
  LOP3 + FADD), 4 for the a split. ptxas keeps 4–5 accumulator tiles in flight: dependent HMMAs are a median of
  13 instructions apart (minimum 3).
- **Staging:** the sender chain of "What round 2 says" (finding 2).
- **Code size:** 5,992 static instructions (96 KB). Part 1's region after the k-loop has 2,932 of them, 648
  stores among them: the TP is unrolled over the 8 edges, with 27 copies of the 24-store receiver flush
  inlined.

## 17. Squarer warp tiles: a warp group shares each W fragment across its batches

**Today.**
- Each warp owns its 8-edge batch from staging to message stores.
- In the k-loop it multiplies the whole W slice of its part by its batch's a. In part 1 that slice is 6 paths × 32
  channels = 192 rows, i.e. 12 m16 tiles, against one n8 tile of edges.
- Per k-step it loads 12 W fragments (LDS.128), splits them (96 LOP3 + FADD) and issues 36 HMMAs.
- All 12 warps of a block load the same W fragments, each for its own 8 edges.

**The change.**
- **Groups:** the warps form groups of G (2, 3 or 4). With 12 warps, a group of 4 holds one warp per scheduler.
- **Staging:** each warp still stages its own batch (a, sh, x), as today.
- **k-loop:** each warp takes 1/G of the part's W tiles, for all G batches of its group, i.e. G n-tiles.
  - Part 1 at G = 2: one m-tile of all 6 paths (6 tiles) × 2 n-tiles. At G = 4: 3 tiles × 4 n-tiles.
  - Either way 48 accumulators, as today; part 0 keeps its 32.
  - Each W fragment is loaded and split once for G batches, and feeds 3G HMMAs.
  - The a fragments of the n-tiles come from the G warps' own a_s slices (2 LDS.32 per n-tile and k-step).
- **Redistribution:** after the k-loop, each warp writes its accumulators into the owner warps' r_s, at the rows it
  computed. After a group barrier, each warp reads its own w from its own r_s and runs its own TP, as today.
- **What stays:** each warp's receivers, chunks, TP, flushes and message stores.
- **Why it is bitwise:** every w is the same MMAs of one W tile and one n-tile, k ascending, so w and the messages
  are bitwise as today.
- **Synchronisation:** two named barriers per batch (`bar.sync id, 32·G`).
  - The first follows the staging: the group's a_s are ready, and their r_s are free.
  - The second follows the r_s writes.
  - A warp that has run out of chunks keeps joining its group's k-loops with an empty batch until the whole group
    is done. A `bar.red.or` on "still has a batch" ends the loop, so the others still get their tiles.
- **Not a 16-edge batch:** those kept all paths in every warp and doubled the accumulators. Here a warp's
  accumulators stay at 48; only the tile changes shape, from 192 × 8 to 96 × 16 (G = 2) or 48 × 32 (G = 4).
- **Shared memory:** unchanged (175,104 B). Each warp's a_s, sh_s and r_s stay its own. The others read its a_s and
  write into its r_s.

**Per warp and 8-edge batch, part 1** (wavefronts as in the census under "Where the time goes"):

| W fragments, LDS.128 | 96 | 48 | 24 |
| a fragments, LDS.32 | 16 | 32 | 64 |

## 14. Pair-shared product: w once per mirror pair

**Mechanism.**
- **The symmetry.**
  - Every edge i ← j with shift s has a mirror j ← i with shift −s in the full neighbour list.
  - If the mirror's vector is formed as the exact negation, (p_i − p_j) − s = −((p_j − p_i) + s), both edges have
    the same length bit for bit.
  - Round 3's w′ research found 2,098 atom pairs (0.56 % of the edges) with only one direction, at the cutoff. So
    the cutoff test that builds the edge list does not always see the two lengths as equal. Whether the force
    call's own edge vectors are exact negations is the CPU check below.
  - OMAT-0's radial input is symmetric in the species pair: its fitted table rows for (s, t) and (t, s) are bitwise
    equal, which SYMMETRIC_PAIRS relies on.
  - So the radial features a, and every w the k-loop forms from them, are the same bits for both edges. Today
    both edges compute them. The product is about half of the forward (F-tc-no-product 0.527 at `27d27f21`), and
    with its staging about two thirds (F-no-tp 0.619 at `6ebc795d`).
- **Why it can pay on the H200.**
  - The edge and its mirror sit in different receivers' warps, so sharing needs w in memory: `[E/2, 1280]` floats,
    0.96 GB per call. Revision 2 dropped the idea for that.
  - But today's forward moves only ≈ 0.37 GB through HBM in 1.69 ms (a and sh once per part, and the messages;
    the H200 renders it with `STORE_W = false`, so no w is stored): ≈ 5 % of 4.8 TB/s. Its time goes to the
    k-loop's shared loads and the TP's latency, so the HBM has room.
- **At each neighbour-list rebuild** (beside the force call, as today):
  - one radix sort by (smaller atom, larger atom, shift) puts every edge next to its mirror;
  - each pair gets a slot p and an owner edge, and `pair_of[e]` maps both edges to p;
  - ownership goes by the parity of i + j, so that each receiver owns about half of its edges; self-images
    (i = j) go by the sign of the shift, and edges without a mirror own themselves;
  - the same permutation is what the w′'s open mirror-order layout needs, so one sort can serve both.
- **Variant (B), bitwise if each pair's w is equal:**
  1. **Product pass:** today's k-loop over the owners in pair order, with STORE_W on and no TP, writing
     `w_pair[p]`. The radial table evaluates a for the owners only. With no TP, this pass is a plain GEMM over the
     owner edges, so its warps can take idea 17's square tiles freely.
  2. **TP pass:** today's fused forward over all edges in receiver order, with the k-loop replaced by 4–6
     coalesced loads per edge and lane from `w_pair[pair_of[e]]`. It needs no W slice in shared memory and no
     k-loop registers, so it can run more warps per SM for the TP's latency.

  Every message is the same sum in the same order. Traffic: 0.96 GB written, 1.9 GB read.
- **Variant (A), rounding:** each receiver's edges are partitioned at rebuild time into owned and partner edges.
  - Pass 1 is today's fused forward over the owned edges, also writing w to the partner's slot.
  - Pass 2 is the product-free forward over the partner edges, starting each receiver from pass 1's message.
  - Traffic: 0.96 GB each way, plus 0.2 GB of messages. The sum order changes (owned edges first).
- **Making the pairs exact:** if the force call's edge vectors are not exact negations, the edge-vector kernel can
  compute each owner's vector once and store its negation for the partner. That moves the partner edges' vectors by
  up to an ulp, a rounding-class change for the gate, and makes each pair's w equal by construction.

**Attacks.** The product with its a staging (≈ 1,100 µs), and half of interaction 2's `radial_table_forward`
(114.7 µs), if nothing else needs a for every edge.

**Ceiling.** Split today's forward into P, the product with its a staging (F-no-tp 0.619 at `6ebc795d`,
≈ 1,100 µs), and T, the rest (the TP, the x gathers and the flushes, ≈ 590 µs at `891bf370`).
- Without the round trip, (B) and (A) both take ≈ ½ P + T ≈ 1,140 µs (0.67), i.e. −550 µs.
- The round trip: (B) writes 0.96 GB and reads 1.9 GB, (A) writes and reads 0.96 GB. Unhidden, at ≈ 4 TB/s, that
  adds ≈ 720 µs to (B) and ≈ 480 µs to (A).
- How much of it hides decides the idea. The product passes are bound by their shared loads, not by HBM, so their
  stores should hide. (B)'s TP pass and (A)'s partner pass would both have to read at ≈ 3 TB/s, with the loads
  issued a batch ahead.
- Both: ≈ −57 µs more from the radial table.

**Expected.** −150 to −350 µs (3–6 % of the step).

**Exactness.**
- (B) is bitwise against today's forward only if today's mirror vectors are already exact negations (the CPU
  check). Otherwise it needs the symmetrised vectors above and the force/stress gate.
- A GPU test on the gate's three structures compares every pair's w.
- (A) changes the sum order: it needs the force/stress gate.

**Falsifier.**
1. **CPU:** read how the neighbour list and the force call form edge vectors and shifts, and check the pair
   symmetry of the radial input for OMAT-0 (the SYMMETRIC_PAIRS check). If the mirror's vector is not the exact
   negation, (B) needs the symmetrised vectors, and both variants go through the gate.
2. **Lab, H200 (minutes).** The H200 base renders the message-only forward (`STORE_W = false`; the w′ reads the
   table, not a stored w). So `F-no-tp` already is P, and the lab's `F-gemm-only`, `F-no-store` and `NS-no-gemm`,
   which expect `STORE_W = true`, do not apply to it. New variants, with a 1.9 GB w buffer:
   - `store-w`: the base with STORE_W on (pass 1 of (A), for all edges);
   - `F-gemm-store`: F-no-tp with STORE_W on (the product pass of (B), for all edges);
   - `F-tp-stored-w`: the k-loop replaced by loads of a stored w, in edge order and issued a batch ahead (the TP
     pass of (B));
   - estimate (B) ≈ ½ F-gemm-store + F-tp-stored-w and (A) ≈ ½ store-w + ½ F-tp-stored-w;
   - build (B) if its estimate is ≤ 0.85, and (A) if only (A)'s is. The real passes read in pair order, a little
     less locally than the bound.
3. **The same pair on the A100-SXM4:** 2.0 TB/s, and its product is ≈ 1.7 ms of 3.25 ms.

**Risks.**
- **L2:** 2–3 GB of streaming per call would evict x (10 MB) and the w′'s table rows. The w stores and loads go
  evict-first (`st.global.cs`, `ld.global.cs`), or x gets an L2 persisting window.
- **Memory:** 0.96 GB of scratch, nothing on the H200 (141 GB).
- **The rebuild:** a sort of 373,760 keys, ≈ 20–40 µs per rebuild (every ≈ 8 steps), beside the force call.
- **Other GPUs:** the RTX 5060 Ti (448 GB/s) cannot pay for the round trip. A default is per GPU (decision 66).
- **Models:** only where the radial input is symmetric in the species pair. The planner checks it as for
  SYMMETRIC_PAIRS; an asymmetric radial input keeps today's path.
- **16 bits:** w stays float32. A 16-bit round trip would leave the bitwise class and the brief's gate rule.

**Effort.** Three to five days: the rebuild-time pairing (with unpaired edges), the owner-only radial evaluation,
the stored-w TP variant of the forward, the symmetrised edge vectors if needed, and tests (pairs, rebuilds,
restarts, swap-MC).

## 18. A compact TP: one flush in a segment loop, then a rolled edge loop

**Today** (SASS of the `891bf370` forward, the round-3 base):
- Part 1's region after the k-loop is 2,932 instructions (47 KB). The TP is unrolled over the 8 edges, with 27
  inlined copies of the 24-store receiver flush (`while (cur < rv[b]) { … }`).
- The whole kernel is 5,992 instructions (96 KB); the k-loop is ≈ 5 KB.
- So a warp walks most of 47 KB of code every batch. The flush copies, mostly skipped, spread its hot path over the
  region.

**Why now.**
- **Registers for idea 17:** 17 at G = 4 needs ≈ 12 more registers in the k-loop, where 163 of 168 are live. A
  rolled TP gathers x per edge, so x (24 registers) and rv (8) are no longer live across the k-loop.
- **F-skew:** its cost has two explanations (round 3, finding 3). If de-phased warps lose time on instruction
  fetch, the base pays part of that too, and a compact TP is the fix. ncu tells which, from the stall reason "no
  instruction".

**Mechanism.**
- **(a) A segment loop (bitwise, about a day):**
  - The batch's TP runs once per receiver segment: flush if the receiver changed, then accumulate the segment's
    edges with today's unrolled, predicated body.
  - A batch has one segment, or two at a receiver change (every ≈ 9 batches). The flush then exists once, and the
    hot path is ≈ 1,150 instructions in one piece.
- **(b) A rolled edge loop (bitwise, two days):**
  - `for (b = 0; b < nb; ++b)`, reading w[g] from r_s per edge (the same 48 loads as today), x one edge ahead from
    L1/L2 or from shared memory (idea 15(b)), and rv by shuffle.
  - The TP falls to a few hundred instructions. About 70 registers come free in the TP and 32 in the k-loop.

**Attacks.** The TP's code size (47 KB per batch) and registers (≈ 149 live), and the k-loop's registers.

**Ceiling.** The base's instruction-fetch stalls (not yet measured), plus what it unlocks in idea 17.

**Expected.** 0 to −150 µs on its own.

**Exactness.** Bitwise: the same operations on the same values in the same order. Predicated-off edges do nothing,
not `acc += 0`.

**Falsifier.**
1. **ncu on the H200 (minutes):** the warp stall reasons of the base and of F-skew-100ns: no instruction, MIO
   throttle, short and long scoreboard, math-pipe throttle. Build (a) if "no instruction" is ≥ 5 % of the base's
   stalls, or if it accounts for F-skew's 9 %.
2. **CPU:** NVRTC builds of (a) and (b) for registers, spills and code size.

**Risks.** (b) needs x per edge: from shared memory (+≈ 9 % shared traffic, so only after idea 17) or prefetched
one edge ahead (less latency hidden than behind the k-loop). (a) has neither problem.

**Effort.** (a) one day; (b) two days.

## 15. The x gathers: indices a batch ahead, then x through shared memory

**What the SASS of the `891bf370` forward shows** (168 registers, no spills):
- **The sender chain:**
  - Both parts load a batch's eight senders into one register (R25 in part 1, R17 in part 0), one after the
    other.
  - Each load issues only after the previous value has passed `snd < 0 || snd >= N`.
  - Each edge's x loads (three in part 1, one in part 0) follow its sender.
- **The order:** the a and sh `float4` loads issue after the chain, the eight receiver loads last.
- **So a batch's staging is serial:**
  - one L2 round trip for the first sender;
  - about six dependent L1 hits for the rest, which share its 32-byte sector;
  - then the x round trip;
  - the a loads, which come from HBM, queue behind all of it.
- **The TP's code:** unrolled over the 8 edges, with the receiver flush inlined between them (finding "Code size"
  above).

**(a) Indices a batch ahead (bitwise, ≈ 30 lines).**
- One warp-wide load per batch: lanes 0–7 read `senders[e0 + b]`, lanes 8–15 `receivers[e0 + b]`. It goes out
  during the previous batch's TP, into one register per lane.
- Each lane gets its x addresses with `__shfl_sync`, so all of a batch's x loads (24 in part 1, 8 in part 0) issue
  back to back.
- The a and sh loads go first, since they depend on nothing.
- The eight rv registers go too: the TP shuffles rv[b] when it needs it.

**(b) x through shared memory, and a rolled TP (bitwise).**
- **The copy:** each lane copies its x values into the warp's slice with `cp.async.ca` right after (a)'s index
  load, and waits (`cp.async.wait_group`) before the TP. No registers are held across the k-loop, whose length
  hides the latency.
- **The slice:** 8 edges × 3 rows × 32 floats = 3 KB per warp in part 1, 1 KB in part 0. The block grows from
  175,104 to 211,968 B of the 232,448 B allowed, so 12 warps stay.
- **The TP:** it reads x and w (from `r_s`) per edge, so its edge loop can stay rolled, with one flush out of
  line.
  - That frees ≈ 70 registers in the TP (w 48, x 24, rv 8, less what the rolled loop holds).
  - It shrinks the TP's hot code about eightfold.
- **After round 3:** the slice's copy and reads add 48 wavefronts per part-1 batch, ≈ 9 % more of the shared
  traffic that limits the forward. So (b) waits for idea 17; (a) adds no shared traffic.

**Attacks.** The x gathers and the chain in front of them; the TP's registers and code size.

**Ceiling.** The x cost at `891bf370`, not yet measured: at most ≈ 210 µs (F-no-xload's 295 µs at `6ebc795d`,
less DEGREE_PARTS' 87).

**Expected.** (a) −20 to −80 µs; (a) and (b) −40 to −150 µs.

**Exactness.** Bitwise: the same values reach the same instructions in the same order.

**Falsifier.**
1. **Lab:**
   - `F-no-xload` again at `891bf370`;
   - a new `F-x-local`: every edge gathers x from atoms 0–7 (`x[b * IN_DIM + …]`). It keeps the instructions and the
     registers, its loads hit L1, and nothing waits on the senders.

   F-x-local bounds what (a) and (b) can get. F-no-xload − F-x-local is the confound of the constant.
2. **CPU:** NVRTC sm_90 builds of (a) and (b), at ≤ 168 registers and no spills. In (a)'s SASS the indices must
   arrive in one load and the x loads issue together.
3. **Rule:** build (a) if F-x-local ≤ 0.97; build (b) after idea 17, if (a) gets less than half of F-x-local's
   saving.

**Effort.** (a) half a day; (b) two days (the slice, `cp.async` groups, the rolled TP with its out-of-line flush).

- Neither dout nor the table's locality (one slab 0.925) limits the w′. The locality of its sh and dsh rows does:
  the w′ over edges stored in mirror order is open (0.887–0.899 of the kernel, ≈ 2 % of the step).

### 16. TC_PRESPLIT on the H200, then a pass-major MMA order: closed

- F-wfrag-no-wsplit (the W splits removed, the loads unchanged; 32 B of spills): 0.853.
- TC_WFRAG_SPLIT (the slice stored as hi and lo planes; 12 warps, 224,256 B; bitwise): 1.070 on the H200, 1.144 on
  the RTX 5060 Ti. The switch stays off.
- (b), the pass-major order, would need more shared loads or more registers.
- Idea 17 cuts the splits by sharing each fragment instead of storing it twice.

### 16-edge batches (the ledger's tensor-core follow-up): dead

- A second n-tile per warp doubles the accumulators past the 168 registers that 12 warps allow. Idea 17 gets the W
  reuse without more accumulators.

## Closed in round 2

### 2. Degree-pure path parts (DEGREE_PARTS): adopted

- `fused_forward.DEGREE_PARTS` (`3ed00b73`, defaults in `891bf370`): part 0 holds the four scalar-input paths
  (x row 0), part 1 the six vector-input paths (x rows 1–3). Bitwise.
- MD b/a: H200 0.9818 / 0.9845; A100-SXM4 0.9920 / 0.9932; RTX 5060 Ti 0.9980 / 1.0032 (off there).
- The gate passes on the H200 and on an A100 PCIe. Benchmark_loup: H200 5.504 ms, A100 PCIe 10.89 ms.
- Lab: 1,777.9 → 1,691.3 µs on the H200, −87 µs. That is 29 % of F-no-xload's 295 µs; finding 2 of round 2 and
  idea 15 follow from it.

### 3. Piece-sorted radial tables: dead

- RTX 5060 Ti: the sorted kernel took 0.866 of the base, but the sort alone 155 µs against the 509 µs kernel.
- On the H200 only interaction 2's table (114.7 µs) was left. Idea 14 halves it another way.

### 4. S tile: dropped

- F-s-tile 0.966 on the H200 at `891bf370` (≈ 57 µs, before the cost of building S); A100 PCIe 0.951.
- With DEGREE_PARTS the coefficient multiplies are 2.9 % (F-tp-no-coef 0.971).

### 6. One launch per BlockLinear (SEGMENT_GROUPED): rejected

- Bound: 15–52 µs per group of four on the H200 and the A100 PCIe.
- Built (`f8facf08`) and bitwise, but the A100-SXM4 MD step gave 0.9987 / 1.0004. On the H200 SEGMENT_ROWS itself
  is off, so the switch does not act there.

### 7. Bulk copies for the a/sh staging: dropped

- F-no-ash4 0.955 on the H200 and 0.971 on the A100-SXM4: the staging is 3–4.5 %, not enough for the mbarriers.

### 8. PDL between our own kernels: dropped

- 1.8–4.7 µs per group of four GEMMs on the H200 (A100 PCIe −1.3 to 4.5 µs), below the 3 µs stop rule.

## Rejected in round 1

### 1. Term-pair interleaved w′ value table (TABLE_PAIRS)

- **Idea:** store TABLE_W's value table as `[groups, 3, Q, 2]`, so that a warp's `float2` load covers 256
  contiguous bytes instead of 768 B over six lines. Bitwise.
- **Measured:**
  - H200 1.0008 / 1.0003;
  - A100-SXM4 1.0058 / 1.0062;
  - RTX 5060 Ti 1.0024 / 0.9998.
- **Lesson:** the L1-wavefront model of the first version was wrong on every GPU. Either the q-major reads already
  coalesce within the lines they touch, or the w′ waits on latency rather than L1 data. The SASS census and
  finding 1 point to latency.
- **Where it could have returned:** as the layout for idea 12(a), had the table loads there needed to bypass L1.
  Idea 12 died in round 3 (FF-dout-one-row 1.001). With the q-major layout, the three term-pair loads of a weight
  reuse the same lines through L1.

### 9. Hoist the piece scale out of the w′ slope (HOIST_SCALE)

- **Measured:**
  - H200 1.0057 / 1.0017;
  - A100 1.0104 / 0.9988;
  - RTX 5060 Ti 0.9985 / 0.9991.
- **Lesson:** 40 FMULs per edge are 1.6 % of the w′'s instructions, and the w′ is not issue-bound.

## Considered and dropped

Other ways to cut the forward's shared loads (fourth round):

- **16 edges per warp in two path halves** (48 accumulators; each W fragment serves two n-tiles, as in idea 17 at
  G = 2). The first half's w waits in r_s while the second half runs, and a and sh cover 16 edges. So each warp's
  slices double (9,472 → 18,944 B), and 12 warps need 288,768 B of the 232,448 B. Idea 17 makes the same split
  across two warps, whose slices already exist.
- **The TP on the MMA's accumulator layout, without the r_s round trip** (96 of ≈ 550 wavefronts per batch). A
  lane holds w for four channels × two edges there, so the TP's message accumulators would grow fourfold (16–24 →
  64–96 per lane), and the receiver sum would need shuffles across the four lanes of a channel.
- **The edges as the MMA's M dimension** (a as operand A, W as operand B). The shared bytes per MMA depend on the
  warp tile's shape, not on which operand holds W. M = 16 also wants 16 edges per warp, the accumulator problem of
  the 16-edge batches.
- **64-channel blocks.** A block would stage each edge's a once for two warps of 32 channels. Each warp's tile stays
  192 × 8, so its W loads per MMA stay too; the staging is ≈ 20 of ≈ 550 wavefronts.
- **W in shared memory below 32 bits per element.** The 3xTF32 product uses about 22 of W's 24 significant bits
  (hi and lo). Fewer stored bits is a lower-precision product, i.e. a 16-bit path for the gate to judge, and a
  packed hi/lo pair still needs 32 bits plus unpacking in place of the split.

Earlier rounds:

- **Reassociating the product through the receiver sum** (Σ_k W_k Σ_e a_e[k] T(x_j, Y_e) instead of
  Σ_e (a_e W) T(x_j, Y_e)).
  - With interaction 2's full x it needs 64 times the accumulators per lane. It pays only for species-only x, as
    in interaction 1's reassociated first sum.
  - w has rank 64 here, and rad-low-rank found no lower rank (65 of 65).
- **Separate accumulators for the small products on the H200 (`tc-2acc`, `tc-3acc`).**
  - On the A100 they spilled 144 and 280 B at 168 registers (1.306, 1.504); the H200 has the same cap.
  - Idea 16(b) would have got the same chain lengths from the order alone; round 3 closed it.
- **Two receivers of a chunk walked together by sender, to share x loads.** It doubles the accumulators (24 → 48
  in part 1).
- **A copy of a block's senders' x in shared memory.** The senders of a block's 24 receivers are ≈ 200 atoms, i.e.
  ≈ 300 KB of x rows for part 1.
- **The w′ reading idea 14's stored w.** The w′ still needs w′ = dw/dr from the table, and the table costs at most
  7.5 % (one slab).
- **Changing the w′'s lane-to-channel ownership to pairs or quads.**
  - Measured: the channel-pair variant ran 1.126 in the H200 sweep (128 registers, so 16 instead of 20 warps per
    SM). Quads need more registers.
- **Two edges per w′ warp (one dout load for both).** It has the same register problem as the channel pairs, and
  round 3 found the dout reuse worth nothing (idea 12, FF-dout-one-row 1.001).
- **A dout locality walk measured by BACKWARD_WALK.** The first version cited BACKWARD_WALK's 1.000 on the H200
  against this. BACKWARD_WALK walks the `edge_outer_sum` backward (interaction 1), not the w′, so the w′'s dout
  locality was untested. It became idea 12, dead in round 3 (FF-dout-one-row 1.001).
- **Deferred CG in the forward (the TP after the receiver sum).**
  - Per edge and lane it accumulates the (m1, m2) outer products and contracts with CG once per receiver: ≈ 36 %
    fewer floating-point operations in the TP.
  - But the accumulators grow from 16–24 to 41–47 per lane, on a kernel whose TP loop holds 149 live registers and
    whose k-loop holds 163. It would cost warps (tc-warps10: 1.081).
- **A rotated-frame (SO(2), eSCN-style) TP.**
  - Rotating each edge onto z cuts the CG terms from 92 to 20 per channel.
  - Rotating the messages back costs ≈ 202 FMAs per channel at l ≤ 3.
- **FP64 tensor cores (DMMA) for the TP.** TC_TP's lesson applies: the preparation of the operands costs as much as
  the FP32 terms. The product also keeps the tensor pipe busy for most of the k-loop (≈ 875 of ≈ 1,000 µs at the
  measured HMMA rate, idea 17).
- **Fewer pieces of higher degree, to fit TABLE_W into the 50 MB L2 for a SIMT-slab forward.**
  - Measured: simt-slab-real 1.076, but simt-slab3 0.838 on the H200, so the table's 164 MB is what costs.
  - Degree 9 on four-times-wider pieces shrinks the table only 2.4× (≈ 68 MB), and it adds ≈ 70 % Horner work to
    the w′.
- **Removing the GEMMs' clone/cat copies.** SEGMENT_ROWS does this for the 1:1 irrep products (A100 default) and
  gave 1.0006 / 1.0000 on the H200.
- **A Hilbert or Morton atom order for gather locality.**
  - x is 10 MB and L2-resident.
  - The forward's work counter spreads receiver chunks over all SMs anyway.
  - The research engine's Morton order gave no gain.
  - Idea 15 hides the x latency without a new order.
- **Radial tables on a side stream beside interaction 1's atom chain.**
  - Measured: launch-two-streams (0.985–1.151×) and lin-concurrent-branches were rejected.
  - Reason: it is the same overlap mechanism.
  - Re-probe it only if the H200 timeline shows interaction 1's atom chain below 50 % SM occupancy.
- **DMMA float64 tensor cores for the product.**
  - Reason: 66.9 TFLOPS against ≈ 170 measured for the mma.sync 3xTF32 product.
- **Radial tables evaluated inside the fused forward.**
  - Reason: eight blocks restage each edge, 8 × 1.6 KB per edge ≈ 4.7 GB of L2 reads per call. This is the
    same failure as omat0-table-in-forward.
- **Running the w′ in (pair, piece) order.**
  - Reason: the table reads become local, but dout (20 KB per edge, 102 MB in all) becomes random.
  - Measured: one slab (0.925) caps the gain from table locality at 7.5 %.
- **An S tile in the w′'s FACTORED sums.**
  - Reason: u = Sᵀg saves only the u FMAs; v keeps the CG form, ≈ 228 → ≈ 218 instructions.
- **Split-K or stream-K for the small GEMMs.**
  - Ledger: lin-split-k was rejected.
  - Reason: it also changes the sums.
- **Smaller CUTLASS tiles.**
  - Measured: omat0-cutlass-tile was rejected on the A100 (1.012–1.017).
  - Reason: the warps per SM stay the same.
- **Skipping skin-shell edges.**
  - Reason: 373,760 edges is 74.75 per atom, the count inside 6 Å for SrTiO3's density (≈ 76), so the force
    call already sees only edges inside the cutoff.

## Interactions

- **Ideas 17 and 14 combine.** 14 halves the number of k-loops, and 17 makes each one cheaper.
  - In 14 (B) the product pass has no TP, so its warps can take any tile shape: a plain GEMM over the owner edges.
  - In 14 (A), 17 applies to pass 1.
- **Ideas 17 and 18:** G = 2 fits today's registers (+4); G = 4 needs 18's savings (+12).
- **Ideas 17 and 15:** 15(a) frees the 8 rv registers in the k-loop, which 17 at G = 2 may need. 15(b), x through
  shared memory, adds ≈ 9 % to the shared traffic, so it waits for 17.
- **Shared memory:** 17 and 18(a) add none; 15(b) adds 36,864 B (211,968 B in all).
- **The mirror permutation:** the w′'s open mirror-order layout and idea 14's pairing need the same permutation from
  the neighbour-list rebuild, so one sort serves both.
- **Ideas 13 and 5** are independent and small.

- **Both are built, idea 17 first** (the conductor's order). G = 2 is being built as `fused_forward.W_GROUP` on

### What round 5 says

1. **14(A) is worth ≈ −320 µs per forward before its own costs** (1,313 against 1,633 µs on the H200): pass 1
   ≈ 900 µs, pass 2 ≈ 413 µs.
   - Pass 2 is then a third of the forward, and it has no k-loop, so neither idea 17 nor pass 1's stores touch it.
   - Its own launch rule can (14(A), "Pass 2's launch rule").
2. **The stores do not cost HBM bandwidth.**
   - store-w writes 1.9 GB in 1.8 ms, about a fifth of the H200's HBM rate. It costs about as many µs on both GPUs
     (+166 on the H200, +183 on the A100), although the A100's HBM is 2.4 times slower.
   - The lab's store-w used plain stores, not the evict-first stores that revision 5 assumed. `render` takes
     STREAM_STORE_W (False) as its default, so 1.9 GB of w rows entered the 50 MB L2 with normal priority, beside
     x's 10 MB.
   - Two bitwise variants separate the L2 reading from the issue reading (14(A), "Pass 1's stores").
3. **Idea 17's bounds agree on both GPUs** (0.878 / 0.876 and 0.817 / 0.813), so the W fragment traffic weighs the
   same in both k-loops and G = 2 should pay on both.
   - The bounds leave out G's extra a loads and its barriers.
   - F-w-quarter's 162 registers do not predict G = 4's: the bound drops A fragments that G = 4 keeps.
4. **The A100 gains less from 14(A)** (0.836): the TP is a larger share of its forward (F-tp-stored-w 0.613), and
   pass 2 is all TP.

### Corrections to revision 5

- **store-w was not evict-first** (finding 2).
- **The 2,098 unpaired edges are very likely padding, not real edges.**
  - The force call runs on the edges within the cutoff, which stage C (`SkinNeighbourList.compact`) moves to the
    front every step, plus padding up to the edge bucket (`edge_bucket_fraction` 0.05 of the initial count).
  - 373,760 is 20 buckets of 18,688, and 373,760 − 2 × 185,831 = 2,098.
  - Stage C's test computes p[r] − p[s] + n·h in float64. For the mirror that is bitwise the negation, so an edge
    within the cutoff always has its mirror within it.
  - The padding lies past the last CSR row (the `row_ptr` search masks it), so neither pass of 14(A) computes it.
    The lab can check this in one line: the single edges' lengths against r_max, or the capture's edge count.
- **The pairing is per step as well as per rebuild.** Stage C compacts the edges again every step, so the passes'
  ranges and each edge's mirror index change every step (14(A), "Pairing and order").
- **Pass 2 cannot hold the next batch's w in registers** (it has 155 already). Revision 5's "a batch ahead" becomes
  a cp.async double buffer in the shared memory that the W slice leaves free.
- **Swap MC needs no new pairing.** Ownership depends on atom indices only, and SYMMETRIC_PAIRS holds for every
  species pair.
- **F-tp-stored-w read a zeroed w buffer** (`_stored_w_args`), so it was a timing bound only. Filled from store-w's
  w, the same render should reproduce the base's messages bit for bit, and the pass-2 screen can check them.

### What round 4 says

1. **Only (B) was bounded, and (A) looks better.**
   - (B) pays for a separate product and then reads w for both edges of every pair (F-tp-stored-w 0.557). Its
     product was CUTLASS's (750 µs). The fused product with stores (≈ ½ F-no-tp, ≈ 0.36) would still leave (B) at
     ≈ 0.91.
   - (A) keeps the owned half fused and reads w for the partner half only. The plan's estimate is
     ½ store-w + ½ F-tp-stored-w = ½ store-w + 0.279.
   - Storing w costs little where the HBM has room: on the A100-SXM4, F-no-store was 0.971 for 1.9 GB at
     2.0 TB/s (`omat0-table-w`). With store-w ≈ 1.03, (A) comes to ≈ 0.79 of the forward, ≈ −345 µs.
   - The 451 µs copy floor does not bind (A). Pass 1's stores overlap its k-loop, which is limited by shared loads,
     not HBM; pass 2's reads are already inside F-tp-stored-w.
   - So idea 14 returns as 14(A), decided by one lab variant, `store-w`.
2. **The forward's limit is its k-loop, not its shared traffic as a whole.**
   - The redistribution through r_s is 96 of a batch's ≈ 550 wavefronts, yet costs 0.2 % (F-tc-no-redistribute
     0.998).
   - Round 3's 0.853 and 1.070 moved the k-loop's W splits and loads. Idea 17 targets those; reshaping the TP's
     shared reads does not pay.
3. **x after 15(a):** 15(a) took a third of F-no-xload's 10 % (0.966 against 0.900). By the kernel review, the rest
   is the gathers' L2 traffic, not the senders → x dependency. Register prefetch (F-x-nodep 1.094) and cp.async
   staging (`PREFETCH = "x"`: A100-SXM4 1.152, RTX 5060 Ti 1.110) lost, so 15(b) is closed.
4. **Shared reads are not free on the H200:** the w′ ran 1.159 with sh and 1.252 with x read from shared memory
   instead of cached global memory.

### Corrections to revision 4

- **Mirror lengths are bitwise equal** for all 185,831 pairs in the force call. Revision 4 read the 2,098 unpaired
  edges as a sign of unequal lengths and planned symmetrised edge vectors for (B). Neither is needed; unpaired edges
  own themselves.
- **Idea 17's "shared wavefronts per batch" row** counts traffic that does not limit the forward (finding 2). The
  k-loop rows are what F-w-half and F-w-quarter test.
- **Idea 18(b)** loses its shared-memory x with 15(b): its x comes one edge ahead in registers.

The w′ research of the same day (section "Mirror-pair w′"):
- sharing the table work between an edge and its mirror buys nothing beyond the mirror order's cache reuse
  (`B-mirror-share` 1.197 on the H200, where the branch costs more than it saves);
- the w′ over edges stored physically in mirror order is open: 0.887–0.899 of the kernel on the H200, ≈ 2 % of the
  step after the copies;
- 2,098 atom pairs (0.56 % of the edges, at the cutoff) have only one direction, so a kernel cannot assume strict
  pairs.

### What round 3 says

1. **The forward's k-loop is limited by its shared-memory loads.**
   - Removing the W splits without touching the loads gains 15 % (F-wfrag-no-wsplit 0.853).
   - Replacing the same splits by a second LDS.128 per fragment loses 7 % (TC_WFRAG_SPLIT 1.070).
   - So the second load per fragment costs ≈ 22 % of the forward, more than the 96 split instructions per k-step
     it replaces.
   - A warp's 8-edge batch in part 1 moves ≈ 550 shared-memory wavefronts (census under "Where the time goes"), 70 %
     of them W fragments: 96 LDS.128 per batch.
2. **The warp tile is the lever.**
   - The product is a GEMM whose warp tile is 192 W rows × 8 edges in part 1 (128 × 8 in part 0). Each m16n8k8 MMA
     takes a 512-byte W fragment for only 8 edges.
   - Per MMA, that tile reads 2.5 times the shared bytes of a 48 × 32 tile, which has the same 48 accumulators.
   - 16-edge batches failed on registers because they doubled the accumulators. Idea 17 keeps the accumulators
     and changes the shape.
3. **F-skew did not test de-phasing as planned.**
   - Its anchor (`const int gid = lane >> 2, tq = lane & 3;`, line 117 of the run's `forward.cu`) is inside the
     batch loop. So the odd warps slept before every batch's k-loop, ≈ 118 times each on the H200, not once before
     their first chunk.
   - The cost barely grows with the requested time (1.094 at 100 ns, 1.114 at 1 µs). A sleep floor of about a
     microsecond per `__nanosleep` would do that; so would a cost of de-phasing itself. The variant cannot tell them
     apart.
   - A one-time offset (the same line before `while (true)`) has not been measured. The conductor dropped idea 10;
     the one-line re-test is optional under "Next measurements".
4. **dout locality is worth nothing in the w′ (FF-dout-one-row 1.001).** What the w′ gains from mirror order
   (0.887–0.899) is the locality of its sh and dsh rows.

### Corrections to revision 3

- **Idea 16 is closed.** Its step (a) is TC_WFRAG_SPLIT in all but name, and it lost. Step (b) needs more shared
  loads or more registers, the two things the k-loop lacks.
- **Idea 14 cannot assume strict pairs:** 0.56 % of the edges have no mirror; they own themselves. (Round 4 found
  the paired edges' lengths bitwise equal in the force call, so no symmetrised vectors are needed.)
- **The census applies to round 3's base:** the runs' `forward.cu` is byte for byte the render this plan's SASS
  census used (the H200 gate run's, `STORE_W = false`).

### What round 2 says

1. **Launch structure does not reach the step.**
   - SEGMENT_GROUPED's bound saved 15–52 µs per group of four GEMMs, but the A100 MD step stayed at 0.9987 /
     1.0004. PDL saves less than 5 µs per group.
   - The two convolution kernels are 60 % of the step: the forward ≈ 1.69 ms and the w′ ≈ 1.6 ms. The third round
     of ideas (14–16) therefore went to their algorithm and data layout, and idea 13 to the end of the ranking.
2. **The x cost is not its bytes.**
   - DEGREE_PARTS halved the x rows each edge gathers (eight row gathers per edge and channel block, now four).
     The kernel gained 87 µs (1,777.9 → 1,691.3), 29 % of F-no-xload's 295 µs.
   - **The SASS at `891bf370` shows why.**
     - Both parts load a batch's eight senders into one register, one after the other.
     - Each load issues only after the previous value has passed its range check, and each edge's x loads
       follow its sender.
     - The a and sh `float4` loads issue after this chain, and the receivers last.
     - The chain is as long with half the rows.
   - **F-no-xload overstates the x cost:** with x a constant, ptxas folds the x·e·coef products and frees the
     24–32 x registers. Idea 15's F-x-local was not run; round 4 adopted 15(a) and closed 15(b).

### What the failures say

1. **The w′ is latency-bound.**
   - **What did not help:**
     - fewer L1 wavefronts on its table reads (TABLE_PAIRS);
     - 40 fewer FMULs per edge (HOIST_SCALE), which is 1.6 % of its instructions;
     - table locality (one slab 0.925, so L2 and HBM misses cost at most 7.5 %);
     - no atomics (1.089).
   - **The SASS census:**
     - A channel pass is 574 instructions: FFMA 330, LDG 90, FMUL 71, CS2R 30, FADD 20, IADD3 17, MOV 10, REDG 4.
     - The 90 loads match 30 for the table (three `float2` per path), 40 for dout, 16 for sh and 4 for x.
     - With four passes and the 202-instruction end reduction, an edge costs ≈ 2,500 warp instructions. Over
       373,760 edges that is ≈ 0.9 ms of issue at one instruction per cycle per scheduler, against 1.61 ms
       measured.
     - At most 89 registers are live (96 allocated, 20 warps per SM). The register caps of 72 and 80 spill.
     - The kernel is 984 static instructions (16 KB): its channel passes are a loop, so its code is small.
   - **So:** what can still pay is the latency of its loads. The 40 dout loads per pass are L2 round trips, because
     the ≈ 19 four-edge blocks of a receiver land on about as many SMs (idea 12). Round 3 found that worth nothing
     (FF-dout-one-row 1.001).
2. **The H200 forward runs its two halves one after the other.**
   - **The bounds at `27d27f21` (base 2,323 µs) add up:**
     - the product: no-product 0.527, i.e. 1,099 µs;
     - the TP with its x gathers: no-TP 0.669, i.e. 769 µs;
     - together 1,868 µs, which leaves 455 µs for staging and the rest.
     - The A100's bounds add up the same way: 3.25 ms = 1.71 ms product + 1.26 ms TP + rest.
   - **The product half looked bound by the tensor pipe at mma.sync's rate** (round 3 found its shared-memory loads
     limit it):
     - one MMA pass instead of three gives 0.717, about as much as no MMA at all (0.705);
     - the product alone took 1.082 ms with mma.sync and 0.554 ms with wgmma (the microbenchmark).
   - **The SASS census of `02a81e13` (KP = 64):**
     - **k-loop:** per k-step 30 `HMMA.1688.F32.TF32`, 86 TF32-split instructions (LOP3 + FADD) and 12 LDS. That
       is 240 HMMA, ≈ 690 split instructions and 96 LDS per 8-edge batch and warp.
     - **TP:** ≈ 95 instructions per edge and lane, 88 of them FMUL or FFMA (part 0: FMUL 45, FFMA 43). The
       receiver-change stores sit behind a branch.
     - **Whole batch:** ≈ 2,900 instructions per warp.
     - **HMMA rate:** at the product bound the tensor pipe takes ≈ 11–12 cycles per HMMA per scheduler.
       Three warps' k-loops then issue on only ≈ 40 % of the cycles, and during the TP the tensor pipe is idle.
   - **Registers:** the peak (163 of 168 live) is in the k-loop, where the x values for the TP (32 registers,
     gathered before the k-loop) and the 40 accumulators are live. The TP loop peaks at 149.
   - **Why the halves do not overlap is open.** After the W fill the warps take their own chunks, with no block
     barrier. So either they stay in lockstep by themselves, or the HMMAs starve the other warps' FP32
     instructions. Idea 10's census was not run; round 3 closed the idea.
3. **A faster product alone did not help.**
   - **WGMMA** halved the product, but its two W planes in shared memory left room for only 8 warps instead of 12.
     The TP, which needs warps to hide its latency, lost more than the product gained.
   - **The pipelined version** kept the A fragments and accumulators live across the TP (244 registers) and lost
     more.
   - **The lever** turned out to be the product's shared-memory loads (round 3): idea 17 shares them, and idea
     14(A) halves the product.
4. **On the RTX 5060 Ti the TP is its per-lane arithmetic.**
   - It is half the forward (F-no-tp 0.517).
   - Its coefficient multiplies (0.977), x loads (0.950) and sh loads (1.017) are small.

## 14(A). Owner-pass forward: the k-loop once per mirror pair

**Status.** Build, after idea 17's G = 2 (round 5: estimate 0.804 of the forward on the H200, 0.836 on the
A100-SXM4; threshold 0.85). This section is the design for the build, with the three points the conductor left
open: pass 2's launch rule, the owners-only radial table, and the pairing's cost on the H200.

**Today.**
- Every edge runs the k-loop. The product with its staging is ≈ 1,142 µs of the 1,673 µs forward (F-no-tp 0.683,
  round 4).
- An edge and its mirror have bitwise-equal lengths (185,831 pairs, round 4), and OMAT-0's radial rows are symmetric
  in the species pair (SYMMETRIC_PAIRS). So both edges form the same a and the same w, bit for bit, and today both
  compute them.

**The change.**
- **Ownership:** each mirror pair has one owner edge. The edge i ← j owns when i + j is even and i < j, or odd and
  i > j (a self-image by the sign of its shift). Each receiver owns about half of its edges. An edge without a mirror
  owns itself; within the cutoff there are none (see "Pairing and order").
- **Pass 1** is today's fused forward (W_GROUP once idea 17 lands) over each receiver's owned edges. Its STORE_W
  writes each owned edge's w into its mirror's row of an [E, Q] buffer (Q = 1,280; 951 MB written).
- **Pass 2** is the product-free forward over each receiver's partner edges: the F-tp-stored-w render made real.
  It reads w from its own rows, starts each receiver from pass 1's message and stores the sum. Pass 1 writes every
  receiver's message, zero where it owns no edge, as today's flush does for a receiver without edges.

### Pairing and order: at each rebuild, and every step

The force call's edges are not the rebuild's. Every step, stage C (`SkinNeighbourList.compact`, in graph AC) moves
the candidates within the cutoff to the front in stable order, and the force call pads them to the edge bucket. So
the pairing has a part per rebuild and a part per step.

- **At each rebuild** (every ≈ 8 steps; the rebuild takes 7.04 ms beside the force calls):
  - The candidates' receiver sort (`sort_receivers`) takes the owner flag as a second key, so each receiver's owned
    candidates come first. Stage C's stable compaction keeps that order at every step, with no sort of its own.
  - `candidate_mirror`: one sort of the candidates' pair keys (lower atom, higher atom, the image shift with a
    canonical sign), as the lab's `mirror_order` does for the edges, then one scatter. A pair with several images
    within cutoff + skin (cells down to one cutoff high) needs the shift in the key; Benchmark_loup has one image.
  - `candidate_owned` (one byte per candidate): the parity rule; a candidate without a mirror is owned.
  - **Cost:** ≈ 430 k candidates within cutoff + skin (0.3 Å), one radix sort of 64-bit keys. The RTX 5060 Ti took
    290 µs to build and sort the keys of the 373,760 edges (round 3, "Mirror-pair w′"); the H200 has 3.7 times the
    SMs and 11 times the bandwidth, so ≈ 30–80 µs, ≈ 50–100 µs with the scatter and the flags. That is ≈ 1 % of
    the rebuild, beside the force calls; ≈ 6–12 µs per step if it were on the critical path.
- **Every step:**
  - Stage C carries the flag and the mirror's new position to the edges, as it carries the shifts:
    `owned.index_copy_(0, destination, candidate_owned)` and
    `mirror_edge.index_copy_(0, destination, destination[candidate_mirror])`. As torch ops ≈ 6–10 µs.
  - The force call's `row_ptr` search (`torch.searchsorted` on the receivers with the padding masked,
    `rewrites/mh1/modules.py`) runs on the key 2 × receiver + (not owned), with 2N + 1 boundaries: the even ones are
    `row_ptr`, the odd ones each receiver's split `mid` between owned and partner edges. ≈ +2–4 µs.
  - In all ≈ 8–14 µs per step, until a fused compaction kernel absorbs the copies.
- **The passes walk per-receiver ranges:** pass 1 [row_ptr[i], mid[i]), pass 2 [mid[i], row_ptr[i + 1]), with RC
  receivers per chunk as today. A batch can straddle two receivers' ranges: with LANE_INDICES each lane maps its
  index to an edge (one compare at RC = 2) and loads that edge's receiver, sender and mirror.
- **Every other kernel is unchanged.** The global order stays receiver-sorted with one CSR; only the order within a
  receiver changes (owned first). Interaction 1's sums see that too, at rounding level.
- **Where sender groups order the candidates** (`sender_groups`, MH-1's segmented first-interaction sum), owned-first
  would split the groups' runs, and the passes would need per-pass index arrays instead of ranges. OMAT-0 does not
  use them.

### Pass 2's launch rule

Pass 2 has no W slice, no a staging and no r_s. Its per-warp shared memory is the sh staging (BATCH × 16 floats,
512 B), so registers, not shared memory, set its occupancy, and nothing ties its warps into one block.

- **Today's rule, under which the lab ran F-tp-stored-w:** one block of 12 warps per SM (175,104 B, with the unused
  W slice), 155 registers, and all part-0 blocks before the part-1 blocks. A warp issues its batch's 48 w loads
  right before its TP and waits for them; only the other warps hide the wait.
- **Why it matters:** pass 2 is ≈ 413 µs at today's rule. Its TP issue is ≈ 265 µs (F-no-tp's 0.317 of the
  forward, for half the edges) and its w stream ≈ 240 µs (951 MB at 4 TB/s). Fully overlapped they would take
  ≈ 270–300 µs.
- **Pass 2's rule** (`launch_rule_partner`):
  - **Parts:** pass 1's DEGREE_PARTS groups (x rows 0, and 1–3). Finer parts would gather x again.
  - **Blocks:** 4 warps each (one per scheduler), `PARTNER_WARPS_PER_SM / 4` blocks per SM. The grid is persistent
    and one-dimensional, `PARTNER_WARPS_PER_SM / 4 × SMs` blocks; a block's slice is `block % slices`, with the part
    as its lowest bit, so both parts share each SM: part 0 is mostly w stream (4 paths, a short TP), part 1 mostly
    TP.
  - **Registers:** `__launch_bounds__(128, PARTNER_WARPS_PER_SM / 4)`. Twelve warps per SM allow 168 (155 needed);
    16 allow 128.
  - **W_PREFETCH:** each warp copies its next batch's w rows (paths × 8 edges × 128 B: 6 KB in part 1) with cp.async
    into a second buffer while its TP runs, and the TP reads w from shared memory: 48 LDS.32 per batch, as the r_s
    round trip that costs pass 1 0.2 %. Twelve warps × 12.8 KB = 154 KB per SM, which fits only because there is no
    W slice.
  - **Evict-first loads** (`__ldcs`, or cp.async with an evict-first policy) for the w rows, so that x and the
    messages stay in L2.
- **Screen** (lab, H200, minutes): F-tp-stored-w with its buffer filled from store-w's w, so each variant must also
  reproduce the base's messages bit for bit:
  - today's rule (0.506);
  - `p2-12`: 3 blocks of 4 warps per SM, parts interleaved;
  - `p2-12-pf`: and W_PREFETCH;
  - `p2-16-pf`: 16 warps per SM at 128 registers, with W_PREFETCH;
  - `p2-12-cs`: `p2-12` with `__ldcs`.

  Take the fastest; keep today's rule, without the W slice, if none gains 3 %.
- **Expected:** 0 to −90 µs (F-tp-stored-w 0.506 → 0.40–0.47; pass 2 runs half of it).
- **With idea 18(b)** the rolled TP's ≈ 90 registers would allow 20 warps per SM.

### The owners-only radial table

- **a has one consumer.** In the H200's TABLE_W path (`_WPrimeBlockConvolution.forward`), `radial_table_forward`
  returns (a, density). a feeds only `fused_folded_message_blocks` and is not saved; the backward reads the w′
  table, not a.
- **The density column is needed for every edge.** It is the same for both edges of a pair, bitwise: the same
  length and the same table row.
- **The change:** the table kernel takes an optional list of rows, the owned edges `e1` (a segmented iota from
  `row_ptr`, `mid` and their prefix `row_ptr1` of owned counts; one small kernel per step).
  - Each listed row gets a's columns, written compactly in pass 1's order: row k for the k-th owned edge. Pass 1
    then reads a at its owned rank, and its a staging stays contiguous.
  - Each listed row also writes its density into its own and its mirror's row (`mirror_edge`).
  - The padding rows keep today's evaluation. Every evaluated row is bitwise today's.
- **Gain:** about half of interaction 2's table, 114.7 µs at `27d27f21` (the current H200 time is not in the
  records): −45 to −55 µs per force call, ≈ 1 % of the step.
- **Plumbing:** the row list per step, and the row option in the Triton kernel, its fake and its tests. About a day.
- **Verdict:** worth it, as the last step of 14(A), once the two passes have passed the gate. It is exact and
  independent of them.

## 17. Squarer warp tiles: a warp group shares each W fragment across its batches

**Status.** G = 2 is being built as `fused_forward.W_GROUP` (branch `fastmace/idea17-wgroup`), bitwise, with an H200
MD A/B after it. Round 5 passed both falsifiers on both GPUs: F-w-half 0.878 on the H200 and 0.876 on the
A100-SXM4, F-w-quarter 0.817 and 0.813. G = 4 follows with idea 18's registers.

**Today.**
- Each warp owns its 8-edge batch from staging to message stores.
- In the k-loop it multiplies the whole W slice of its part by its batch's a. In part 1 that slice is 6 paths × 32
  channels = 192 rows, i.e. 12 m16 tiles, against one n8 tile of edges.
- Per k-step it loads 12 W fragments (LDS.128), splits them (96 LOP3 + FADD) and issues 36 HMMAs.
- All 12 warps of a block load the same W fragments, each for its own 8 edges.

**The change.**
- **Groups:** the warps form groups of G (2, 3 or 4). With 12 warps, a group of 4 holds one warp per scheduler.
- **Staging:** each warp still stages its own batch (a, sh, x), as today.
- **k-loop:** each warp takes 1/G of the part's W tiles, for all G batches of its group, i.e. G n-tiles.
  - Part 1 at G = 2: one m-tile of all 6 paths (6 tiles) × 2 n-tiles. At G = 4: 3 tiles × 4 n-tiles.
  - Either way 48 accumulators, as today; part 0 keeps its 32.
  - Each W fragment is loaded and split once for G batches, and feeds 3G HMMAs.
  - The a fragments of the n-tiles come from the G warps' own a_s slices (2 LDS.32 per n-tile and k-step).
- **Redistribution:** after the k-loop, each warp writes its accumulators into the owner warps' r_s, at the rows it
  computed. After a group barrier, each warp reads its own w from its own r_s and runs its own TP, as today.
- **What stays:** each warp's receivers, chunks, TP, flushes and message stores.
- **Why it is bitwise:** every w is the same MMAs of one W tile and one n-tile, k ascending, so w and the messages
  are bitwise as today.
- **Synchronisation:** two named barriers per batch (`bar.sync id, 32·G`).
  - The first follows the staging: the group's a_s are ready, and their r_s are free.
  - The second follows the r_s writes.
  - A warp that has run out of chunks keeps joining its group's k-loops with an empty batch until the whole group
    is done. A `bar.red.or` on "still has a batch" ends the loop, so the others still get their tiles.
- **Not a 16-edge batch:** those kept all paths in every warp and doubled the accumulators. Here a warp's
  accumulators stay at 48; only the tile changes shape, from 192 × 8 to 96 × 16 (G = 2) or 48 × 32 (G = 4).
- **Shared memory:** unchanged (175,104 B). Each warp's a_s, sh_s and r_s stay its own. The others read its a_s and
  write into its r_s.

**Per warp and 8-edge batch, part 1** (wavefronts as in the census under "Where the time goes"):

| | Today (G = 1) | G = 2 | G = 4 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| W fragments, LDS.128 | 96 | 48 | 24 |
| a fragments, LDS.32 | 16 | 32 | 64 |
| shared wavefronts in the k-loop (W + a) | 400 | 224 | 160 |
| shared wavefronts per batch | ≈ 550 | ≈ 370 | ≈ 310 |
| split instructions (W + a) | 800 | 448 | 320 |
| a-fragment registers (bh, bl) | 4 | 8 | 16 |
| HMMA | 288 | 288 | 288 |

The k-loop rows are the ones that count: the r_s round trip outside the k-loop costs 0.2 % (F-tc-no-redistribute
0.998, round 4), so 17's cross-warp r_s writes should cost little too.

**Attacks.** The k-loop's W fragment loads, 70 % of the forward's shared-memory traffic, and its W splits.

**Ceiling.** The k-loop at the MMA rate.
- Today the k-loop takes ≈ 1,000 µs: F-no-tp's ≈ 1,100 µs less the staging.
- Its 89.7 M HMMAs alone would take ≈ 875 µs at the marginal 10.2 cycles per HMMA and scheduler measured at

**Expected.**
- Round 3 measured −248 µs for removing all W splits with the loads unchanged (F-wfrag-no-wsplit).
- G = 2 removes 44 % of the splits and 44 % of the k-loop's shared wavefronts. G = 4 removes 60 % of each.
- Round 5: F-w-half 0.878 (−200 µs) and F-w-quarter 0.817 (−299 µs), without G's extra a loads and barriers.

**Exactness.** Bitwise.

**Falsifier** (round 5: both builds on).

## 18. A compact TP: one flush in a segment loop, then a rolled edge loop

**Today** (SASS of the `891bf370` forward, the round-3 base):
- Part 1's region after the k-loop is 2,932 instructions (47 KB). The TP is unrolled over the 8 edges, with 27
  inlined copies of the 24-store receiver flush (`while (cur < rv[b]) { … }`).
- The whole kernel is 5,992 instructions (96 KB); the k-loop is ≈ 5 KB.
- So a warp walks most of 47 KB of code every batch. The flush copies, mostly skipped, spread its hot path over the
  region.

**Why now.**
- **Registers for idea 17:** 17 at G = 4 needs ≈ 12 more registers in the k-loop, where 163 of 168 are live. A
  rolled TP gathers x per edge, so x (24 registers) and rv (8) are no longer live across the k-loop.
- **Registers for 14(A)'s pass 2:** pass 2 is all TP. At 155 registers it runs 12 warps per SM; (b)'s ≈ 90 would
  allow 20 (14(A), "Pass 2's launch rule").
- **F-skew:** its cost has two explanations (round 3, finding 3). If de-phased warps lose time on instruction
  fetch, the base pays part of that too, and a compact TP is the fix. ncu tells which, from the stall reason "no
  instruction".

**Mechanism.**
- **(a) A segment loop (bitwise, about a day):**
  - The batch's TP runs once per receiver segment: flush if the receiver changed, then accumulate the segment's
    edges with today's unrolled, predicated body.
  - A batch has one segment, or two at a receiver change (every ≈ 9 batches). The flush then exists once, and the
    hot path is ≈ 1,150 instructions in one piece.
- **(b) A rolled edge loop (bitwise, two days):**
  - `for (b = 0; b < nb; ++b)`, reading w[g] from r_s per edge (the same 48 loads as today), x one edge ahead in
    registers from L1/L2 (x through shared memory, 15(b), is closed), and rv by shuffle.
  - The TP falls to a few hundred instructions. About 70 registers come free in the TP and 32 in the k-loop.

**Attacks.** The TP's code size (47 KB per batch) and registers (≈ 149 live), and the k-loop's registers.

**Ceiling.** The base's instruction-fetch stalls (not yet measured), plus what it unlocks in idea 17.

**Expected.** 0 to −150 µs on its own.

**Exactness.** Bitwise: the same operations on the same values in the same order. Predicated-off edges do nothing,
not `acc += 0`.

**Falsifier.**
1. **ncu on the H200 (minutes):** the warp stall reasons of the base and of F-skew-100ns: no instruction, MIO
   throttle, short and long scoreboard, math-pipe throttle. Build (a) if "no instruction" is ≥ 5 % of the base's
   stalls, or if it accounts for F-skew's 9 %.
2. **CPU:** NVRTC builds of (a) and (b) for registers, spills and code size.

**Risks.** (b) needs x per edge, prefetched one edge ahead, which hides less latency than today's gathers behind the
k-loop; shared memory is out (15(b) closed, round 4). (a) has no such problem.

**Effort.** (a) one day; (b) two days.

## Considered and dropped

Other ways to cut the forward's shared loads (fourth round):

- **16 edges per warp in two path halves** (48 accumulators; each W fragment serves two n-tiles, as in idea 17 at
  G = 2). The first half's w waits in r_s while the second half runs, and a and sh cover 16 edges. So each warp's
  slices double (9,472 → 18,944 B), and 12 warps need 288,768 B of the 232,448 B. Idea 17 makes the same split
  across two warps, whose slices already exist.
- **The TP on the MMA's accumulator layout, without the r_s round trip** (96 of ≈ 550 wavefronts per batch). A
  lane holds w for four channels × two edges there, so the TP's message accumulators would grow fourfold (16–24 →
  64–96 per lane), and the receiver sum would need shuffles across the four lanes of a channel. The round trip
  itself costs 0.2 % (F-tc-no-redistribute 0.998, round 4).
- **The edges as the MMA's M dimension** (a as operand A, W as operand B). The shared bytes per MMA depend on the
  warp tile's shape, not on which operand holds W. M = 16 also wants 16 edges per warp, the accumulator problem of
  the 16-edge batches.
- **64-channel blocks.** A block would stage each edge's a once for two warps of 32 channels. Each warp's tile stays
  192 × 8, so its W loads per MMA stay too; the staging is ≈ 20 of ≈ 550 wavefronts.
- **W in shared memory below 32 bits per element.** The 3xTF32 product uses about 22 of W's 24 significant bits
  (hi and lo). Fewer stored bits is a lower-precision product, i.e. a 16-bit path for the gate to judge, and a
  packed hi/lo pair still needs 32 bits plus unpacking in place of the split.

Earlier rounds:

- **Reassociating the product through the receiver sum** (Σ_k W_k Σ_e a_e[k] T(x_j, Y_e) instead of
  Σ_e (a_e W) T(x_j, Y_e)).
  - With interaction 2's full x it needs 64 times the accumulators per lane. It pays only for species-only x, as
    in interaction 1's reassociated first sum.
  - w has rank 64 here, and rad-low-rank found no lower rank (65 of 65).
- **Separate accumulators for the small products on the H200 (`tc-2acc`, `tc-3acc`).**
  - On the A100 they spilled 144 and 280 B at 168 registers (1.306, 1.504); the H200 has the same cap.
  - Idea 16(b) would have got the same chain lengths from the order alone; round 3 closed it.
- **Two receivers of a chunk walked together by sender, to share x loads.** It doubles the accumulators (24 → 48
  in part 1).
- **A copy of a block's senders' x in shared memory.** The senders of a block's 24 receivers are ≈ 200 atoms, i.e.
  ≈ 300 KB of x rows for part 1.
- **The w′ reading idea 14(A)'s stored w.** Pass 1 stores w for the partner edges only, the w′ still needs
  w′ = dw/dr from the table, and the table costs at most 7.5 % (one slab).
- **Changing the w′'s lane-to-channel ownership to pairs or quads.**
  - Measured: the channel-pair variant ran 1.126 in the H200 sweep (128 registers, so 16 instead of 20 warps per
    SM). Quads need more registers.
- **Two edges per w′ warp (one dout load for both).** It has the same register problem as the channel pairs, and
  round 3 found the dout reuse worth nothing (idea 12, FF-dout-one-row 1.001).
- **A dout locality walk measured by BACKWARD_WALK.** The first version cited BACKWARD_WALK's 1.000 on the H200
  against this. BACKWARD_WALK walks the `edge_outer_sum` backward (interaction 1), not the w′, so the w′'s dout
  locality was untested. It became idea 12, dead in round 3 (FF-dout-one-row 1.001).
- **Deferred CG in the forward (the TP after the receiver sum).**
  - Per edge and lane it accumulates the (m1, m2) outer products and contracts with CG once per receiver: ≈ 36 %
    fewer floating-point operations in the TP.
  - But the accumulators grow from 16–24 to 41–47 per lane, on a kernel whose TP loop holds 149 live registers and
    whose k-loop holds 163. It would cost warps (tc-warps10: 1.081).
- **A rotated-frame (SO(2), eSCN-style) TP.**
  - Rotating each edge onto z cuts the CG terms from 92 to 20 per channel.
  - Rotating the messages back costs ≈ 202 FMAs per channel at l ≤ 3.
- **FP64 tensor cores (DMMA) for the TP.** TC_TP's lesson applies: the preparation of the operands costs as much as
  the FP32 terms. The product also keeps the tensor pipe busy for most of the k-loop (≈ 875 of ≈ 1,000 µs at the
  measured HMMA rate, idea 17).
- **Fewer pieces of higher degree, to fit TABLE_W into the 50 MB L2 for a SIMT-slab forward.**
  - Measured: simt-slab-real 1.076, but simt-slab3 0.838 on the H200, so the table's 164 MB is what costs.
  - Degree 9 on four-times-wider pieces shrinks the table only 2.4× (≈ 68 MB), and it adds ≈ 70 % Horner work to
    the w′.
- **Removing the GEMMs' clone/cat copies.** SEGMENT_ROWS does this for the 1:1 irrep products (A100 default) and
  gave 1.0006 / 1.0000 on the H200.
- **A Hilbert or Morton atom order for gather locality.**
  - x is 10 MB and L2-resident.
  - The forward's work counter spreads receiver chunks over all SMs anyway.
  - The research engine's Morton order gave no gain.
  - LANE_INDICES (15(a)) took the index chain out of the x gathers without a new order.
- **Radial tables on a side stream beside interaction 1's atom chain.**
  - Measured: launch-two-streams (0.985–1.151×) and lin-concurrent-branches were rejected.
  - Reason: it is the same overlap mechanism.
  - Re-probe it only if the H200 timeline shows interaction 1's atom chain below 50 % SM occupancy.
- **DMMA float64 tensor cores for the product.**
  - Reason: 66.9 TFLOPS against ≈ 170 measured for the mma.sync 3xTF32 product.
- **Radial tables evaluated inside the fused forward.**
  - Reason: eight blocks restage each edge, 8 × 1.6 KB per edge ≈ 4.7 GB of L2 reads per call. This is the
    same failure as omat0-table-in-forward.
- **Running the w′ in (pair, piece) order.**
  - Reason: the table reads become local, but dout (20 KB per edge, 102 MB in all) becomes random.
  - Measured: one slab (0.925) caps the gain from table locality at 7.5 %.
- **An S tile in the w′'s FACTORED sums.**
  - Reason: u = Sᵀg saves only the u FMAs; v keeps the CG form, ≈ 228 → ≈ 218 instructions.
- **Split-K or stream-K for the small GEMMs.**
  - Ledger: lin-split-k was rejected.
  - Reason: it also changes the sums.
- **Smaller CUTLASS tiles.**
  - Measured: omat0-cutlass-tile was rejected on the A100 (1.012–1.017).
  - Reason: the warps per SM stay the same.
- **Skipping skin-shell edges.**
  - Reason: 373,760 edges is 74.75 per atom, the count inside 6 Å for SrTiO3's density (≈ 76), so the force
    call already sees only edges inside the cutoff.

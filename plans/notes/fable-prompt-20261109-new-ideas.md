# Brief: new ideas to make MACE-OMAT-0 medium molecular dynamics faster

You are advising a GPU performance team. We have optimised a MACE inference engine for about a month. Ordinary kernel
tuning (tiling, occupancy, register caps, fusion, sorting) is exhausted. The two most recent wins did not come from
tuning: they came from **rewriting the arithmetic so the hardware does fewer operations**. We want more ideas of that
kind, plus any genuinely different route you can argue for with numbers.

Everything you need is in this brief. If you also have the FastMACE repository, the files named in section 6 hold
the details. Think hard, check your arithmetic, and prefer a few deep, concrete ideas over a long list.

## 1. What we run

- **Engine.** Our own single-GPU inference engine for pretrained MACE potentials:
  - PyTorch orchestration, with the whole MD step captured as CUDA graphs;
  - custom NVRTC CUDA kernels for the edge work;
  - CUTLASS 3×TF32 GEMMs for the node linear layers;
  - generated CUDA kernels for the symmetric contractions.

  Forces and stress come from hand-written backward kernels that are exact autograd equivalents. The engine runs one
  structure at a time.
- **Model.** MACE-OMAT-0 "medium", float32, 89 elements:
  - two interactions, 128 channels, cutoff 6.0 Å;
  - edge spherical harmonics up to l = 3 (16 components), hidden features 128x0e + 128x1o;
  - symmetric contractions of correlation order 3;
  - "density" interaction blocks, where the message is divided by (1 + a learned per-atom density sum).

  Interaction 2's radial part is 1,280 per-edge weights (10 Clebsch–Gordan paths × 128 channels). They depend on
  edge length and on the (sender, receiver) species pair, and are read from a certified cubic spline table packed to
  8 bytes per weight. Interaction 1's radial part is a tabled outer sum per (receiver, sender species).
- **Benchmark.** An SrTiO3 10×10×10 supercell with 5,000 atoms:
  - Langevin MD at 300 K with a 1 fs step;
  - about 370,000 directed edges (about 74 neighbours per atom);
  - a Verlet skin list with a 0.5 Å skin, rebuilt every 8–13 steps.
- **Hardware.** An A100-SXM4-80GB (sm_80, 108 SMs) on an HPC cluster, with up to about 7 at once, and an H200 (sm_90,
  132 SMs) in the cloud. An RTX 5060 Ti (sm_120) is the local test card. It is memory-bound, so it does not predict
  FMA-versus-load trades on the A100.
- **Speed.** Milliseconds per MD step for 5,000 atoms, before the two wins below:

  | Model | H200 | A100 |
  | --- | ---: | ---: |
  | OMAT-0 medium | 3.37 | 6.51 |
  | OMAT-0 small | 2.12 | 4.17 |

## 2. Where the time goes (A100, one force call, about 6.2 ms of kernel time)

| Part | µs | Limit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Interaction-2 backward through the radial weights ("w′": per edge, the gradients for sender features, harmonics and length) | 2,177 | FP32 FMA pipe at 83 % |
| Interaction-2 forward (per receiver: Σ over edges of weights × sender features × harmonics, 10 CG paths) | 1,255 | FP32 FMA pipe at 84 % |
| Interaction-1 tabled outer sums, forward and backward | 832 | L1 bandwidth, load latency |
| Node linear layers (3×TF32 tensor-core GEMMs) | ~980 | 3×TF32 tensor-core rate |
| Symmetric contractions (generated, forward and backward) | 188 | FMA pipe at 93 % |
| Everything else (geometry, harmonics, density, glue) | ~800 | small and spread out |

On the H200 the two big edge kernels are bound by instruction issue (77–82 %), not by the FMA pipe.

## 3. The two wins that set the direction (measured on the A100 on 9 November)

1. **Factored symmetric contraction.**
   - **What it does.** Each output irrep of the correlation-3 contraction is a weighted sum of 29 basis polynomials
     in the node features. Their span is 13 invariants plus 16 vector covariants. We pick a cheaper basis built from
     memoised CG pair products, chosen greedily with a rank check. The change of basis is folded into the weights
     once at plan time (w′ = w K). The backward kernel is generated by reverse-mode differentiation of the same
     straight-line program.
   - **Cost.** 413 operations plus 61 output FMAs. The previous generated kernel did about 1,040 FP32 operations per
     channel in the forward and 1,500 in the backward.
   - **Result.** Forward 93 → 55 µs (0.59×). Forward plus backward 226 → 147 µs (0.65×), with the backward capped at
     128 registers.
2. **Selective shared geometry in the interaction-2 forward.**
   - **What it does.** Per CG path, it picks the cheapest of three forms:
     - the current code;
     - factoring w·x out of single-input paths;
     - hoisting g = Σ_j c·Y_j: computed once per edge tile into shared memory, then reused by all 128 channels.
   - **Cost.** FMULs fall from 69 to 48 per edge and channel; shared-memory loads rise from 9 to 28.
   - **Result.** 1,255 → 1,088 µs (0.867×), at 80 registers.

**Lesson.** On the A100 the edge kernels are FMA-bound. Moving per-channel work into per-edge or per-tile work that
all 128 channels share pays off, even at the cost of extra shared-memory traffic. Exact algebraic rewrites of the
CG structure are where the gains are.

**Next on our list (do not re-propose; improve it if you can).** A receiver-adjoint w′ backward:
- form R_ab = Σ_o C g_o once per edge at the output-gradient stage (47 values per channel), so the per-channel
  backward contracts against R;
- the constraints are registers, shared memory (6 CTAs per SM on the A100) and spills.

## 4. Closed: measured or argued out (do not propose these)

- **Precision.**
  - FP16, BF16 or FP8 anywhere on the force path (fails the accuracy gate by 3× to over 1,000×).
  - 1×TF32 and 3×FP16.
- **Tensor cores and other units.**
  - Tensor cores for the per-edge tensor product in any layout (a 3×TF32 build was 28 % slower).
  - FP64 units as a second pipe.
  - Strassen-type GEMM schemes for the node layers.
- **Radial tables.**
  - B-splines, quadratic tables, and a shared low-rank h(r) spline.
  - Polynomial moments per (receiver, species, table piece) for interaction 1: at 300 K the groups hold 1.2–1.3
    edges against a break-even of 4.4.
- **Edges, ownership and fusion.**
  - Mirror edges (i→j and j→i sharing work), 1.3–3.8× slower.
  - Receiver- or sender-owned backward, megakernels, persistent glue kernels, TMA and bulk copies.
  - Overlapping the GEMMs with the edge kernels.
  - Register caps, warp counts, prefetch depth.
  - Edge sorting or reordering.
  - Species-blocked edge order (1.25× slower).
- **Skipping and other operators.**
  - Certified skipping of edges, paths or channels (an oracle skips only 0.4 %).
  - FFT, mesh or FMM operators; radial tail pruning.
  - Force extrapolation without a correction.
- **Graph-level.**
  - Hoisting species-only kernels out of the force graph: about 1.1 % on the A100 and 1.3 % on the H200. It is
    sized and queued, so do not count it.
  - A whole-expression compiler for the force graph.
  - A Wigner-6j edge-to-node rewrite (cancellation).

## 5. What we want

Ideas in this order of preference:

1. **Exact operation-count reductions in the remaining big parts.**
   - The w′ backward (2,177 µs), beyond the receiver-adjoint plan above.
   - The interaction-1 outer sums (832 µs).
   - The interaction-2 forward after geom (1,088 µs).

   Use the CG and SO(3) structure, the linearity in the weights, the shared per-edge factors, the 9 species pairs
   and the spline structure. Count the FMAs, loads and registers per edge and channel before and after, and compare
   with the numbers above.
2. **Applying the factored-basis trick elsewhere.** Where else is a fixed polynomial in features evaluated through a
   redundant basis? Candidates: the product of harmonics with the CG paths, the density normalisation, the node
   linear layers composed with the contractions, and the backward of all of these.
3. **Moving work from edges (370,000) to nodes (5,000) or species pairs (9)**, exactly, and with the backward
   included.
4. **Time coherence across MD steps** with a controlled error and a stated drift check, but only if the arithmetic
   shows more than 10 % per step.
5. **Anything else genuinely different** that does not appear in section 4, with numbers.

## 6. Constraints and repository pointers

**Constraints.**

- **Accuracy.** Exact rewrites must agree to float32 rounding. Anything else must pass the force and stress gate:
  within 3× the float32 reference's own error against float64.
- **Generality.** Any structure, any species set, both GPUs, and OMAT-0 small (64 channels) too.
- **Adoption.** We adopt a change only if it gains at least 1 % of the MD step in an A/B run in both build orders.

**Repository pointers**, if you have the repository (branch `fastmace/bspline-lab`):

- `fastmace/kernels/cuda/sc_factored.py`: the factored contraction, its SSA program and reverse-mode backward;
- `tools/spline_read_lab.py`: the geom transform, in `geom()`;
- `results/other-ways-20261109/`: the latest measurements;
- `docs/optimisation-ledger.md`: everything tried so far.

## 7. What to return

For each idea, ranked by expected gain per MD step on the A100:

- **Mechanism.** Precise enough to prototype: what is computed, where (per edge, tile, node, species pair or plan
  time), and what is reused.
- **Arithmetic.** Operations, loads and registers per edge and channel, before and after. The expected µs saving
  against section 2, with the overhead included.
- **Exactness.** Exact, rounding-only, or a controlled approximation (with its control).
- **Backward.** How the force and stress path changes. A forward-only idea is half an idea.
- **Cheapest falsifying test.** What we would build first to kill it in a day.
- **Why it is new.** Why it is not an item in section 4.

Return 3–8 ideas. Close with a short verdict: how much faster than 6.51 ms on the A100 can this model plausibly run
at the same accuracy, and which route gets there?

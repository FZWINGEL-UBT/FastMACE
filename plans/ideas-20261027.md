## Ranked shortlist: float64

The float64 package (NbCl5) spends most of its time in a few places, per force call:
- atom-level GEMMs, emulated: 124 ms, 49 %;
- edge elementwise: 41 ms, 16 %. That includes the streaming backward, geometry and the tables;
- the symmetric contraction: 28 ms, 11 %;
- the atom elementwise kernels: 24 ms, 10 %;
- the folded-layer forward GEMM: 22 ms, 9 %. It runs at the native FP64 rate (0.42 TFLOPS), because the
  emulation does not pay at K = 65.

NaNbO3 has more edges per atom. There the folded forward is 35 ms (13.5 %) and the edge elementwise 63 ms (24.5 %).
Since f64-T03, the chunked input-gradient products take about 15 ms on NbCl5.

### D2 counts (why the w-tables pay in float64)

**Arithmetic per edge** (both cells):
- **Forward:** `a` from the table (65 outputs × 17 terms ≈ 1.1k FMA), then `a Wᵀ` (65 × 1280 = **83k FMA**). A
  w-table needs **21.8k FMA** (1280 × 17), 3.8× fewer.
- **Backward:** `da = dw W` (83k), then the table backward. A w-table needs `w'` (1280 × 17) plus the dot,
  about 23k.

**Time estimate, NbCl5:**
- 51–56k edges × 21.8k ≈ 1.2 G FMA. At 70 % of FP64 peak (0.175 T FMA/s) that is about 9 ms, against 22 ms for
  the forward GEMM.
- The backward drops from about 15 to about 9 ms.
- Saving ≈ 19 ms of ~245 ms (**8 %**). NaNbO3: about 30 ms of ~214 ms (**14 %**).

**The catch: coefficient traffic.**
- A float64 piece holds 1280 × 17 × 8 B = 174 KB. Interaction 1 has 366 pieces on NbCl5, about 64 MB.
- So edges must be grouped by (pair, piece): a per-step counting sort of a 32-bit key.
- `w` rows are then written to their receiver-ordered slots (10 KB contiguous rows).
- In the chunked backward, each 4-L2 chunk touches at most all pieces: about 64 MB of coefficient reads per
  chunk, 4–5 chunks per call. That is acceptable against FP64-bound time, but the kernel must order each chunk's
  edges by piece.

**Float32:**
- Coefficients are 56 KB per piece; interaction 1 has 53–65 pieces, about 3–4 MB, which fits L2.
- The float32 folded GEMM is not arithmetic-bound (8 TFLOPS, 28 % of peak), and OPT8 already removes it in the
  forward. So D2 is a float64 idea first (F4 below).

**Reading.** The input gradient runs at 14 GFLOP in 3.2 ms on NaNbO3, about **4.4 TFLOPS, 15 % of FP32 peak**:
- a quarter-L2 float32 chunk is about 1,600 edges, so it is many small skinny products;
- the forward GEMM of the same size reaches 7.2 TFLOPS.

The eager sum (18.1 ms on NaNbO3) exceeds the GPU span (15.9 ms), so part of the input gradient overlaps the
streaming backward. How much of it is on the critical path is the first thing to measure (F0).

Together, the two folded products are **17 % (NbCl5) and 28 % (NaNbO3)** of the eager kernel time. That raises
the stake of F1 and adds F0.

The large blocks are near hardware limits:
- **Atom GEMMs:** 37–50 %, at 13–15 of 16.2 TFLOPS on large cells. Every tensor-core and split-precision route
  is rejected on sm_120.
- **Atom elementwise:** at DRAM bandwidth.
- **Streaming convolution:** bandwidth-bound on `w`/`dw`.
- **Symmetric contraction:** at 72–96 % of memory speed of light.
- **Launch gaps:** 0.5–5.5 %.

So the remaining levers are arithmetic reductions in the second interaction's radial-to-convolution chain (the
only FFMA-bound part, per OPT8) and small items.

**D5, dropped on paper.**
- **Size:** MH-1's symmetric contraction acts on 512 channels × 16 components per atom (l ≤ 3).
- **Formulation:** as one dense product, it needs the degree-≤3 monomials of 16 variables, 969 values per
  (atom, channel). On NbCl5 that is 1944 × 512 × 969 ≈ 0.96 G float64 values, **7.7 GB** per force call.
- **Traffic:** writing and reading that once takes about 34 ms at 448 GB/s. The whole float64 symmetric
  contraction (forward and backward) takes 28 ms today.
- **Alternative:** a fused kernel that builds the monomials on chip would need its own INT8-Ozaki GEMM kernel.
  That is far beyond a falsifier, for at most the contraction's 11 %.

**F2, dropped on paper.**
- **Today:** the full OPT8 fused backward is 1.12–1.13× the chain's chunked backward (opt8-T01). Its three w-tile
  products are 545 M of its 887 M instructions (FFMA).
- **Ideal case:** tensor-core products at twice the FFMA rate cut the fused backward to about 0.69 of its time,
  for example 2.29 → 1.58 ms on NbCl5.
- **Step gain:** against today's default (fused forward 1.05 ms plus chunked backward 2.05 ms), that is
  3.10 → 2.63 ms, about **0.47 ms, 2.4 % of the NbCl5 step**, and about 3 % on NaNbO3.
- **Against it:** OPT5-T06/T08 found such mixed SIMT and tensor-core kernels slower than cuBLAS on sm_120, because
  of issue-slot contention, and normalised FP16×3 needs an accuracy re-check.
- **Verdict:** a ceiling below 3 % before any of those penalties does not justify a kernel.

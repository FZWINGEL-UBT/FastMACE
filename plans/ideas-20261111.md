# Four inference ideas not in the ledger, and a forward-ghoist re-screen (11 November 2026)

Source: an owner request (10 November 2026) for new ways to make the force call faster, with training and MD
time-step changes excluded. Checked against main `d7dd7e23`: the ledger's "Closed directions", "Open or untried" and
OMAT-0 tables, `results/step-limiters-20261109/` (README and both `limiters.csv`), the two breakthrough reports
under `plans/notes/` and the assessment in `results/omat0-breakthroughs-assessment-20261108/`.

**Nothing below is built or measured.** Every size is an estimate from numbers already on file. The ledger appendix
and the large idea files (`h200-ideas-20261105.md`, `codex-ideas-20261106.md`, `ideas-20261105-round2.md`) were
searched by keyword, not read in full, so an idea may exist there under other words. The ledger has no row for any
of this yet.

## Where the time is

Kernel time per force call, OMAT-0 medium, SrTiO3 10×10×10, from `results/step-limiters-20261109/h200/limiters.csv`
and `a100-packed8/limiters.csv`:

| Per force call | H200 | A100 80GB PCIe (PACKED8) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Edge kernels (w′, spline forward, both tabled outer sums) | 2,049 µs (63 %) | 4,373 µs (67 %) |
| 3×TF32 GEMMs | 609 µs | 1,069 µs |
| cuEq symmetric contraction (before `SC_GENERATED`) | 192 µs | 402 µs |
| Other kernels (elementwise, reductions, copies, geometry) | 408 µs | 668 µs |
| Total | 3,258 µs | 6,512 µs |

The H200's edge kernels are issue-bound (w′ 77 %, forward 82 %); the A100's are FMA-pipe-bound. The GEMMs run at
the tensor pipe's mma.sync rate on both.

## 1. Far-shell edges in half precision, two channels per instruction

**The idea.** `SORT_BY_LENGTH` already sorts each receiver's edges by length within each sender group, so the far
edges are the tail of each group. Run the edges beyond a radius r_s through packed half arithmetic (`__half2`,
`__hfma2` from `cuda_fp16.h`), so one instruction carries two channels:
- The far shell's table rows are stored as halves, with no PACKED8 decode.
- `x` and `dout` are read from half copies, scaled by exact powers of two to stay in the half range.
- The far-shell partial sums are merged into the float32 accumulators once per receiver.

It fits the NVRTC kernels first (`spline_forward`, `wprime_backward`: 1,730 of the H200's 2,049 µs). The tabled
outer sums are Triton kernels and would need an NVRTC version.

**Why the gate might allow it.** The cutoff envelope damps the far edges. `skip_oracle.py`
(`results/omat0-breakthroughs-assessment-20261108/outside-box-report/`, CPU float64, SrTiO3 4×4×4, σ = 0.05 Å)
masks every interaction-2 edge beyond r_s:

| r_s (Å) | Edges beyond | Max force change when masked (eV/Å) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5.50 | 26.4 % | 4.4e-2 |
| 5.80 | 12.8 % | 6.4e-3 |
| 5.90 | 4.3 % | 1.9e-3 |

The production rounding error on that structure is 3.57e-5 eV/Å and the gate allows 3×, so about 7e-5 eV/Å may be
added. Half arithmetic has 11 significant bits; over a short chain of operations it is right to about 1 part in
1,000. Two scalings, which disagree:
- **Optimistic:** the half-precision error is 1e-3 of what masking costs. That gives 6e-6 eV/Å at 5.8 Å and 4e-5 at
  5.5 Å; both fit.
- **Pessimistic:** masking measures a net sum, while rounding noise follows the sum of magnitudes. Production
  float32 rounding (6e-8 per operation) already gives 3.57e-5 eV/Å, so the force is a sum of terms hundreds of
  times larger than itself. Scaling the far shell's terms by the cutoff envelope (my rough estimate, not a
  measurement) puts the shell beyond 5.8 Å between 0.5× and 4× the rounding error, on either side of the 2× that
  is free, and the shell beyond 5.5 Å about ten times higher.

The paper estimate cannot decide the 5.8 Å shell, and it excludes the 5.5 Å one unless the optimistic scaling
holds. If only the shell beyond 5.9 Å passes, the idea is worth about 1 % of the step and stops at the lab bar.
Only the emulation below decides.

**Closest ledger entries.**
- `prec-fp16-edge-weights`, `prec-precision-screen`: 16-bit arithmetic over the whole path, 3–110× and 2–1,100×
  over the budget. This idea restricts it to edges whose whole contribution is small.
- `certified-skip`: dropping far edges, rejected by the oracle above. Cheapening them was not assessed.
- `table-compressed`: float16 coefficients over the whole table fail the certificate (3.16 with only c2 and c3 in
  float16). A far-shell table needs its own certificate, weighted by what the shell contributes.
- No entry uses packed half SIMT arithmetic. `omat0-fp16-tc` and `omat0-hybrid16` are FP16 tensor-core products.

**Size.** A far-shell edge costs about half of today's instructions per channel, a little less in the forward,
where it also drops the PACKED8 decode.
- Shell beyond 5.8 Å (12.8 % of SrTiO3's edges, 9.7 % at uniform density): about 130 µs on the H200 and 280 µs on
  the A100, 4 % of the step on both.
- Shell beyond 5.5 Å (26.4 %): about 270 µs and 580 µs, 8–9 %.
- Shell beyond 5.9 Å (4.3 %): about 45 µs and 95 µs, 1.4 %, under the 68 µs lab bar on the H200.

On the H200 the gain needs HFMA2 to issue like an FFMA. On the A100 it needs HFMA2 to run outside the saturated
FP32 FMA pipe; which pipe executes it there is not on file.

**First tests, in order.**
1. CPU: in `skip_oracle.py`, round the far edges' inputs and messages to float16 instead of masking them (about ten
   lines). Stop if the shell beyond 5.8 Å adds more than 1× the rounding error.
2. RTX 5060 Ti: add HFMA2 to `tools/fma_dual_pipe_bench.py`, alone and mixed with FFMA. Stop below 1.5 results per
   issued instruction.
3. Offline census of a half2 edge loop for sm_90 and sm_80 against two float channels.

**Stretch.** The same shell through FP16 tensor cores in one pass. No 3× split is needed there, because 11 bits
are all the shell requires. The tile-padding losses of `omat0-tc-tp` and `fp64-tensor-pipe` still apply.

## 4. GEMM cross terms in FP16, big·big kept in TF32

**The idea.** 3×TF32 computes A_hi W_hi and two cross products that are about 2,000× smaller. Keep A_hi W_hi in
TF32 (m16n8k8). Run both cross products as one FP16 GEMM over 2K (m16n8k16), which carries twice the multiply-adds
per tensor instruction. The GEMMs are bound by the tensor instruction rate, so that is where the time goes.

**What is on file.**
- `omat0-fp16x3-gemm` (5 November): all three products as one FP16 GEMM over 3K, rows and columns scaled by powers
  of two. The GEMM alone took 0.49–0.66 of the 3×TF32 kernel (5000×2048×128: 88 against 143 µs). The error was
  1.1–2.0e-6 against 1.1–2.7e-7, because the FP16 MMA accumulates more coarsely; rejected on accuracy. The unfused
  split cost 78–138 µs, as much as the GEMM saved.
- `omat0-hybrid16` (5 November): exactly this split, but inside the old fused forward. Accuracy was FP32-class
  (max|d|/max|a| 7e-7 to 1.7e-6). It saved a third of the MMA time and lost it to the per-fragment FP16 packs and
  the register limit: 1.017 on the A100, 0.851 on the 5060 Ti.

The ledger shows no trial of this split in the standalone CUTLASS GEMMs, which have no 12-warp register limit to
hit and reuse each A tile across all N columns.

**Size.** 1/3 + 2/3 × (0.49–0.66) = 0.66–0.77 of the GEMM time: 140–210 µs on the H200 and 250–360 µs on the A100,
4–6 % of the step. On the H200, 104 µs of the GEMM time are wave tails at M = 5,000 that this does not touch, so
the lower end is the likelier one there.

**Risk.** The split and the half conversion must be fused into the kernel's operand staging, and the row maxima
for the scaling need a producer or one extra pass.

**First test.** `tools/fp16x3_gemm_bench.py` with big·big in TF32 and the two cross terms in FP16, split fused.
Stop if the error is above 3×TF32's 2.7e-7, or the time above 0.85 of 3×TF32.

## 2. Two half-systems out of phase

**The idea.** Split the atoms into halves A and B and give the force graph two branches. While one half's edge
kernel fills the GPU, the other half's atom chain (GEMMs, contraction, elementwise kernels) runs beside it:

| Stage | Branch 1 | Branch 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layer 1 forward | edges A, then edges B | chain A beside edges B; chain B runs alone |
| Layer 2 forward and w′ | forward A, forward B, w′ A, w′ B | chain A beside forward B; chain B beside w′ A |
| Layer 1 backward | chain A, then edges A, edges B | chain B beside edges A |

The per-atom arithmetic is unchanged, so the results stay in today's rounding class.

**What can hide.** Only kernels that do not fill the GPU. In `limiters.csv`, the kernels outside the edge kernels,
GEMMs and contraction take 408 µs on the H200 and 668 µs on the A100, and issue nothing for 290 and 503 µs of that
(Σ duration × (1 − issue share)). With two halves, a half to three quarters of each chain runs beside an edge
kernel: 145–220 µs on the H200 and 250–380 µs on the A100, 4–6 % of the step.

**What cannot.** GEMMs and edge kernels beside each other are close to zero-sum: the opt5-T01 scheduler probe
behind `launch-sched-iso` (SIMT and MMA warps on one SM, at most 1.07×, as quoted in `tiled-concurrent-tiles.md`)
and `launch-het-megakernel` (gate 1.04 on sm_120, 0.92 on the A100).

**Why it is not `tc-overlap-bound`.** That bound (38 µs) counted the work that is already independent in the
graph. This creates independence by splitting the system. `overlap_rebuild` is the precedent: 4.31 ms of rebuild
work every 7.7 steps hides almost entirely beside the H200's force graph.

**Risks.** Twice the launches (the H200's graph gaps are 63 µs for 143 kernels today). Half-size GEMMs fill waves
worse than today's M = 5,000 launches. Small kernels may wait behind long-lived edge blocks; an SM split through
green contexts, as planned for tile lanes in `tiled-concurrent-tiles.md`, would avoid that.

**First test.** Capture two half-size force graphs and replay them on two streams against back to back, on the
target GPU inside an existing packed job. Stop if the pair is not at least 3 % faster.

## 3. Machine search over kernel formulations, scored by the offline census

**The idea.** On the H200 the two big edge kernels are 53 % of the kernel time and issue-bound, so their time
follows the instructions per edge loop. The offline census (compile for sm_90 on the CPU, count instructions,
registers and spills) is a free score. Search the ways to group and stage the trilinear sums of w, x, the
harmonics and dout, with an e-graph or an evolutionary loop over the templates' choices, under the register cap.
Only the best few go to the kernel lab.

**Why it is new.** `FACTORED`, `CG_FOLD`, `forward-ghoist`, `deferred-cg-forward` and `wprime-split-sums` are
points in that space, each found and timed by hand. No entry searches it.

**Size.** Unknown. Each 10 % of instructions removed from w′ and the forward is about 5 % of the H200 step. On the
A100 the score would be the FMA count, since w′ is FMA-bound there.

**First test.** Reproduce today's instruction counts for both kernels from the census, then check that the search
finds `FACTORED` again from the unfactored form. If it cannot, the cost model is wrong and the idea stops.

## Re-screen: forward-ghoist on the H200

Not a new idea: `forward-ghoist` is REJECTED in the ledger (9 November, `results/packed-table-lab-20261109/`). It
is listed here because the rejection rests on a GPU with a different limiter.

**What it is.** The channel-independent CG sums g_oi = Σ_j c_oij e_j are hoisted per edge into shared memory, and
each thread computes acc_o += (w x_i) g_oi.

**What was measured.**
- sm_90 SASS: the forward's edge loop drops from 227 to 192 instructions per edge per thread (FMUL 62 → 22, LDS
  8 → 26).
- RTX 5060 Ti: 1.07 of the real kernel, and 1.43× with every edge on one slab (1,810 against 1,261 µs). The shared
  g reads and their fill cost more than the multiplies removed.
- It was stopped before the H200.

**Why look again.**
- The H200's forward is 82 % issue-bound with the FMA pipes at 51 % (step-limiters, 9 November). The lab report's
  own count gives an issue floor of about 320 µs for 227 instructions.
- The two GPUs already disagreed on a like question: replacing w′'s table loads with one integer op each gave
  0.716 on the 5060 Ti and 1.043 on the H200 (`wprime-zero-byte`).

**Caveats.**
- The lab variant fills g with harmonics, so its output is wrong. It shows the instruction shape only; the real
  rewrite computes g at staging, and that fill is part of what lost on the 5060 Ti.
- PACKED8 is the H200 default since 9 November and lengthens the loop, so 35 instructions are a smaller share than
  the 15 % they were.
- 18 more shared loads per edge go the wrong way if the forward's shared-memory reads are near their limit.

**Bound and stop rule.** If every removed instruction became saved time, 35 of 227 instructions of a 638 µs kernel
is 96 µs, and about 70 µs on the longer PACKED8 loop. The lab bar is 68 µs, so it passes only if nearly all of
that is realised. One H200 run of `tools/spline_read_lab.py` `ghoist`, packed into an existing job: stop for good
if the forward is above 0.89 of real.

## Worked through and dropped

- **Two float32 lanes packed in one double FMA.** The two products overlap in the mantissa; only about 13 bits per
  lane survive.
- **A global radial basis for interaction 1** (fewer radial channels per edge). `rad-low-rank` found rank 65 of
  65, so no basis has fewer than 64 functions.
- **Texture-unit interpolation for the tables.** The hardware's filter weights have 8 fractional bits.
- **Strassen on the GEMMs.** One level saves an eighth, under 56 µs on the H200 before its additions.
- **INT8 for the GEMM cross terms.** 7 bits where the cross terms need about 11.

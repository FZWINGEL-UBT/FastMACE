## The rule (`fastmace/neighbors/adaptive.py`)

The skin list stays valid while no atom has moved more than skin/2 since its build, so the skin sets the
rebuild rate. Rule 11 uses no tables:

- **The fastest atom.** sigma = sqrt(k_B T_max / m_min) is one velocity component's deviation for the
  lightest mass. The speed of an atom in units of sigma is Maxwell-distributed. z_N is the speed that about
  one of N atoms exceeds: N · [erfc(z/√2) + √(2/π) z e^(−z²/2)] = 1. For example, z_216 = 3.61 and
  z_1944 = 4.20.
- **Ballistic per step.** d = z_N · sigma · dt. Over a rebuild interval (tens of fs), the ions keep their
  velocity.
- **Skin:** max(0.3 Å, 2 · n* · d), rounded up to 0.01 Å, with n* = `TARGET_REBUILD_STEPS` = 16 steps
  per rebuild. Displacement moves add 2 · δ_max (`Workload.max_displacement_A`).
- **Pair-buffer headroom:** max(1.15, 1 + 3 · (skin/2) / (r_c + skin)). Over one interval, the shell of
  candidates within skin/2 of r_c + skin turns over.
- **Edge buckets:** 2 · ceil((headroom − 1) / edge_bucket_fraction) + 1 padded edge buckets can be
  visited inside the capacity, above and below the first count.
  - The plan records this count in the `EngineConfig.recapture_budget` decision.
  - The budget itself is 3-T04's rule, the graph variant cap: one new bucket re-captures several stage
    graphs, so the bucket count would undercount re-captures. This is a merge resolution with 3-T04
    (main 49e20b1).

**Check against data.** The model predicts a rebuild every 24 steps for NaCl at 300 K and 0.5 fs with the
0.3 Å skin; 1a-noise-floor measured about 25. At 300 K and 0.5 fs, NaCl, NbCl5 and NaNbO3 stay on the
0.3 Å floor.

**Where it applies** (conductor answer 3-T05 1–2):
- **MC moves or declared species changes:** `"auto"` takes the rule's values.
- **MD-only workloads:** `"auto"` keeps 0.3 Å and 1.15, so default plans do not change. The decisions
  `workload.max_temperature_K` and `NeighbourConfig.skin` record what the rule would give.

**Other resolutions:**
- Explicit values are kept: rule 11 is a preference.
- Evaluation plans and `FixedPlanner` plans have no workload and keep 0.3 Å.
- The lightest mass is the lightest of the structure's masses and the superset species' standard masses.

The fused integrator reads `neighbours.skin` when it is constructed (`fused_integrator.py:207-209`), so it
follows the planned skin. A CPU test pins this.

A larger skin does not change the edges within the cutoff of a receiver-sorted list, nor their order. The
CPU test `test_a_larger_skin_keeps_the_sorted_edges_within_the_cutoff` compares 0.3 Å with 0.9 Å. So the
skin changes the rebuild rate, not the forces.

## GPU: fixed against adaptive skin (`tools/ab_skin.py`)

**Protocol.** There are two MH-1 hybrid float32 NVT engines (`mh1-r2scan`) from the minimal planner in one
process, on the same structure, temperature and timestep:
- side a: fixed `skin=0.3`, headroom 1.15;
- side b: rule 11's skin and headroom, passed explicitly so that the MD-only plan uses them.

Each side warms up for 1,500 steps outside the compile-counter window. Then come 12 alternating blocks of
300 steps (ABAB for build order ab, ABBA for ba). The timestep is 1 fs, the production case, at 1500 K and
1000 K. The result is the ratio of medians b/a. "Rebuilds" counts the neighbour rebuilds during the 3,600
timed steps of each side (`GraphEngine.info`). Every run is valid: nothing compiled while timed, and no
other GPU process ran. No capacity grew in any run.

## What the numbers show

- **The rule does what it is built for.** At 1 fs the fixed 0.3 Å skin rebuilds:
  - every 5.0–5.3 steps at 1500 K;
  - every 6.2–6.4 steps at 1000 K.

  Rule 11's skins rebuild every 13.3–13.8 steps on both GPUs, close to the target n* = 16. The ballistic
  model overestimates the fastest atom's drift a little, so the measured interval is shorter than the
  target. That is 2.2–2.7× fewer rebuilds, and fewer pipelined redos: 32 → 18 on local NaCl at 1500 K.
- **Step time is neutral within noise.**
  - Every b/a lies inside its machine's noise floor: local 0.988–1.004 (floors 1.1 % and 0.4 %); L4
    0.997–1.001 (floors 0.2 % and 0.6 %).
  - The largest values above 1 are local NaCl 1500 K ab at 1.0042 (floor 1.1 %) and L4 NaCl 1500 K ab at
    1.0013 (floor 0.2 %). The opposite build order gives 0.988 and 0.997 for the same cases.
  - NbCl5 is faster by 0.1–0.3 % in 6 of its 7 runs; that is at the edge of the noise.
- **Why so little time changes.** The default `overlap_rebuild` (OPT2-T04) already runs these early
  rebuilds on a side stream beside the force graph: `rebuilds_beside` equals the rebuild count. The
  larger candidate set (+39 % at 0.85 Å for NaCl: 14,040 candidates against 10,126) apparently costs about as much
  in compaction as the saved rebuilds gave back.
- **Headroom.** No capacity grew with either side in any run. Rule 11's headrooms of 1.15–1.19 suffice
  here, and so does 1.15 with the fixed skin. The bucket count (7–9) was not tested by growth.
- **Default plans** at 300 K are unchanged. MD-only plans keep 0.3 Å by the conductor's answer; the
  CPU tests pin this.

**For the conductor.** Rule 11 does not slow any measured case, so n* stays at 16. As an MD-only default
it would cut rebuilds by 2–3× at high temperature, but it would not speed up today's graph tier, since
`overlap_rebuild` hides the rebuilds. Its value is for workloads where rebuilds are not hidden:
- displacement-MC trials that leave the graph (3-T10);
- the compiled tier (3-T06);
- plans without `overlap_rebuild` (NPT, speculative).

Not measured here: NPT, where the deformation-aware test applies, and the displacement skin on the GPU
(3-T10's measurement).

## What the estimate counts

`fastmace/planner/memory.py`. The estimate uses no GPU table. It reads the model's widths
(`ModelTraits.dims`, read from the irreps by `capabilities.model_dims`) and the plan's sizes.

| Term | From |
| --- | --- |
| weights | parameters × bytes of the force precision |
| activations | per interaction: per edge, the gathered sender features, each radial network's input, its hidden units × the floats its backward keeps (2 for MACE's RadialMLP: Linear → LayerNorm → SiLU; 1 for e3nn's FullyConnectedNet), its output (the tensor product weights) and the messages; per node, node features, scattered messages, the product's terms. Once per call: the edge geometry, spherical harmonics and radial basis. The backward's transient is one interaction's message gradient, the widest. With a fused convolution (OEq in the hybrid backend, cuEq's fused convolution) the sender features and messages are not kept per edge. |
| rewrites | each enabled record's declared term (`Rewrite.memory`, `fastmace/rewrites/memory.py`) |
| neighbour list | the MACE pair list's capacity × its bytes per slot (`neighbors/skin.py`) |
| D3 list | `neighbors.adaptive.rule_12_d3` (3b-T02), where D3 is on |
| workspace | 64 MiB of library workspaces a capture allocates (`engine/graph.py`), on CUDA |

The edges are both directions of every pair within `r_max`, at the structure's uniform density, plus one
edge bucket. The list capacity uses cutoff + skin at the pair-buffer headroom. The graph tier's variants
share one pool, so the largest variant counts once.

`SAFETY` multiplies the activations and the rewrite terms. It is one factor for every model and GPU.

**Build against run.** The prediction is the larger of the run's estimate and the build's. Both hold:
- the two copies of the weights, the pristine model and the execution copy;
- the buffers that enabled rewrites keep beside them (`Rewrite.resident_memory(dims, width)`):
  - `block_linear` keeps each converted linear's weight and its transpose, `2 × linear_weights`;
  - `merged_residual` keeps `linear_1` and `linear_res` stacked again with their transpose,
    `2 × residual_weights`;
  - both come from `ModelDims`, read from the model's shared-weight equivariant linears;
- the 64 MiB library workspace, on CUDA.

The run adds the force call's terms. The build adds the largest transient of a rewrite's self-check, which
each record declares as `Rewrite.selfcheck_memory(dims, width, sms)`. The device's SM count comes from
`Hardware.sms` (`multi_processor_count`, detected). Today `segment_gemm` declares one:
- its self-check probes `MIN_TRITON_WAVES × sms × BLOCK_M` atoms, so that every product takes Triton on the
  device;
- the probes are as wide as `linear_2` (the target irreps);
- 9 such tensors are live at once (8.0–8.1 measured on the tiny model).

The A100 memory history showed why this term is needed: the build's peak was 9.6× the run's (below).
Decision 51's drops follow the run's terms, and a rewrite turned off takes its self-check's transient with it.

## The action (decision 51)

The planner compares the part not yet on the device with `MARGIN` = 90 % of the free GPU memory. CUDA's
count is used when this process has initialised CUDA; otherwise the device's total memory is used. When the
estimate is above the margin:

1. The rewrites whose terms add an edge-sized buffer go off, largest term first, until the estimate fits.
   Each goes together with the records that require it. Each gets rule 12's reason (`rule-12-memory-drop`).
2. If the estimate is still above, the plan stays as it is and warns (`rule-12-memory-warn`, a
   `ResourceWarning`).
3. A rewrite the caller set explicitly is never turned off; an explicit `"on"` is kept and named in the
   warning. Rule 11's skin and headroom, the tier and the precision never change.

An out-of-memory error at build or at the first capture carries the plan's `memory` decision in its message
(`planner/fallback.py`). `Plan.predicted_memory_bytes` holds the total.

## CPU results (tiny models)

`tests/cpu/test_memory_estimate.py`. Each force call's peak is measured with a dispatch-mode tracker of live
storages (`tests/_support/memory_peak.py`), since the CPU allocator keeps no peak. It covers the forward plus
the force backward, with frozen weights as in the engine.

**Activations against the peak** (the four tiny families, float64 and float32, NaCl 2×2×2 and 3×3×3):
unscaled, the estimate is 0.84–0.91 of the measured peak in every case. The ratio does not depend on the
family; float32 gives the same ratios. With `SAFETY` = 1.25 the ratios are 1.05–1.14. The test asserts
1 ≤ ratio ≤ 1.5.

The per-edge slope was measured with 1–3 interactions and radial widths (64, 64, 64) and (32). It shows:
- 2 floats kept per hidden unit for MACE's RadialMLP and 1 for e3nn's network;
- MH-1's `density_fn` as a second per-edge network;
- a backward transient of one interaction's message width, independent of the radial width.

**Declared rewrite terms against the measured change** of the peak per edge (tiny MH-1 and residual
models, float64, bytes per edge; float32 halves both columns):

| Record | Declared | Measured |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `factor_species` | +1920 | +2076 |
| `fold_cutoff` | +400 | +384 |
| `joint_radial_input` | −1072 | −1072 |
| `reassociate_first` | +336 | +277 |
| `fused_radial_hidden` | −3072 | −3136 |
| `generic_reassociate` (with its segmented sum) | +328 | +269 |

The test allows 25 % or 16 floats per edge. `density_first`, the linear family, the fusion family and the
species tables change no per-edge tensor (measured 0 to −1.3 floats per edge), so they declare no term. The
streaming convolutions' term (+8 bytes per edge, their int32 senders and receivers) is CUDA-only and is
checked by the GPU peaks below.

On the released models, `fold_cutoff` and the reassociations should come out negative: their tensor product
weights are wider than the last hidden width + 1. The signs of the other terms there follow from each
model's widths; the GPU runs record them.

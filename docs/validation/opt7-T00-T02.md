# OPT7-T00 to T02: LunaTP and the streaming convolution backward (27 October 2026)

Plan: `plans/opt-7.md`. Records and scripts: `results/opt7-T00-T02-20261027/`. All on the local RTX 5060 Ti
(sm_120, driver 610.88), WSL2; FastMACE side `~/environments/mace_cueq_oeq` with `fastmace/opt7-plan` on
`PYTHONPATH`; LunaTP side `~/environments/lunatp_screen` (a copy of `mace_cueq_oeq` with `e3nn==0.6.0` and
`LunaEquivariance` 0.1.0 at `5a24ffe`, no dependencies, built with nvcc 13.3 for sm_120; MACE is broken there by
design, the scratch venv runs no model; list `lunatp_screen.requirements.txt`).

**Verdict: none of the three hypotheses is worth a kernel on this GPU.** LunaTP itself is slower on every large
cell; the backward's sender-gradient atomics cost at most 5 % of it; and the backward's extra traffic (output
gradients re-read per edge) is real but its known remedy, receiver ownership, loses more to parallelism than it
saves. The `w`/`dw` traffic (half the backward's L2 bytes, two thirds of the forward's) stays the lever, and that is
the fused table → `w` tile → convolution kernel of the post-radial-tables plan, owned by another session.

## Operands

`record.py`: one eager force call (`TORCHDYNAMO_DISABLE=1`) per cell, default float32 hybrid plan, rattled cell;
the second interaction's streaming convolution: `x`, `sh`, the per-edge weights `w = a W^T`, senders, receivers,
CSR rows, the output gradient `dout`, and the OEq problem (10 `uvu` paths, `128x0e+128x1o` in, 5120 out, `ir_mul`).
MH-1 and OMAT-0 medium have the same convolution shapes. Edges: NaCl 6,912, NbCl3O 17,734, NbCl5 52,416, NaNbO3
84,072.

Path weights: OEq's `TPProblem` with `"none"` normalisation applies the square root of the given path weight, so the
benches pass squared weights; then the vendored kernels reproduce the recorded message exactly (`normcheck.py`).

LunaTP against the streaming kernels: forward and all three gradients within 1.1e-6 relative (`t00.jsonl`).

on an H100, not against the receiver-streaming kernels. **Stop rule (≥ 1.3× on NaNbO3): not met.**

## T01 (a): the sender-gradient atomics

`t01a.py`: the edge-owned backward's four `atomicAdd` into `dx` per (edge, channel), rewritten in the generated
source only: `racy` (the same read-modify-write without atomics: wrong result, same traffic) and `drop` (no `dx`;
the compiler also drops its arithmetic). Same process, three alternating rounds, median (ms):

| Case | base | racy | drop |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| MH-1 NaCl | 0.232 | 0.247 (1.07) | 0.236 (1.02) |
| MH-1 NbCl3O | 0.602 | 0.645 (1.07) | 0.601 (1.00) |
| MH-1 NbCl5 | 1.809 | 1.905 (1.05) | 1.771 (0.98) |
| MH-1 NaNbO3 | 2.839 | 2.980 (1.05) | 2.737 (0.96) |
| OMAT-0 (the same four) | | 1.02–1.07 | 0.95–0.99 |

The atomics are nearly free (Blackwell's L2 reductions): a `dx` that cost nothing would save at most 5 % of the
backward, at most 1 % of a step. **Stop rule (≥ 25 % of the backward): not met; T01 (b), a source-owned backward,
is not built.**

## T02: where the bytes go

`ncu_probe.py` under Nsight Compute 13.3 (`--cache-control all`, clocks unlocked; `dram__bytes_*` are not
available under WSL, so L2 traffic `lts__t_bytes` and L1 global loads stand in), one forward and one backward:

| | NbCl5 forward | NbCl5 backward | NaNbO3 forward | NaNbO3 backward |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| time (ncu) | 0.76 ms | 1.66 ms | 1.17 ms | 3.14 ms |
| memory throughput (% of peak) | 94 | 84 | 94 | 81 |
| L2 bytes | 422 MB | 1,094 MB | 642 MB | 1,673 MB |
| of which `w` (forward) or `w` + `dw` (backward), compulsory | 268 MB (64 %) | 537 MB (49 %) | 430 MB (67 %) | 861 MB (51 %) |
| L1 global loads | 490 MB | 2,204 MB | 786 MB | 3,535 MB |
| L2 reductions (atomics) | 0 | 0.84 M | 0 | 1.35 M |

Half of the backward's L2 traffic is not `w`/`dw`: mostly each receiver's 20 KB `dout` row, read once per edge
(about 27 times per atom), and the sender rows of `x`. The plan's rule (non-`w` ≥ 30 %) passes for the backward.

**The receiver-owned backward** (`ncu_probe_rcv.py`; the kernel FastMACE keeps behind `receiver_backward`, rejected
26 October) reads each `dout` row once: NbCl5 927 MB of L2 (−15 %) and 1,174 MB of L1 loads (−47 %), yet
2.10 ms against 1.66 ms; NaNbO3 1,502 MB (−10 %), 3.31 against 3.14 ms. It runs at 69–72 % of memory throughput
with 5× the reductions (`dsh` per warp). One block per receiver serialises a receiver's edges and loses
parallelism; the saved bytes do not come back as time. Path grouping, which also needs a receiver-owned loop to keep
its accumulators, would face the same trade.

## Ceilings (from `plans/opt-7.md` §1)

If the backward's non-`w` traffic vanished at no other cost, a bandwidth-bound backward would gain at most about
2× (s ≈ 2): step 1.05 (MH-1 NbCl5), 1.10 (MH-1 NaNbO3), 1.12–1.14 (OMAT-0). The receiver-owned measurement shows
the cost is not zero. Removing `w`/`dw` instead (idea 2) removes about half of the backward's bytes and two thirds of
the forward's without changing who owns an edge.

## Derivative activity specialisation

Not measured: in MD every first derivative of the streaming convolution is consumed (§2 of the plan). The l = 0
column of `dsh` (1/16 of `dsh`) is the only discardable work; the kernel was not changed for it.

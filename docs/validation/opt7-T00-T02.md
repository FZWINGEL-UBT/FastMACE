# OPT7-T00 to T02: LunaTP and the streaming convolution backward (27 October 2026)

Plan: `plans/opt-7.md`. Records and scripts: `results/opt7-T00-T02-20261027/`. All on the local RTX 5060 Ti
(sm_120, driver 610.88), WSL2; FastMACE side `~/environments/mace_cueq_oeq` with `fastmace/opt7-plan` on
`PYTHONPATH`; LunaTP side `~/environments/lunatp_screen` (a copy of `mace_cueq_oeq` with `e3nn==0.6.0` and
`LunaEquivariance` 0.1.0 at `5a24ffe`, no dependencies, built with nvcc 13.3 for sm_120; MACE is broken there by
design, the scratch venv runs no model; list `lunatp_screen.requirements.txt`).

**Verdict: none of the three hypotheses is worth a kernel on this GPU.** LunaTP itself is slower on every large
cell; the backward's sender-gradient atomics cost at most 5 % of it; and the backward's extra traffic (output
gradients re-read per edge) is real but its known remedy, receiver ownership, loses more to parallelism than it
saves. The `w`/`dw` traffic (half the backward's L2 bytes, two thirds of the forward's) stays the lever, and that is
the fused table → `w` tile → convolution kernel of the post-radial-tables plan, owned by another session.

**Follow-up (owner: "Are you sure it was a fair investigation?").** Three gaps were checked; see "Fairness
follow-up" at the end. The backward was re-timed as LunaTP's backward kernel directly (no subtraction): the NaCl
"0.76–0.81" of the T00 table was an artefact of subtracting input copies as large as the kernel (direct: 0.251 ms
against 0.240, 1.05). Its kernel parameters were swept: the best (vector width 2) is 0.92 on NaCl, 1.09 on NbCl3O,
1.22 on NaNbO3 and 1.50 on NbCl5. Nsight Compute shows why: LunaTP's source-owned backward executes 25 % fewer
instructions but moves 1.5× the L2 bytes and 2.6× the L1 loads, at 27–32 % occupancy (128 registers); our edge-owned
backward is bandwidth bound, so the extra traffic decides.

## Operands

`record.py`: one eager force call (`TORCHDYNAMO_DISABLE=1`) per cell, default float32 hybrid plan, rattled cell;
the second interaction's streaming convolution: `x`, `sh`, the per-edge weights `w = a W^T`, senders, receivers,
CSR rows, the output gradient `dout`, and the OEq problem (10 `uvu` paths, `128x0e+128x1o` in, 5120 out, `ir_mul`).
MH-1 and OMAT-0 medium have the same convolution shapes. Edges: NaCl 6,912, NbCl3O 17,734, NbCl5 52,416, NaNbO3
84,072.

Path weights: OEq's `TPProblem` with `"none"` normalisation applies the square root of the given path weight, so the
benches pass squared weights; then the vendored kernels reproduce the recorded message exactly (`normcheck.py`).

## T01 (a): the sender-gradient atomics

`t01a.py`: the edge-owned backward's four `atomicAdd` into `dx` per (edge, channel), rewritten in the generated
source only: `racy` (the same read-modify-write without atomics: wrong result, same traffic) and `drop` (no `dx`;
the compiler also drops its arithmetic). Same process, three alternating rounds, median (ms):

| Case | base | racy | drop |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| MH-1 NaCl | 0.232 | 0.247 (1.07) | 0.236 (1.02) |
| MH-1 NbCl3O | 0.602 | 0.645 (1.07) | 0.601 (1.00) |
| MH-1 NbCl5 | 1.809 | 1.905 (1.05) | 1.771 (0.98) |

| OMAT-0 (the same four) | | 1.02–1.07 | 0.95–0.99 |

The atomics are nearly free (Blackwell's L2 reductions): a `dx` that cost nothing would save at most 5 % of the
backward, at most 1 % of a step. **Stop rule (≥ 25 % of the backward): not met; T01 (b), a source-owned backward,
is not built.**

## T02: where the bytes go

`ncu_probe.py` under Nsight Compute 13.3 (`--cache-control all`, clocks unlocked; `dram__bytes_*` are not
available under WSL, so L2 traffic `lts__t_bytes` and L1 global loads stand in), one forward and one backward:

| | NbCl5 forward | NbCl5 backward | NaNbO3 forward | NaNbO3 backward |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| time (ncu) | 0.76 ms | 1.66 ms | 1.17 ms | 3.14 ms |
| memory throughput (% of peak) | 94 | 84 | 94 | 81 |
| L2 bytes | 422 MB | 1,094 MB | 642 MB | 1,673 MB |
| of which `w` (forward) or `w` + `dw` (backward), compulsory | 268 MB (64 %) | 537 MB (49 %) | 430 MB (67 %) | 861 MB (51 %) |
| L1 global loads | 490 MB | 2,204 MB | 786 MB | 3,535 MB |
| L2 reductions (atomics) | 0 | 0.84 M | 0 | 1.35 M |

Half of the backward's L2 traffic is not `w`/`dw`: mostly each receiver's 20 KB `dout` row, read once per edge
(about 27 times per atom), and the sender rows of `x`. The plan's rule (non-`w` ≥ 30 %) passes for the backward.

**The receiver-owned backward** (`ncu_probe_rcv.py`; the kernel FastMACE keeps behind `receiver_backward`, rejected
26 October) reads each `dout` row once: NbCl5 927 MB of L2 (−15 %) and 1,174 MB of L1 loads (−47 %), yet
2.10 ms against 1.66 ms; NaNbO3 1,502 MB (−10 %), 3.31 against 3.14 ms. It runs at 69–72 % of memory throughput
with 5× the reductions (`dsh` per warp). One block per receiver serialises a receiver's edges and loses
parallelism; the saved bytes do not come back as time. Path grouping, which also needs a receiver-owned loop to keep
its accumulators, would face the same trade.

## Ceilings (from `plans/opt-7.md` §1)

If the backward's non-`w` traffic vanished at no other cost, a bandwidth-bound backward would gain at most about
2× (s ≈ 2): step 1.05 (MH-1 NbCl5), 1.10 (MH-1 NaNbO3), 1.12–1.14 (OMAT-0). The receiver-owned measurement shows
the cost is not zero. Removing `w`/`dw` instead (idea 2) removes about half of the backward's bytes and two thirds of
the forward's without changing who owns an edge.

## Derivative activity specialisation

Not measured: in MD every first derivative of the streaming convolution is consumed (§2 of the plan). The l = 0
column of `dsh` (1/16 of `dsh`) is the only discardable work; the kernel was not changed for it.

## Fairness follow-up (27 October 2026)

The T00 numbers were questioned on three counts: LunaTP ran with its default parameters only, its backward was
timed by subtraction, and the conclusion about path grouping was an inference.

**Direct backward timing.** `luna_bench.py` now also times LunaTP's backward kernel itself
(`lunatp.uvu_conv.interface.run_backward`, the function its autograd calls; outputs equal the streaming kernels' to
5.6e-7). NaCl: 0.251 ms (ours 0.240); NbCl5: 2.741 ms (ours 1.792). The subtraction had removed 0.22 ms of input
copies from a 0.5 ms total on NaCl, which produced the false 0.76–0.81.

**Parameter sweep** (`patch_luna.py` adds environment overrides to the scratch venv's installed copy only;
`sweep.sh`; direct timing, MH-1 operands; ms and ratio to the streaming backward). LunaTP's own choice for this
problem: one fused program, 128 threads per source atom, vector width 1, 16 resident warps (launch bounds 4 blocks),
not segmented.

| Setting | NaCl | NbCl3O | NbCl5 | NaNbO3 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| default | 0.240 (1.00) | 0.730 (1.19) | 2.750 (1.53) | 3.996 (1.41) |
| resident warps 8–32, 32–128 threads (12 settings) | 0.234–0.256 (0.97–1.07) | 0.703–0.730 (1.14–1.19) | 2.709–2.872 (1.51–1.60) | 3.754–4.162 (1.32–1.47) |
| vector width 2, 16 warps, 64 threads | 0.226 (0.94) | 0.671 (1.09) | 2.707 (1.51) | 3.496 (1.23) |
| vector width 2, 8 warps, 64 threads | **0.220 (0.92)** | 0.667 (1.09) | 2.695 (1.50) | 3.475 (1.22) |

Every setting stayed exact (all three gradients within 1e-5 of the streaming kernels). LunaTP's default heuristic
leaves at most 8 % (NaCl) to 13 % (NaNbO3) on the table; tuned, it is still 9–50 % slower on every cell above NaCl.

**Nsight Compute** (`ncu_cmp.sh`, `ncusum.py`; one backward each, `--cache-control all`, so times are cold-cache):

| | ours, NaCl | LunaTP, NaCl | ours, NbCl5 | LunaTP, NbCl5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| time (µs) | 195 | 382 + 11 (reduce) | 1,664 | 2,609 + 34 |
| L2 bytes | 143 MB | 224 MB | 1,116 MB | 1,726 MB |
| L1 global loads | 291 MB | 767 MB | 2,204 MB | 5,821 MB |
| instructions | 21.3 M | 15.8 M | 161 M | 120 M |
| memory throughput | 81 % | 87 % | 84 % | 83 % |
| SM throughput | 45 % | 25 % | 38 % | 27 % |
| achieved occupancy | 65 % | 27 % | 66 % | 32 % |
| registers per thread | 64 | 128 | 64 | 128 |
| grid (blocks of 128) | 1,728 | 216 | 13,104 | 1,944 |

LunaTP's source-owned backward needs no `dx` atomics and executes about 25 % fewer instructions (path grouping,
product and coefficient reuse). But walking edges by sender gathers each receiver's `dout` row and each edge's `w`
row out of order, which costs 1.5× the L2 traffic and 2.6× the L1 loads, at half our occupancy. Both kernels sit at
81–87 % of memory throughput: on this GPU the backward is bound by bytes, not instructions, and the instructions
LunaTP saves cannot pay for its bytes. On an H100 (3.35 TB/s, 50 MB L2) bandwidth is relatively cheaper, which fits
its published gains there; that balance was not measured here.

**What carries over:** nothing that would pay on sm_120. Vector width 2 helped LunaTP by 8–13 %; whether wider loads
help our edge-owned backward is the next section.

## Wider loads in the streaming backward (owner request, 27 October 2026)

`wide.py`: a source transform of the generated `oeq_projected_backward` (float32). Each lane of an edge's 32-lane
group handles two adjacent channels per iteration (64 per pass instead of 32): every per-edge weight, output-gradient
and sender-feature element is read with one 8-byte `float2` load for both channels (136 load sites), the weight
gradients are stored as `float2` (10 sites), sender-gradient atomics stay scalar. Same process, the unchanged and the
wide kernel alternating in 5 rounds of CUDA-graph replays with an L2 flush; median of the rounds.

Exactness: `dw` bitwise equal; `dx` and `dsh` within 2.4e-7 relative (the order of a lane's channel sums changes;
`dx` was already nondeterministic through its atomics).

| Cell | local RTX 5060 Ti (sm_120), MH-1 | OMAT-0 | murphy RTX 4000 Ada (sm_89), MH-1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NaCl | 0.931 | 0.935 | 0.951 |
| NbCl3O | 0.962 | 0.964 | 1.001 |
| NbCl5 | 0.971 | 0.977 | 1.003 |
| NaNbO3 | 0.961 | 0.964 | 0.998 |

Every local round was below 1 (0.91–0.99); on murphy every round above NaCl was within ±0.4 % of 1. murphy: NAS
`mace_cueq_oeq`, the branch's `fastmace/` from a `git archive` in a scratch folder
`/mnt/homes/zwingel/opt7-wide-20261027` (operands deleted afterwards; GPU and other users' processes checked idle
before the run). Records `wide-local.jsonl`, `wide-murphy.jsonl`.

At the step level: the backward is 9–19 % of an MH-1 step and 15–25 % of an OMAT-0 step locally, so 0.3–1.7 % per
step on sm_120 and about 0 on Ada except NaCl (≈0.5 %). Under the owner's rule of 26 October 2026 (about 1 % on only
some cases does not change a default) this is not a candidate; the transform is kept in the results as a screen
tool, not in FastMACE. The path-grouping conclusion of T02 stays an inference from the
receiver-owned kernel; LunaTP's node-centric forward, which does group paths, is within 0–11 % of our streaming
forward.

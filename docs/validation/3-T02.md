## The refresh functions under capture

`tests/gpu/test_superset_gpu.py` (fast tier, `mace_cueq_latest`): **2 passed in 13.6 s**.

- **Setup:** tiny float64 models on CUDA, eager. The superset is O, Na, Cl and Fe; Fe is absent from the structure and is the model's last column.
- **Capture:** every refresh function that the force field registers (`register_derived`) is captured in one CUDA graph.
- **Event:** two atoms swap species, two more become Fe, and the per-atom charges change (joint-embedding case). Then one replay.
- **Checks:**
  - the buffers keep their addresses;
  - every derived buffer equals a fresh construction's, within rtol 1e-14;
  - energy, forces and stress equal a fresh construction's, within rtol 1e-11.
- **Cases:**
  - an MH-1-like model with the MH-1 package (segmented first sum: four sender groups);
  - a generic residual model with an OMOL-0-style joint embedding. Its table follows the total charge, refreshed on the device.

The first attempt (`attempt1/`) failed on two bugs in my test and tool code, not in the library:
- the test checked the sender-group count in a case that has no sender groups;
- `superset_cost.py` touched CUDA before `configure_allocator`, so the engine refused to build.

Both are fixed on this branch.

## The cost of a superset larger than the structure

`tools/superset_cost.py` (`mace_cueq_oeq`): MH-1 `matpes_r2scan`, hybrid float32, NVT at 300 K, NaCl 3×3×3 (216 atoms), minimal planner (what `Simulation` builds). Both sides run in one process, with 12 timed blocks of 300 steps each, alternating in `abba` order.

| Side | Superset | Reassociation slots | Sender groups | Median step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| structure | Na Cl | 2 | 2 | 2.921 ms |
| superset | O Na Cl Nb | 4 | 4 | 2.955 ms |

- **Ratio (superset over structure):** 1.0115.
- **Pair ratios:** from 0.984 to 1.033. Ten of the twelve are above 1.
- **Spread within each side:** 11 % and 7 %, much larger than this machine's NaCl noise floor of 1.1 %. The PC was not quiet: other sessions' CPU suites may have run beside it.
- **Reading:** the superset costs about **1 % of the step time** on NaCl. That is at the noise floor, but consistently positive.

**Memory.** The comparison is not clean:
- The first side's build kept 247 MiB, the second's 159 MiB. The first side also pays one-time costs, so a larger superset showed no larger footprint.
- The warm-up peaks (367 and 526 MiB) include the resident first side, so they are not comparable.
- The superset's extra state is small: per-atom sums over 4 instead of 2 slots in the first interaction, and a 4 × C feature table.

**Physics check.** After the timed blocks, both sides have the same temperature (311.6264 K) and potential energy per atom (−6.85365558 eV), within float32 rounding.

**Conclusion.** The default superset stays "the structure's species" (SPEC §8.5). A declared superset of four species on NaCl costs about 1 % of the step. A quiet-machine A/B, and NbCl5 (more atoms per species), would pin the number down if 3-T20 needs it.

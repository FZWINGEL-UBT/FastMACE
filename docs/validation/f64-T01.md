## 1. Where float64 time goes

Profile (`run.sh`; `runs/out-*-f64/categories.csv`), per force call, hybrid float64:

| | NaCl | NbCl5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| force call | 74 ms | 591 ms |
| atom-level linear layers (GEMMs) | 44.7 ms (61 %) | 371 ms (63 %) |
| radial folded last layer (GEMM) | 10.1 ms (14 %) | 74 ms (13 %) |
| radial hidden layers, other radial GEMMs | 6.3 ms (9 %) | 44 ms (7 %) |
| convolutions, symmetric contraction, elementwise | ~12 % | ~13 % |

The DGEMMs reach 0.31–0.35 TFLOPS, 70–75 % of the GPU's FP64 peak (1/64 of FP32): float64 is bound by FP64
arithmetic. Plain cuEq float64 is 1.4× slower than the hybrid (117 / 891 ms per step), mostly in the radial folded
layer (243 ms per NbCl5 call against 74).

## 2. cuBLAS FP64 emulation (integer Ozaki scheme)

cuBLAS 13 emulates DGEMM with INT8 tensor-core products (`CUBLAS_EMULATE_DOUBLE_PRECISION=1`; strategy
`CUBLAS_EMULATION_STRATEGY=performant` (default) or `eager`; mantissa bits
`CUBLAS_FIXEDPOINT_EMULATION_MANTISSA_BIT_COUNT`). It is read when cuBLAS creates its handle, so it is per
process. It works on sm_120 (kernels `cublasLt_fused_imma_dgemm_kernel_sm80`) and inside the captured graphs.

**GEMM screen** (`gemm_emu.py`, `runs/emu.jsonl`): MH-1's shapes; errors on 64 sampled rows against a long-double
reference, scaled by |A||B|, for normal operands and for rows and columns spread over 2^±20:

| | 13608×512×512 | 1944×512×512 | 56320×1280×65 | 56320×64×64 | max error, normal / spread |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| native DGEMM | 20.7 ms | 3.05 ms | 39.7 ms | 1.43 ms | 6e-16 / 2.7e-15 |
| emulation, default | 8.2 | 1.01 | 39.4 (native) | 1.42 (native) | 8e-17 / 2.5e-16 |
| emulation, eager | 9.8 | 0.96 | 26.4 | 2.78 | 1.3e-16 / 9.7e-16 |
| eager, 40 mantissa bits | 3.8 | 0.63 | 16.4 | 1.87 | 6e-14 / 6e-12 |

The default strategy emulates the large atom-level products (about 3× faster) and leaves small and skinny ones
native. Eager also emulates the radial 1280×65 products (1.5×) but slows the 64×64 ones. At its default mantissa
setting the emulation is **more** accurate than native DGEMM (exact integer accumulation). Fewer mantissa bits
lose accuracy fast and were not pursued.

**Forces** (`f64acc.py`, one rattled cell; against native hybrid float64): default emulation max |ΔF| 6.9e-16
(NaCl), 4.4e-15 (NbCl5); eager 1.6e-15 and 8.2e-15. Plain cuEq float64 against native hybrid: 2.0e-15 and
1.1e-14.

## 3. Rounding-class rewrites in float64

`species_tables`, `fold_readout_tail` and `fold_product_linear` are off in float64 by OPT-DEF-2 (rounding class).
Asked for explicitly they apply (`rewrites=on`): step 0.91 on both cells; forces max |ΔF| 1.1e-15 (NaCl),
5.7e-15 (NbCl5) against native. `segment_gemm` and `grouped_launch` were not tried: their Triton GEMMs are float32
only, and with the emulation the cuBLAS products are the faster path anyway.

## 4. Float64 radial tables

`radial_tables` extended to float64 models (`fastmace/rewrites/radial/__init__.py`,
`fastmace/kernels/triton/radial_table.py`):

- **Tables in the model's dtype.** The kernels take float64 coefficients and lengths (the Clenshaw accumulators
  follow the coefficient dtype); float32 tables are unchanged.
- **Fit.** Degree `DEGREE_64 = 16`, tolerance `TOLERANCE_64 = 3e-13` of the local scale with a floor of
  `FLOOR_64 = 0.1` of each output's largest value (so within 3e-14 of it). The **derivative series is fitted from
  the network's exact derivatives** (the float32 path differentiates the value series): differentiating amplified
  the series' rounding by about degree²/width, held the derivatives at 5e-10 and drove every piece to the depth
  limit (4,096 pieces on the tiny model). Fitted directly: 4e-14.
- **Domain.** `R_LO_64 = 0.25` Å, exact in float32. Triton passes the kernels' scalar bounds as float32; 0.3
  rounds up to 0.30000001, and an edge at exactly 0.3 Å then counted as below the domain with a zero slope
  (certificate 3e10 on CUDA, 0.29 on the CPU). Found with `cert64.py`.
- **Certificate.** The float32 certificate divides the table's error by the float32 network's; in float64 the
  network is the reference itself (error zero), so float64 tables are held to a fixed bound instead:
  `CERTIFY_BOUND_64 = 1e-12` of each output's scale, at the w level and the edge density, values and r-derivatives,
  as before. Ratio > 1 fails the self-check (rule 13 turns the record off).
- **Planner.** Explicit `radial_tables="on"` is now allowed in float64 plans outside reproducible plans (the
  refusal text changed; its code stays `radial-tables-needs-float32`). Default still `never_automatic`.

**Certificates** (`cert64all.py`; every pair, both interactions): tolerance 1e-12 failed (1.20–1.96); **3e-13**
passed with 0.17–0.52 (NaCl 23 and 420 pieces, NbCl5 253 and 366); 1e-13 passed with up to 683 pieces. In MD
plans the certificates were 0.17–0.52 (`runs/round3.txt`). On one pair, the table's error against the CPU float64
chain was 2.7e-14 (values) and 7.3e-14 (derivatives), the GPU float64 network's own 1.9e-14 and 3.0e-14
(`cert64.py`).

**Forces** against native hybrid float64: max |ΔF| 4.1e-15 (NaCl), 2.9e-14 (NbCl5); with the rewrites and eager
emulation as well, 3.9e-15 and 2.9e-14. The tables are 2–3× the stock backends' scatter (2.0e-15, 1.1e-14), the
largest contribution of the three measures, still about 1e-13 of the largest force.

**Speed:** 0.90 (NaCl) and 0.91 (NbCl5), as expected from the radial hidden layers' share (9 %, 7 %).

**Tests:** `tests/cpu/test_radial_tables.py`: the float64 refusal tests are replaced by
`test_float64_tables_follow_the_float64_network` (tiny model: float64 tables, fit within `TOLERANCE_64`, forces
within 1e-11 of the network, relative) and `test_the_planner_allows_float64_tables_outside_reproducible_plans`.
With `test_planner.py` and `test_reproducible.py`: 121 passed, 20 skipped (CPU, 4 workers). Kernel check
(`kernel64.py`): the Triton kernels against `evaluate` in float64, forward 9e-15, backward 6e-15.

## 5. Paired-float32 arithmetic (not pursued)

`df64_bench.py`: a chain of 64 multiply-adds per element, 16.8 M elements. Native float64 200 G FMA/s, paired
float32 422 G FMA/s (2.1×). The kernel's result was only accurate to 1.9e-6 (the compiler did not keep the error
terms exact), so 2.1× is an upper bound. It would apply only to the part of the step outside the GEMMs and the
tables (about 10–13 %): not worth a kernel family.

## 6. INT8-slice float32 GEMMs (arXiv 2610.35164, FlashSO(2)) — rejected on sm_120

The paper emulates float32 GEMMs with INT8 slices and 1-D block scales (one scale per 128 entries along K) on a
GH200 and runs UMA 3.3× faster. `int8emu.py`: a Triton kernel with p INT8 slices per operand and 128-wide block
scales, B sliced once (constant weights), A's slicing timed separately (PyTorch, unfused); errors against float64,
scaled by |A||B|; cuBLAS SGEMM with TF32 off:

| Shape | SGEMM | one INT8 product (cuBLASLt) | 3 slices (Emu33, 6 products) | 4 slices (10 products) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 13608×512×512 | 518 µs, 4.1e-7 | 261 µs | 599 µs, 2.4e-6 | 965 µs, 5.7e-8 |
| 1944×512×512 | 101 µs, 1.7e-7 | 91 µs | 90 µs, 2.4e-6 | 139 µs, 5.1e-8 |
| 1944×384×2048 | 346 µs, 4.2e-7 | 118 µs | 324 µs, 3.3e-6 | 355 µs, 6.8e-8 |
| 56320×1280×64 | 2486 µs, 4.3e-7 | 498 µs | 778 µs, 1.2e-6 | 1067 µs, 3.7e-8 |

(`runs/int8emu.jsonl` has all ten shapes; A's slicing adds 0.2–47 ms unfused.) One INT8 product is only about 2×
faster than SGEMM on this GPU, against about 30× on the paper's GH200. Three slices are 5× less accurate than
float32 (fails the force gate's 2× rule); four are more accurate but 1.9× slower. The one skinny shape where it
wins is served by FastMACE's own Triton kernel, not SGEMM, and its A is the 288 MB edge-sized operand, whose
slicing costs more than the gain. A general rule would be: INT8-slice float32 pays only where the INT8 : FP32 rate
exceeds the product count (≥ 10 for float32 accuracy); not on sm_120. Float64 emulation pays here because the
INT8 : FP64 ratio is about 100×.

## Owner decisions (27 October 2026)

The owner decided all four ("2 do that", after "1 yes default" for decision 62): **SPEC §18 decision 63**.

1. **Hybrid for float64 runs.** FastMACE's planner already picks the hybrid backend for float64 plans that are not
   reproducible; the ResearchModule's `main.py --backend cueq` float64 references are documented there as plain
   cuEq's bitwise-reproducible path, with `--backend hybrid --dtype float64` as the fast float64 run.
2. **`EngineConfig.fp64_emulation`** (`auto` rule `on_for_float64_cuda_with_reduced_rate_fp64_unless_reproducible`):
   cuBLAS's emulation, strategy `eager`, set by `FixedPlanner.build` before the model's first float64 product
   (`model.compile.configure_fp64_emulation`); the build checks with a profiled 512³ product that emulated kernels
   run and warns if a cuBLAS handle created earlier keeps native ones (`fp64_emulation_active`). The rule is
   measured, not a table: `kernels.triton.fp64_rate.fp64_rate_ratio` times a chain of multiply-adds in float32 and
   float64 in Triton (no cuBLAS handle), about 3 s on first use, cached per device; on below
   `FP64_EMULATION_RATE = 1/8`. The compute capability cannot decide: the RTX 5060 Ti is 12.0, like no data-centre
   part. Measured: RTX 5060 Ti 1/74.
3. **Rounding-class rewrites** (`species_tables`, `fold_readout_tail`, `fold_product_linear`) on in float64 plans
   that are not reproducible (rules renamed without `float32`).
4. **Float64 radial tables** on with `fold_cutoff` in float64 plans that are not reproducible (rule
   `on_with_fold_cutoff_unless_reproducible`); explicit `"on"` in reproducible float64 plans is refused.

## f64-T03: float64 chunks for the chunked backward (27 October 2026)

The package's NbCl5 float64 profile showed 73 input-gradient products of 768 edges (37.9 ms, 15 % of the step) at
the native float64 rate: `chunked_conv_backward` sizes its chunk to a quarter of the L2, and float64 halves it.
Emulated products of that size gain little. A microbenchmark of the same product over NbCl5's 56,320 edges (eager
emulation): 768-edge chunks 33.6 ms, 3,072 18.7, 12,288 **15.3**, 24,576 16.2, unchunked 19.5. The folded forward's
product reshaped (transposed, split into column blocks, inner dimension padded to 128) gained nothing (24.6–36.0 ms
against 25.1 as is). Rule (`oeq_streaming.l2_chunk_edges`): float64 chunks hold `UNCHUNKED_L2_MULTIPLE` (4) L2s
(13,056 edges on 32 MiB); float32 unchanged. MD step, float64 package, two alternating rounds:

| Cell | 768-edge chunks (main) | 4-L2 chunks | Ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NbCl5 | 272.4 / 273.0 ms | 244.8 / 245.1 ms | **0.90** |
| NaNbO3 | 253.6 / 252.6 ms | 214.4 / 213.8 ms | **0.85** |

Forces on NbCl5: 2.7e-14 eV/Å from stock float64 (2.7e-14 before), 2e-15 from the previous package. Nsight on the
other float64 kernels (streaming forward and backward, cuEquivariance's symmetric contraction, the SiLU/sigmoid
elementwise): float64 pipe 84–89 % busy, memory 11–59 %, so bound by float64 arithmetic; neither tuning nor
emulation reaches them, and double-float arithmetic is the remaining route (f64-T04). Records:
`results/f64-T03-20261027/`.

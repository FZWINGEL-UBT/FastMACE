# f64-T04: double-float falsifier for the FP64-bound kernels (27 October 2026)

Question: can "double-float" arithmetic, where a float64 value is carried as two float32 numbers (hi + lo) and
computed on the FP32 units, beat native float64 in the kernels of the float64 package that Nsight shows bound by
the FP64 pipe? Those kernels are the streaming convolution (forward 6.8 ms, backward 26.3 ms) and cuEquivariance's
symmetric contraction (27.5 ms) of the 254 ms float64 MH-1 step on NbCl5 3×3×3 (`f64-T01.md`). The task had two
falsifiers with stop rules. Step 1 tests the arithmetic alone and must reach ≥ 2.0× native FP64 throughput at
≤ 1e-13 relative error. Step 2 tests a double-float streaming forward on recorded operands and must reach ≥ 1.5×
at ≤ 1e-13 on NbCl5 and NaNbO3. Step 2 runs only if step 1 passes.

Branch `fastmace/f64-T04` from `main` (`2345af2c`). Scripts and raw results: `results/f64-T04-20261027/`. The
GPU was the local RTX 5060 Ti (sm_120) in WSL2 with `~/environments/mace_cueq_oeq` (torch 2.14.1+cu130,
`cuda.bindings`, NVRTC 13). Every GPU run went through the local GPU reservation.

**Verdict: step 1 fails, so step 2 was not run.** Correct double-float reaches **1.94×** native float64
throughput, below the 2.0× bar. Its error against float64 is **1.3e-13 to 4.6e-13**, also above the 1e-13 bar.
The only variant above 2× (2.8×) uses an addition with no error bound under cancellation.

## Step 1: microbenchmark (falsifier A)

`step1_df64_micro.py` is CUDA C compiled with NVRTC through `cuda.bindings`, as `compile_cubin` does, with
`--fmad=false`. The exact product uses explicit `__fmaf_rn`, and every other operation uses `__fadd_rn` or
`__fmul_rn`, so the compiler can neither contract nor reorder the error-free transforms. Earlier Triton screens
lost exactness because the compiler did exactly that.

The formulation is Joldes, Muller and Popescu, "Tight and rigorous error bounds for basic building blocks of
double-word arithmetic", ACM TOMS 44(2), 2017:

- **Product:** DWTimesDW3 (Alg. 12), FMA-based, 9 FP32 operations, relative error ≤ 4u².
- **Accurate addition (`dd_acc`):** AccurateDWPlusDW (Alg. 6), 20 operations, error ≤ 3u² + 13u³.
- **Sloppy addition (`dd_sloppy`, information only):** SloppyDWPlusDW (Alg. 5), 11 operations. It has no relative
  error bound when the operands have opposite signs, and a convolution sums terms of both signs.

Here u = 2⁻²⁴.

The workload is one fused multiply-add chain per element: x ← a·x + b, 512 iterations, 4 independent chains per
thread over 2²⁰ threads (2.1e9 FMAs). The operands x₀, a and b are loaded from float64 memory and converted to
pairs. The baseline is native `__fma_rn` in float64. Timing uses 7 alternating blocks of 5 launches in one process,
reporting the median. Accuracy is checked two ways. The first compares 64 sampled elements with an exact
evaluation in 80-digit decimal. The second compares all 4.2 M results with native float64, as
max |dd − fp64| / max |fp64|. The recurrence is contractive, and its condition number is about 1/(1 − a).

| configuration | fp64 ms | dd_acc ms (× fp64) | dd_acc error vs fp64 | vs exact (sample) | dd_sloppy ms (×) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| a ∈ [0.99, 0.999], float64 inputs | 10.86 | 5.58 (**1.94**) | 4.6e-13 | 2.2e-13 | 3.88 (2.80) |
| same, inputs exactly representable as pairs | 10.89 | 5.62 (1.94) | 2.1e-13 | 1.3e-13 | 3.87 (2.81) |
| a ∈ [0.5, 0.95], float64 inputs | 10.92 | 5.64 (1.94) | 1.4e-13 | 1.3e-14 | 3.92 (2.79) |
| same, pair-exact inputs | 10.88 | 5.59 (1.95) | 1.3e-13 | 1.5e-14 | 3.87 (2.82) |

The files are `step1_rtx5060ti.json`, `step1_rtx5060ti_pair_inputs.json`, `step1_rtx5060ti_a05.json` and
`step1_rtx5060ti_a05_pair_inputs.json`. Native float64 itself is within 6e-16 to 9e-16 of exact.

What the numbers say:

- **Throughput:**
  - Native FP64 runs 198 GFMA/s, about 1/60 of the nominal FP32 FMA peak (about 11.8 TFMA/s at boost clock).
  - Accurate double-float needs 29 FP32 operations per FMA and reaches 385 G dd-FMA/s (11.2 T FP32 operations/s), about 94 % of that FP32
    peak. Its ceiling on this GPU is therefore about 2.0–2.1×, and with any overhead it falls just short.
  - The float32 chain (0.36 ms, 30×) is memory-bound in this kernel and is shown only for scale.
- **Accuracy:**
  - A pair holds 48–49 significand bits, not 53. Each double-float multiply-add therefore rounds at about
    7u² ≈ 2.5e-14, roughly 200 times float64's unit roundoff of 1.1e-16.
  - Over a few hundred dependent operations at moderate condition, the worst element reaches 1.3e-13.
    Pair-exact inputs barely change this, so the arithmetic causes the error, not the float64 → pair conversion.
  - Converting float64 to a pair also loses up to about 2⁻⁴⁹ (1.8e-15) per value, because 53 bits do not fit into
    2 × 24.
- **Stop rule:** not met (1.94× < 2.0×, and error > 1e-13). Recorded; stopped.

## Step 2: prepared, not run (falsifier B)

The stop rule kept step 2 from running: no operands were recorded and nothing was timed. The tools exist in case
the owner wants the measurement anyway:

- **`ddconv.py`** converts the rendered float64 streaming source to a double-float forward kernel by source
  transformation:
  - `scalar_t` becomes a pair struct with overloaded `*`, `+` and `+=`;
  - loads become `load_df`, the store becomes `store_df`, and the hex float64 coefficients become pairs folded at
    compile time;
  - pointers stay `double*`.
- **Load conversion variants:** `cvt` uses F2F conversions; `bits` uses integer bit slicing, I2F and Fast2Sum, and
  flushes values below 2⁻¹²⁶.
- **Other scripts:**
  - `render_check.py` renders and compiles both variants for sm_120.
  - `record_f64.py` and `step2_dd_forward.py` are the float64 operand recorder and the alternating CUDA-graph
    benchmark with an L2 flush.
  - `step2_run.sh` runs both cells.

The only measurement taken was a compile-only static SASS count of the forward kernels, on the MH-1 problem from
the OPT7 NbCl5 record (`step2_static_sass_counts.txt`):

| kernel | FP64-pipe instructions | FP32-pipe instructions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| native float64 | 89 DMUL, 86 DFMA | — |
| df64 `cvt` | 70 DADD, 110 F2F.F64.F32, 60 F2F.F32.F64 | 2510 FADD, 526 FFMA, 376 FMUL |
| df64 `bits` | 40 DADD, 80 F2F.F64.F32 (store only) | 3152 FADD, 788 FMUL, 598 FFMA, 182 I2FP, 182 FSEL |

These are static counts, not dynamic ones, and they were not timed. They point the same way as step 1:

- The kernel needs about 20–25 FP32 instructions per native FP64 instruction, against an FP64:FP32 rate of about
  1:60 on this GPU. That margin is smaller than step 1's arithmetic alone suggests once conversions and FSEL are
  added.
- The `cvt` conversion puts the loads back on the FP64 pipe.

## Decision for the owner

Double-float does not clear the bar on sm_120:

- The accurate formulation reaches at most about 2× native FP64 in pure arithmetic, measured 1.94×.
- It carries about 1e-13 error rather than float64's 1e-16, so it is a third precision class between float32 and
  float64, not a float64 substitute.

The options:

1. **Close double-float** for the convolution and the symmetric contraction. This is the recommendation of the
   stop rule.
2. **Accept a "near-float64" class** with error around 1e-13 and the sloppy addition (2.8× arithmetic, no
   cancellation bound), then run step 2 with the prepared scripts. This would be a new precision mode needing an
   owner decision. It is not covered by the float64 package's accuracy contract (forces within 3e-14 eV/Å of
   native).

Repeating on a Colab L4 (Ada, FP64 at 1/64) was not done. Its ratio is similar to this GPU's, so it would not
change the verdict.

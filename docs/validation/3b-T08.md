# 3b-T08: PolarMACE stress against finite differences (owner decision D2, SPEC decision 41)

Status: investigation only. There is no qualification, and PolarMACE + D3 stays refused in 3b.
Plan: [phase-3b](../../plans/phase-3b.md), task 3b-T08.
Background: plans phase-1a line 901, phase-1b lines 1510 and 1618, phase-2 lines 1013–1016 and 1583,
SPEC §18 decision 26, and `fastmace/reference/records.py`'s open finding.

## Summary

1. **The 4.4e-4 eV/Å³ gap is a defect of the finite-difference reference, not of the stress.**
   - The PolarMACE energy has steps as a function of the cell. graph_longrange keeps the
     k-vectors with |k| ≤ `kspace_cutoff` (1.8467 Å⁻¹), with no taper. A k-vector at the cutoff
     still has Gaussian weight exp(−k²σ²/2) = 0.022 (σ² = 2.25 Å²) in each of the density and
     potential bases.
   - In the gate's NbCl5 3×3×3 configuration (the 1a-T19 self-test's `hydrostatic-0`), the pair
     (12, ±6, 3) sits at 1.000028 × the cutoff. In primitive-cell indices it is (4, 2, 1), a Bragg
     vector of the crystal. A strain of +1e-4 in xx, zz or xz moves it inside the cutoff, and the
     energy steps by 4.75e-3 eV, about 2.4 µeV per atom.
   - Central differences then measure that step: 4.75e-3 eV / (2 h V) = 4.36e-4 eV/Å³ at h = 1e-4,
     1.46e-4 at 3e-4 and 4.4e-5 at 1e-3. That is 1/h, the signature of a step, in exactly the three
     components whose strain moves the pair.
   - FastMACE's float64 reference reproduces 1a-T19's number to four digits: 4.3643e-4 against
     4.3643e-4, and the same xx, zz and xz entries. The yy entry and every shear entry agree to
     1e-8.
2. **The analytic stress is exact.**
   - With h ≤ 3e-5, no k-vector changes sides, and finite differences agree with the analytic
     stress to 1.0e-9 eV/Å³.
   - With the included k-set frozen, every h agrees up to the O(h²) truncation. At h = 1e-4 the
     gate gives 8.3e-9 with the pair flipping, against 4.36e-4 with the live set. At h = 1e-3 it
     gives 8.5e-7 against 4.4e-5; the ideal 3×3×3 cell gives 5.9e-7 at the same h.
   - A scan of E(e_xx) in steps of 1e-5 shows a single step of 4.75e-3 eV between e_xx = 5e-5 and
     6e-5, where the pair enters. Every other increment is smooth (6.83e-3 eV per 1e-5).
   - On the ideal (unstrained, unrattled) 3×3×3 cell, the k-vectors that cross are not Bragg
     vectors. Their structure factor is zero in the perfect crystal, so the steps vanish, and
     finite differences converge as h² down to 1.3e-9 (h = 3e-5).
   - So the gap is not the k-space stress formula, not the strain path and not precision. The
     issue 1642 correction is complete.
3. **Why NaCl 3×3×3 and NbCl5 2×2×2 passed:** no Bragg vector of those cells lies within the step
   of the cutoff. Whether a cell fails is geometric chance. NbCl5's primitive cell has 222
   reciprocal-lattice vectors inside the cutoff. Over isotropic scalings of ±3 %, 2.0 % of cells
   have one within a relative 1e-4 of it.
4. **Hybrid stress precision is float32 rounding, with no defect.**
   - Against the float64 reference, the float32 stress of every path is off by 0.8–2.7e-7 eV/Å³,
     about 0.01–0.04 MPa or 2e-5 of the stress.
   - The hybrid is 0.9× (ideal) and 2.1× (gate) the stock float32 yardstick. Plain cuEq float32
     is 1.1× and 1.5×. FastMACE's float64 path equals the reference to 1e-15.
   - So the hybrid sits at the top of the float32 scatter. That is enough to miss the gate's 2×
     ratio rule or its 1e-7 floor on some configurations (1b-T23: 1.2e-7 on NaCl), but it is far
     below anything a barostat feels.

## What NPT with PolarMACE would need

**In one line:** the hard k-space cutoff makes the PolarMACE energy discontinuous under strain.
Either of two things would avoid that: a smooth cutoff (a taper, which changes the model), or a
k-set held fixed during each barostat interval and chosen again only between intervals. With the
second, the steps fall at the re-selections, where they can be recorded.

- **Nothing in the stress itself.** The analytic stress is the derivative of the energy wherever
  the energy is differentiable, to 1e-9 eV/Å³ in float64.
- **Awareness of the cutoff steps.** BAOAB NPT integrates the analytic stress, so it samples the
  continuous potential whose derivative that stress is. That potential differs from the model's
  energy by the sum of the steps crossed since the start.
  - In a crystal a step is extensive: 4.75 meV for 1944 atoms here.
  - Volume fluctuations of 1 % cross about one Bragg pair. The melt–quench protocol's 16 % swings
    cross about 18.
  - In the liquid the structure factor has no Bragg peaks, and the steps shrink to the size of the
    ideal-cell flips (not measurable here).
  - Energies that compare different cells see the steps: an energy-conservation check of the
    extended system, or a Monte Carlo volume move if one is ever added. Forces, stress and MD
    sampling do not.
- **A self-test that can run on any structure.** The issue 1642 self-test runs only on NaCl
  3×3×3 (decision 26, `tools/force_stress_gate.py`), because NbCl5 failed it for this reason.
  Finite differences with the included k-set frozen, as `tools/polar_stress_fd.py --frozen-kset`
  does, test the stress formula on every structure.
- **A decision on the gate's stress rule for PolarMACE**, before POLAR-1-S NPT can pass it. The
  float32 stress scatter of PolarMACE (1–3e-7 eV/Å³ here) is of the order of the 1e-7 floor
  (owner decision 7). There are three ways to settle it, all owner decisions and none made here:
  a PolarMACE-specific floor (for example 5e-7 eV/Å³, still 0.08 MPa), more gate configurations
  per cell mode so that the yardstick is not a single draw, or float64 PolarMACE for NPT.

### Small fix, proposed and not applied (the conductor decides)

- `ReferenceCalculator.check_stress_fix` (`fastmace/reference/calculators.py`) could hold the
  included k-set of the unstrained cell during its finite differences, as the tool's
  `KSetProbe(frozen=True)` does.
- The self-test would then validate the stress formula on the run's own structure. The NaCl-only
  rule and the open finding in `fastmace/reference/records.py` could be retired.
- This touches reference code, not a library default, so I have not changed it.
- A smooth k-space cutoff (a taper in graph_longrange) would remove the steps from the model
  itself. That changes the model's energy, so it is upstream's choice and outside FastMACE.

### The flipping pair

The included set depends only on the cell. `tools/polar_stress_fd.py --replay` repeats
graph_longrange's bounds and cutoff test on the host (`replay.json`). It matches the GPU probe's
counts in every component and step, and gives:
- gate cell: (12, −6, 3) and (12, 6, 3), |k| / cutoff = 1.000028, inside graph_longrange's
  integer bounds (17, 16, 6). They cross the sphere at +1e-4 in xx (to 0.999976), zz (0.999995)
  and xz (0.999987), and at no −1e-4 strain;
- ideal cell: (1, ±16, 0) at yy +1e-4 and (5, ±15, −1) at yy −1e-4. Neither is a multiple of 3
  in every index, so neither is a Bragg vector of the 72-atom primitive cell.

- Six step sizes, two configurations and an 81-point scan in one float64 job took longer than
  the 870 s limit (about 5 s per evaluation). The jobs were split.

  `fastmace.reference` reproduces 1a-T19's number to four digits.

## Addendum: the frozen k-set in the self-test (4-T11, 23 October 2026)

The small fix above is applied (plan task 4-T11, phase 4).
- `ReferenceCalculator.check_stress_fix` runs its finite differences inside
  `fastmace.longrange.polar.frozen_kset(model, cell)`. That holds the k-vectors included for the
  unstrained cell (graph_longrange's bounds and |k| ≤ cutoff, as a call with stress computes them) while
  each of them still moves with the strain, as `KSetProbe(frozen=True)` does. The result records
  `kset: "frozen"` and the number of included k-vectors. Production never sets it.
- `tools/force_stress_gate.py` runs the self-test on the first float64 reference's own structure, its
  `hydrostatic-0` configuration. The NaCl 3×3×3 rule (decision 26) is gone.
- The polar record's `validation` names the run's own structure and the frozen k-set, and its open
  finding is retired. The record's digest changes with it, so the polar record id changes.
- CPU test (`tests/cpu/test_reference_policy.py`, tiny PolarMACE, mace_cueq_latest on the CPU): a cell
  scaled so that the (3, 0, 0) pair sits at 1 + 5e-6 times the cutoff. Live finite differences at
  h = 1e-4 are off by 1.1e-5 in xx only. Frozen ones are off by at most 2.7e-7, the O(h²) truncation,
  and every other component is the same either way.
- GPU test: `tests/gpu/test_reference_gpu.py::test_polar1s_stress_check_passes_on_the_gate_configuration`,
  POLAR-1-S float64 reference, h = 1e-4, the gate's `hydrostatic-0` configurations, tolerance 1e-7 eV/Å³.

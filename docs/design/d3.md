# D3 dispersion: the phase 3b contract

Plan task 3b-T01, 22 October 2026. Spec: SPEC §11.2, §7.7 "Dispersion", §10.6, §12.1;
decisions 40–43 (SPEC §18); `plans/phase-3b.md`, "Conductor's choices" 1–3 and 5. The D3 list
(3b-T02) and kernel (3b-T03) implement this contract, and 3b-T04 integrates them.
`tests/cpu/test_d3_contract.py` pins it, and `tests/cpu/test_d3_references.py` checks the
references against each other.

Without D3 nothing changes. `ForceField(dispersion=None)` is the default, and its identity
has no dispersion key, so the identity digests of D3-free runs are unchanged.

## The record: `fm.D3` (`fastmace/longrange/d3.py`)

| Field | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `functional` | required | the name of the parameter set, e.g. `"r2scan"` or `"pbe"` |
| `damping` | `"bj"` | Becke–Johnson; the only choice |
| `pair_cutoff_bohr` | `40.0` | pair cutoff |
| `cn_cutoff_bohr` | `40.0` | coordination-number cutoff |
| `three_body` | `False` | the ATM term; `ForceField` refuses `True` (`d3-three-body`) |
| `smoothing` | `"none"` | cutoff smoothing; the only choice |
| `precision` | `"float64"` | the precision of the reductions (CN, energy, virial); the only choice |
| `autoang` | `0.52917726` | Å per bohr, `torch-dftd`'s `d3_autoang` |
| `autoev` | `27.21138505` | eV per hartree, `torch-dftd`'s `d3_autoev` |
| `s6`, `s8`, `a1`, `a2` | from `functional` | the Becke–Johnson numbers |

**The numbers.**
- If all four are left out, they come from `BJ_PARAMETERS[functional]`. These are the sets of
  `torch-dftd` 0.5.1's `get_dftd3_default_params("bj", xc)`, where its `rs6` is a1, `s18` is s8
  and `rs18` is a2:

  | Set | s6 | s8 | a1 | a2 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `r2scan` (doi 10.1063/5.0041008, Table 1) | 1.0 | 0.7898 | 0.4948 | 5.7308 |
  | `pbe` | 1.0 | 0.7875 | 0.4289 | 4.4407 |

- An unknown functional without the numbers raises `ValueError`.
- If the numbers are given, all four must be given, and they are used as given. `functional`
  then only names them.
- Either way the record holds the numbers, so equal records are equal corrections:
  `D3(functional="r2scan") == D3(functional="r2scan", s6=1.0, s8=0.7898, a1=0.4948, a2=5.7308)`.
- s6 and a2 must be positive, s8 and a1 non-negative (some sets have s8 = 0).
- `fastmace init` writes the four numbers into the `[dispersion]` table.

**Cutoffs in Å** are converted with the record's own constant: `pair_cutoff_A(record)` and
`cn_cutoff_A(record)` give 40 × 0.52917726 = 21.16709040 Å. Distances in the kernel are
compared in Å against these values. In bohr, they would be divided by `autoang`.

**Identity.** `identity_entry(record)` holds every field plus `"tables"`, which is
`REFERENCE_TABLES`. `ForceField.identity` stores it as `physics["dispersion"]`. A resume with
any other number, cutoff, constant or table is a different force field (§10.6).

**Tables.** Every D3 term uses `torch-dftd` 0.5.1's `nn/params/dftd3_params.npz`:
- its arrays are `c6ab` (95, 95, 5, 5, 3), `r0ab` (95, 95), `rcov` (95) and `r2r4` (95), all
  float64;
- its SHA-256, recorded in `REFERENCE_TABLES`, is `52b7b35a…729f3d92`;
- 3b-T03 copies these arrays bit for bit and checks the hash.
  `test_installed_tables_are_the_contracts` checks the installed file.

## Precision (conductor's choice 3)

- The **pair terms** (distances, damping, C6 interpolation weights per pair, the pair force and
  the CN derivative) run in the plan's force precision: float32 or float64.
- The **reductions** run in float64:
  - the CN sum per atom;
  - the energy;
  - dE/dCN per atom;
  - the virial.
- The forces are accumulated in the force precision, as MACE's are.
- `D3.precision` names the reduction precision, so only `"float64"` is accepted. The record does
  not hold the force precision: the plan sets it, and the identity records it as
  `force_precision`.
- The gate compares float32 plans against the float64 reference tier.

## `ForceField(dispersion=...)` (`fastmace/model/forcefield.py`)

`ForceField(model, atoms=None, *, precision="float32", rewrites="auto", species=None,
dispersion=None)`

- `dispersion` takes:
  - `None`: no D3;
  - `"d3bj"`: expanded with `d3.expand` from the model's training functional. This raises
    `ValueError` when the functional is unknown or already contains dispersion (§7.7);
  - an `fm.D3`: kept as given. A functional other than the training one adds a warning to
    `ForceField.dispersion_warnings`.
  - Any other type raises `TypeError`, and any other string raises `ValueError`.
- `ForceField.dispersion` is the complete record or `None`.
- **Refusals** (`PlanRefused` at construction):
  - `d3-three-body`: `three_body=True`. No D3 term implements ATM.
  - `d3-polar`: PolarMACE with D3 (decision 41). PolarMACE NPT is not qualified.
- Plans carry the record, and their force field has the D3 term (see "Integration" below). The
  `d3-phase-3b` refusals were removed in 3b-T04.

## Integration (3b-T04)

**The term.** `D3Term` (`fastmace/longrange/d3_term.py`) is the one `DispersionTerm`. It owns a
`D3PairList` and a `D3Kernel` in the force precision. A copy in another precision gets its own
kernel on the same list (`kernel_for`), and every build prepares every kernel.
`D3Kernel.prepare` refills its CSR buffers in place when their shapes are unchanged. A list
rebuild then moves no address, and captured graphs survive it. It returns True when an address
moved.

**The force field.** `DispersedForceField` (`fastmace/model/dispersed.py`) wraps the plan's
`MACEForceField` and forwards every attribute it does not override.
- **Without D3,** the planner builds the plain `MACEForceField`, so D3 off is the old code path.
- **Force call:** MACE's call, then D3's three passes on the same positions; the outputs are summed.
  - The energy is float64.
  - The forces are in the force precision.
  - With a variable cell, the stress is MACE's plus `-virial / V`.
  - Both terms run in the force stage, so one captured graph holds them. The eager and compiled
    tiers call the same function; only MACE's model is compiled.
- **Energy only:** MACE's forward plus `evaluate_energy` (MC energies).
- **Rebuilds.**
  - `needs_rebuild` is the OR of both lists' device flags.
  - `rebuild` rebuilds the MACE list as before. It rebuilds the D3 list only when that list's own
    test at the rebuild positions asks for it, at the smallest fraction the engine tests with.
  - No flag is kept between steps, so a test at an MC trial's positions leaves nothing behind.
  - `rebuild_all` rebuilds both lists at every canonical restart point (`GraphEngine.canonicalise`).
  - The fused integrator stage ORs `d3_needs_rebuild` into the flags its kernels wrote. D3 rebuilds
    run on the main stream, never beside the force call.
- **Registry:** `force_field.d3.list.cell` and `.shift_vectors` are derived from the cell. Every
  other `force_field.d3.*` buffer is reset by a rebuild.
- **Species (conductor's choice 5):** the kernel indexes its tables by atomic number through
  `numbers`. `register_derived` points every kernel at `state.species`, so a species event refreshes
  nothing in D3. No per-atom D3 table exists that could go stale.
- **MC displacement trials:** `save_trial` and `restore_trial` copy both lists and the kernels'
  buffers (`fastmace.mc.displace`).
- **The float64 MC copy:** `DispersedForceField.following` adds a float64 kernel on the leader's D3
  list (`fastmace.mc.evaluator.float64_copy`).

**Plans.**
- `Plan.dispersion` is the record. It has a `dispersion` decision and is in the restart record
  (written only when set).
- `MinimalPlanner.plan` takes `d3_cutoff_A` from the record, so rules 11 and 12 size the D3 list.
- `FixedPlanner.build`, `build_evaluation` and the gate's reduced-cell force field wrap the MACE
  force field when the plan has D3.

**Reference and gate (§12.1).**
- `records.record_for(..., d3=record)` adds the optional-physics entry `d3`, with
  `identity_entry(record)` as its parameters, so the record is non-stock. PolarMACE with D3 is
  refused.
- `ReferenceCalculator` adds `torch_dftd3_calculator(record)` to MACE's energy, forces and stress,
  as ASE's `SumCalculator` sums them. Its `info()` labels the result non-stock.
- `torch-dftd` runs in float64 for both the reference and the float32 yardstick, so the yardstick
  measures MACE's float32 alone.
- The gate's job and the reference tier (`ReferenceForceField`) pass the plan's record.

**Front ends.** `fm.md`, `fastmace.ase` (`dispersion=`), `fm.calc` / `FastMACECalculator(dispersion=)`
and the CLI pass the record into `fm.ForceField`. With D3, `fm.calc` lists no per-atom `energies`.
The report's physics line shows the record and "non-stock"; a functional other than the training
one is a report warning. `metadata.json` has the record in `force_field_identity.physics` and the
plan, and the D3 counters in `force_field.d3`. `simulation.json` stores the record, so a resume
rebuilds the same force field, and the identity refuses a different one.

## The interface: `DispersionTerm` (`fastmace/longrange/d3.py`)

```python
class DispersionTerm(abc.ABC):
    def __init__(self, record: D3, numbers): ...          # numbers: int64 device tensor, fixed address
    def build(self, positions, cell) -> bool: ...          # counted and timed; calls _build
    def needs_rebuild(self, positions, cell) -> Tensor: ... # device bool, capturable
    def evaluate(self, positions, cell, *, virial=True) -> DispersionResult: ...
    def evaluate_energy(self, positions, cell) -> Tensor: ...  # 0-d float64; no force or virial writes
    def species_changed(self) -> None: ...                 # default: nothing to refresh
    def counters(self) -> dict: ...                        # builds, rebuilds, regrowths, build_seconds
```

- **Inputs.** Positions and the cell are float64 device tensors in Å. The cell's rows are the
  lattice vectors. `numbers` stays at one address, and species events change it in place.
- **`build`** (subclasses implement `_build`):
  - it runs on the host side and may synchronise and grow buffers;
  - it returns True when the capacity grew or buffer addresses otherwise changed, so the engine
    must capture its graphs again;
  - it adds one to `builds`, its host wall time to `build_seconds`, and, when it returned True,
    one to `regrowths`;
  - `rebuilds` is the number of builds after the first.
- **`needs_rebuild`** returns a device bool and is capturable: no host synchronisation and no
  data-dependent shapes. The engine ORs it into the fused stage's rebuild flag, as it does the
  MACE list's. 3b-T02 implements its own displacement and deformation-aware strain test
  against its own reference positions and cell, as `SkinNeighbourList.needs_rebuild` does with
  the D3 cutoff. The test includes the strain term of rule 11 (conductor's choice 4).
- **`evaluate`** returns `DispersionResult(energy, forces, virial)`:
  - `energy`: 0-d float64, eV;
  - `forces`: (N, 3) in the force precision, eV/Å;
  - `virial`: (3, 3) float64, eV, or `None` when `virial=False`.
  - The GPU implementation is capturable: `where` masks over the stored pairs and fixed shapes.
- **`evaluate_energy`** returns the energy alone, a 0-d float64 tensor in eV, with no force or
  virial writes. MC energy evaluations use it. 3b-T03 implements it, and it is capturable too.
- **Virial sign:** `virial = -dE/dε`, MACE's convention (its `virials`). The stress in ASE's sign
  (positive under tension) is `-virial / V`, which `DispersionResult.stress(volume)` returns.
  BAOAB's pressure path already uses this convention.
- **`species_changed`** refreshes per-species derived state (C6 table rows, radii) after
  `numbers` changed (conductor's choice 5). These are derived buffers in the registry.

The list (3b-T02) and the kernel (3b-T03) may be separate classes. The term that 3b-T04 puts
in the force graph is the one `DispersionTerm`: it owns the list (`_build`, `needs_rebuild`)
and runs the three passes (`evaluate`).

## The pair list: `D3PairList` (`fastmace/neighbors/d3_list.py`, 3b-T02)

`D3PairList(cell, cutoff, skin, device, *, variable_cell=False)`. `cutoff` is the larger of the pair
and CN cutoffs in Å; `skin` is rule 11's D3 skin (`NeighbourConfig.d3_skin`).

**Content.** Every undirected pair within `cutoff + skin` at the build is stored once:

- pairs `i < j`, with every periodic image;
- each self-image pair `(i, i, n)` once: `n` lexicographically positive, `-n` dropped, and no
  zero image.

Every image within range comes from the cell widths, with no minimum-image assumption. The
kernel masks `r^2 <= cutoff^2` in registers and adds each pair's contribution to both atoms. A
self-image pair therefore adds to `CN_i` twice, which matches the full double sum, and its forces
cancel. The list is never compacted between builds.

**Layout** (12 bytes per stored pair):

| Buffer | dtype, shape | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `index` | int32 `[2, capacity]` | `index[0] = i <= index[1] = j` |
| `image` | int32 `[capacity]` | a row of `shifts` |
| `shifts` | float64 `[shift_capacity, 3]` | integer lattice shifts, unique, lexicographic; unused rows zero |
| `shift_vectors` | float64 `[shift_capacity, 3]` | `shifts @ cell` in the current cell (`set_cell` refreshes it) |
| `count` | int64 scalar | stored pairs |

- **Pair vector:** `positions[j] - positions[i] + shifts[image] @ cell`. The positions are the
  unwrapped state positions, and the cell is the current one. A pair's shift absorbs the atoms'
  whole-cell offsets at the build, as `SkinNeighbourList`'s unit shifts do, so there are no
  per-atom offsets.
- **Order:** the pairs are sorted by `(i, j, shift)`, and `shifts` is lexicographic, so the order
  is also `(i, j, image)`. The pairs of atom `i` form one contiguous run, which gives the CSR row
  offsets.
- **Determinism:** the order is canonical. The same positions, cell and capacities give the same
  buffers bit for bit, on the CPU and on CUDA alike.
- **Padding:** slots from `count` to `capacity` hold `i = j = N` (one past the last atom) with
  shift row 0.

**Build and rebuild.**

- **`build(positions, cell=None) -> bool`** runs on the host side. It is True when a buffer moved,
  that is, when a capacity grew or the atom count changed. It uses the pair screen of
  `SkinNeighbourList.build_gpu` restricted to `i <= j` (`pair_search.candidate_pairs(half=True)`),
  then a float64 decision and a sort on a unique key. The torch code is device-agnostic, so the
  CPU tier builds the same list. `canonicalise(positions, cell)` is the canonical restart point.
- **`needs_rebuild(positions, cell=None, fraction=1.0)`** is a 0-d device bool with no host
  synchronisation, so it is capturable. With `cell` (variable cell) it copies the cell in, as
  `set_cell` does. The test is `SkinNeighbourList`'s displacement test, or its deformation-aware
  test, with the D3 cutoff, against the list's own reference positions and build cell.
- **`set_capacity(capacity, shift_capacity=None)`** restores a resumed run's capacities.
- **`save_list` and `restore_list`** support MC displacement trials with a fixed cell, as on the
  MACE list.
- **`counters()`** gives `d3_builds`, `d3_build_time_s`, `d3_last_build_time_s`, `d3_pairs`,
  `d3_capacity`, `d3_shifts`, `d3_shift_capacity`, `d3_images` and `d3_skin_A`. `buffers()`
  returns the buffers for the registry (3b-T04).

**Planner** (`MinimalPlanner.plan(..., d3_cutoff_A=...)`).

- **Rule 11** gives the skin `max(1.0 Å, 2 n d + cutoff f / (1 - f))`:
  - `d` is the fastest atom's displacement per step at `max_temperature_K`;
  - `f` is the barostat's thermal Frobenius strain over `n = 50` steps: 3σ of `sqrt(k_B T / W)` with
    ASE's barostat mass, times √3 for a hydrostatic cell or 3 for a full cell;
  - the floor and `n` are provisional until 3b-T06.
- **Rule 12** records the estimated pairs and memory (code `rule-12-d3`).
  - Above 25 % of the GPU memory it warns (`rule-12-d3-warn`).
  - It refuses (`rule-12-d3-memory`) when the list's buffers alone exceed the free memory.

## References (`fastmace/reference/d3.py`)

Both references return `(energy eV, forces (N, 3) eV/Å, stress (3, 3) eV/Å³ in ASE's sign)` as
float64 numpy arrays, and both use exactly the record's numbers.

- **`torch_dftd3(atoms, record, dtype="float64", device="cpu")`** uses
  `torch_dftd3_calculator(record, ...)`, which builds a `TorchDFTD3Calculator`:
  - `damping="bj"` is passed explicitly, because its default is zero damping;
  - s6, s8, a1 and a2 are set on `dftd_module.params`, so any record works, not only its named
    sets;
  - ATM is off and there is no smoothing;
  - cutoffs are `pair_cutoff_A(record)` and `cn_cutoff_A(record)`.

  The same calculator is what 3b-T04's reference tier adds to MACE as a `SumCalculator`
  (§12.1).
- **`simple_dftd3(atoms, record)`** uses Grimme's simple-dftd3 through PyPI's `dftd3` 1.6.0 (a
  manylinux wheel; no build was needed):
  - `RationalDampingParam(s6, s8, s9=0, a1, a2)`;
  - `set_realspace_cutoff(pair, cn, cn)` in bohr, with smoothing widths of 0;
  - positions and cell divided by the record's `autoang`, and energy, forces and virial
    converted with its `autoev`, so the two references compare implementations, not constants;
  - 3D periodic cells only.
- **tad-dftd3 0.7.0** is installed (local `mace_cueq_latest`), but it is not a reference here.
  Its `dftd3()` takes no cell or periodicity, so it cannot evaluate the periodic cells FastMACE
  runs.

**Cutoff conversion in `torch-dftd`.**
- `torch-dftd` builds its pair list from the cutoff in Å. It then masks the CN with
  `cnthr / ase.units.Bohr` against distances divided by its own `d3_autoang`.
- `mace_mp`'s `cutoff = 40 * ase.units.Bohr` therefore takes pairs to 39.9999963 d3-bohr and the
  CN to exactly 40.
- The wrapper passes `40 * autoang` Å, which puts both at exactly 40 d3-bohr. At NbCl5 density
  the shell between the two holds next to no pairs, and its energy is far below the gate.

**Agreement** (`tests/cpu/test_d3_references.py`, 22 October 2026). The fixtures are NaCl (64
atoms), NbCl5 (36) and NaNbCl6 (32), each rattled by 0.05 Å and strained by up to 3 %, with
both the `r2scan` and `pbe` sets:

| Quantity | Largest difference | Tolerance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| energy | 2.2e-8 relative (up to 1.5e-7 eV) | 2e-7 relative |
| forces | 2.6e-9 eV/Å (`pbe`; `r2scan` 4.2e-10) | 2e-8 eV/Å |
| stress | 1.5e-10 eV/Å³ | 2e-9 eV/Å³ |

The energy offset is nearly the same relative value in every case, which points to a table or
constant detail and not to pair selection. The stress matches central differences of the energy.
3b-T03's float64 kernel is held to these tolerances against both references.

The references are optional:
- `pip install 'fastmace[d3-reference]'` installs `torch-dftd==0.5.1` and `dftd3==1.6.0`;
- without them the cross-check tests are skipped;
- `fastmace_cpu` has them.
